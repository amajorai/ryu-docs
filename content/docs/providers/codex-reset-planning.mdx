<Callout type="warn">
  Ryu does not include a reset calendar or planner, and it cannot redeem a reset. This page explains
  how to reason about Codex resets by hand. Codex OAuth
  [account routing](/docs/providers/account-routing#managed-pi-accounts) reads recent usage windows
  only to choose an account for new conversations. Those readings are not a reset history or a plan.
</Callout>

## Compare usable allowance with reset counts

A reset plan should distinguish how many times an allowance refreshes from how much of that
allowance you can actually use. Start with a calendar that shows the weekly reset date reported by
your provider and your next billing date.

For Codex, a full banked reset refreshes eligible usage windows and changes the weekly reset
schedule. The original upcoming weekly reset does not also occur. Your resumed usage determines the
new weekly period. Confirm actual times and banked-reset expiration in your OpenAI account's
**Settings → Usage**. See
[OpenAI's banked-reset explanation](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001498-how-banked-codex-resets-work).

## Read a reset timeline

- Mark each new weekly date, and cross out the original date it replaces.
- Count actual reset events only. Do not count crossed-out original dates.
- Keep the billing date visible. A calendar month is longer than four weeks, and billing dates are
  comparison boundaries, not usage resets.
- Public reset announcements are not your account's history. A banked reset being granted is not the
  same as it being used, and scheduled forecasts are not completed resets. Announcement dates can
  differ from the time your account received a reset.
- Your past weekly resets are not public. Do not invent weekly events before the first date you know.

If an allowance is already exhausted, a banked reset can restore work sooner. If the weekly reset is
imminent, a banked reset replaces that refresh and buys less time. The best date depends on your
workload, your starting balance, and the period you compare.

## Forced resets and unused allowance

A forced reset replaces unused allowance and starts a fresh week. The unused part does not carry
forward. This can shorten the current usage window and move the following weekly reset past a
billing or cancellation date.

Unused balance deleted at a reset is not automatically lost usage. At a slow usage pace, frequent
resets may replace mostly unused capacity without increasing the work you complete. At a faster pace,
they may restore capacity you can consume. Neither case is a refund, a cash loss, or a purchased
credit, and a timeline does not show a company's intent.

## Token totals and API-equivalent cost

Token totals do not establish subscription utilization, because tokens have different allowance costs
across models and workloads. An API-equivalent cost estimate (for example, from
[models.dev pricing](https://models.dev/)) uses current standard rates. It is not a historical
invoice, a subscription charge, or proof of money lost to resets.

Use [usage history](/docs/surfaces/desktop/user-guide/usage-history) to compare the activity Ryu
recorded with your planning assumptions. Historical token counts and API-equivalent cost estimates do
not establish a subscription account's remaining allowance or reset time.

## Limits of a forecast

Whole-day, constant-pace forecasts do not account for five-hour limits, variable model costs,
changing workload, or reset expiration. They are manual planning estimates. Verify the account's
current reset time before you act.
