This guide covers sound in Video Studio: clip cleanup and effects, pan and
channels, speed and pitch, the mixer, ducking, waveform video, local music and
sound effects, and voiceover recording. Start with the
[Video Studio overview](/docs/apps/video-studio); linked audio, J-cuts, and
L-cuts are in [timeline editing](/docs/apps/video-studio-editing#detach-and-recover-audio).

## Clean up a clip

Select an audio clip, or a video clip with sound, to open its audio settings.
**Audio cleanup** offers **Original audio**, **Podcast voice**, **Noisy room**,
and **Music** presets, or set the parts yourself:

- **Denoise (%)** and **Voice enhance (%)** in graded steps.
- **Voice isolation (%)** uses the RNNoise speech model bundled with Video
  Studio (the model ships inside the sidecar, so no extra download is needed).
  It needs FFmpeg's `arnndn` filter; without the filter or the model, the
  inspector explains that voice isolation is unavailable.
- **Normalize loudness** brings the clip to -16 LUFS.

Some graded cleanup is applied only in the export, and the inspector notes when.
The original media is never changed.

## Audio effects

**Voice and audio effects** holds a stack of up to eight effects. Add pitch,
echo, reverb, EQ, bass boost, treble boost, telephone, radio, robot, chorus,
flanger, megaphone, chipmunk, deep voice, vibrato, or tremolo, set each one's
amount (and semitones, frequency, or gain where it applies), bypass it, move it
up or down, or remove it. Eight presets fill the stack in one step: Warm voice,
Phone call, Old radio, Stadium announcer, Cathedral, Robot voice, Chipmunk, and
Deep voice.

Pitch effects use FFmpeg's `rubberband` filter when the node has it; otherwise
they use a tape-style shift that also changes timing slightly. Effects the
preview cannot play are marked **Render only** and are heard in the export.

## Pan and channels

**Pan** runs from L -100 to R +100 with constant-power panning and can be
keyframed. **Channels** maps the source to stereo, mono, left only, right only,
or swapped channels. Copying a clip's audio settings carries its cleanup,
effects, pan, and channel mode.

## Speed, pitch, and volume

Clip speed runs from 0.1× to 100×. Faster settings are limited so the clip stays
at least 0.04 seconds long and longer than its fades, exit transition, and
keyframes; faster presets are disabled and the inspector shows the highest
speed the clip allows. Linked clips change speed together, and a locked partner
track blocks the change. The preview plays between 0.0625× and 16× of the
source and resyncs as needed; the export is exact.

Turn **Preserve pitch** off for tape-style speed changes. Audio faster than 16×
is muted unless **Keep audio above 16x** is on; that switch clears itself when
the speed drops to 16× or less. A muted clip is silent in the preview and the
export at any speed.

Clip volume is shown in dB, up to about +12 dB. On the timeline, a selected
clip's amber volume handle sets its volume by dragging or with the arrow keys
(1 dB steps, 6 dB with Shift); on a clip with volume keyframes it moves every
keyframe by the same amount. Cmd/Ctrl-click the volume line to add a keyframe at
its current level, then drag the point to change its level and time.

To smooth slow motion visually, see
[media derivatives](/docs/apps/video-studio-media-workflows#stabilize-interpolate-and-enhance).

## Mix tracks

Open **Mixer** in the Exports area, or use the controls in each track header.
Each track has a gain from -60 to +12 dB and a pan from L100 to R100, applied in
the export; the preview applies them too. Mute and solo stay in the track
header. Hiding a clip or a track silences it in both the preview and the
export.

The **Mixer** panel also holds the master meter, which shows peak and RMS for
each channel. Its clip light latches when the level passes -0.1 dBFS; click it
to reset. Turn on **Play audio while scrubbing** to hear short snippets while
you drag the playhead; the choice is saved in this browser.

## Duck music under speech

For a music or effects layer, set **Duck under other audio** to **Duck under
voice** to lower it while other audible layers speak. Three fields appear
under the setting:

| Field | Range | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Duck amount (dB)** | 3 to 24 | 9 | How far the layer dips while the other audio plays. 6 to 12 dB suits most music under speech. |
| **Duck attack (ms)** | 5 to 500 | 20 | How quickly the layer goes down once the other audio starts. |
| **Duck release (ms)** | 50 to 2000 | 250 | How quickly the layer comes back once the other audio stops. 250 to 700 ms sounds natural. |

The dip is the set amount however loud the voice is. Quiet room tone on the
other layers does not trigger it. Ducking also plays in the preview, with the
same amount, attack and release as the export, so you can tune it by ear
while editing. A ducked layer that runs past the last voice clip comes back
to full level after the voice ends. Leave **Keep full mix** selected when the
layer should stay at its own level. **Paste settings** with the audio group
copies the ducking settings to other clips.

## Turn audio into waveform video

Select an audio clip and set **Audio visualization** to **Waveform**. The video
draws the clip's samples with your choice of color and height. Layer it over
footage, frame and animate it like any visual clip, and combine it with titles
and captions for podcast and voice clips.

## Local soundtracks

**Local soundtrack** in the Media area composes an original score on your node,
without a provider. Choose a duration, a tempo, and one of nine styles: warm,
bright, tense, ambient, cinematic, lo-fi, upbeat, corporate, or suspense. The
score layers pluck, pad, bass, arpeggio, kick, hat, and snare parts, and
intensity sections make some passages quieter (quieter sections drop the drums
and arpeggio).

**Plan from timeline** asks Ryu's language model to plan sections for your
edit. Section changes snap to picture cuts first, then to the score's bars.
When the timeline has **Beat** markers, the soundtrack keeps their measured
tempo and starts its bars on the first beat marker. If the model returns a plan
that cannot be used, the panel asks you to try again. Long scores with layers
or sections are composed in the background.

To ask for particular music, type it in **Describe the music** (for example,
"slow lo-fi piano that builds into warm drums") before you plan. The model maps
your words to the style, tempo, instrument layers, and how the energy rises and
falls; Beat markers still set the tempo when they exist.

When you have marked an In/Out range on the timeline, **Score** can be set to
**The marked range**. The plan and the soundtrack then cover only that range,
and **Create soundtrack** places the result at the range start on a free audio
track. **Score this range** in the timeline's range bar does the same in one
step.

## Sound effects

Add a procedural sound effect from the same area: whoosh, riser, hit, impact,
click, pop, swoosh, or ding, with an optional duration. A seed makes the effect
repeatable. Video Studio runs at most two local audio jobs (soundtracks or
sound effects) at a time; a third request is refused until one finishes.

## Record a voiceover

Open **Record** in the Media area. A voiceover starts with a 3-2-1 count-in
over the preview. Turn on **Play the timeline while recording** to hear the edit
as you speak (use headphones). **Stop and add** places the take at the
playhead; takes you leave waiting can be added or discarded later. Recordings
are saved in a **Recordings** folder in the library.

A voiceover never goes on the main picture track or on a locked or muted
track, and it prefers tracks you can hear. When no suitable track is free, the
take stays in the library and the status line says so.

Recording uses Ryu's microphone recorder and needs the optional media-recording
permission (`media:record`) for Video Studio, separate from enabling the app.
Approve it when Ryu asks, or later in the app's permissions; only the
first-party Video Studio app can request it. Screen and camera
recording (WebM, up to 30 minutes) work only where the host allows capture.
When a permission is not granted, the Record tab says so instead of recording.
