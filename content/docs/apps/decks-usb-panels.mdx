This page lists the decks in this family that Decks supports and how each one maps to Ryu keys, dials, and lights. To give keys labels, colours, and actions, start with [Decks](/docs/apps/decks). Before Ryu opens a deck, [close other deck software](/docs/apps/decks#close-other-deck-software), and on Linux add the [udev rules](/docs/apps/decks-linux).

## Device notes

### Stream decks, macropads and other USB panels

Some decks have buttons without a screen. Ryu numbers them after the screen
keys: the Stream Deck Neo's two touch buttons (Touch 1 and 2), a Loupedeck's
round buttons (Button 1 and up; on the CT, the labelled buttons follow), and
the Ajazz AKP03's and Mirabox M18's three lower buttons, and the Logitech MX
Creative Keypad's two page buttons (Page left and Page right). On a Loupedeck, the
round buttons glow
in the key's colour; on the others only the action matters. The Mirabox N1 is
the exception: its two buttons and its knob each have a small screen, so they
show their label on their colour too (the knob as a key named Knob).

The small info screen on a Stream Deck Neo is one more key, Info screen. It shows
its label on its colour. It has no touch, so its action runs only from Test (or
from a workflow that reacts to another key).

On a Loupedeck Live, Live S or CT, the keys are areas of one touchscreen: a
touch presses the key under your finger until you lift it. On a Loupedeck Live
(or Razer Stream Controller) and a Loupedeck CT, each side strip is split into
three strip keys, one beside each knob (Left strip 1 to 3 and Right strip 1 to 3
in Decks, top to bottom). They show their label on their colour and press like
the other touch keys. The round screen in the middle of the CT's wheel is one
more key, Wheel: it shows its label on its colour, and touching it presses it.
Pushing or turning the wheel itself still works as a dial.

The touch strip above the dials on a Stream Deck +, a Stream Deck + XL or an
Ajazz AKP05 / Mirabox N4 is split into strip keys, one above each dial (Strip 1
to 4 in Decks, or 1 to 6 on the + XL). Each shows its label on its colour, and a
tap presses it. On a Stream Deck + or + XL, a swipe along the strip raises
`@ryu/decks#strip.swiped` with the strip keys under the start and the end of the
swipe and its direction (`left` or `right`).

On the Work Louder boards, Ryu drives the keys mapped to the agent keycodes
(`KV_OAI_AG00` to `KV_OAI_AG05`). The Codex Micro ships with them. On a Creator
Micro 2, assign those keycodes to the keys you want in Work Louder Input first.
Its other keys keep sending whatever Input maps them to. Boards that only send
keystrokes, such as the original Creator Micro, can still trigger Ryu through
Ryu's keyboard shortcuts.

The Xencelabs Quick Keys has no key screens. Its display shows the first eight
characters of each of Keys 1 to 8's labels as text, with no colour, and its
ninth key, Button, shows nothing. Its wheel is a dial: pushing the wheel is the
dial press. With the wireless dongle, Ryu drives the first Quick Keys the
dongle reports and ignores any other paired to it.

The Blackmagic Design panels have no screens. Decks shows each key under the
name printed on it, at its place on the panel. On the Speed Editor and Replay
Editor only some keys have a light, which is on for any colour except black or near-black; on the
ATEM Micro Panel every key lights in the nearest of eight colours. The jog wheel
is Dial 1, and the T-bar works as a dial too (Dial 2 on the Replay Editor, Dial 1
on the ATEM Micro Panel): moving it turns the dial by its travel. These panels
have no brightness setting, and Ryu drives them over USB only, not Bluetooth.

The 203 Systems Mystrix's 64 pads light in their key's colour. Open the
Companion app in Matrix OS first: Ryu sees the pads and lights them only while
it is open, and they go dark while another app or the FN action menu is open,
then come back. The FN key and the touch bars stay with Matrix OS. Brightness
dims the pads' colours. The Mystrix 2 is not supported yet.

The Framework Laptop 16 RGB Macropad's 24 keys light in their key's colour.
Ryu sees the macropad only after you flash it with the custom QMK firmware made
for Bitfocus Companion (the macropad keymap of Julusian's framework_qmk_firmware).
With Framework's stock firmware it keeps typing keys as usual and Ryu does not
detect it. Brightness dims the keys' colours.

The Adafruit MacroPad RP2040's 12 keys light in their key's colour. Ryu drives
it only on the Companion CircuitPython HID firmware from Maxim Kulkin's
nodejs-macropad: copy its `boot.py`, `code.py`, `settings.py` and
`lib/neopixel.mpy` onto the board's CIRCUITPY drive, then press the reset
button. With other CircuitPython code, Ryu shows the MacroPad with a note on
what to copy and leaves it alone. The encoder, its button, the screen and the
speaker are not used, and at most six held keys are seen at once. Brightness
dims the keys' colours; full brightness is about half the LEDs' drive, to stay
within what a USB port supplies.

The Haute42 B16 leverless controller works only after you flash it with
SoundMinded's SurfaceCore firmware; with its stock firmware it stays a game
controller and Ryu does not detect it. Its sixteen round buttons, B01 to B16,
light in their key's colour, and the five function buttons (Fn1 to Fn5) are keys
without a light. Decks places the keys after the SurfaceCore Companion layout,
which is close to, but not exactly, where they sit on the controller.
Brightness dims the colours. The firmware does not debounce its buttons and can
miss a press or release when several buttons change at once, so a key can stay
held in Ryu until you press it again.

On a Native Instruments Maschine MK2, Mikro MK2 or Jam, the pads and buttons are
keys, named as printed (the MK2 and Mikro pads by NI's numbers, Pad 1 at the
bottom left). On the MK2 and Mikro MK2 a pad presses once you hit it firmly
enough; the Jam's matrix pads are plain buttons that press as soon as you touch
them. The pads and group
buttons light in their key's colour (on the Jam, the nearest of its own colours,
which also goes for the matrix and scene buttons); the other buttons have a
light of one fixed colour that glows brighter for a brighter key colour, and the
MK2's Main button has none. On the MK2 the eight knobs are dials 1 to 8 and the
master encoder dial 9, and its two displays together are the info screen.

On the Mikro MK2 the encoder is dial 1, pushing it presses the dial, and its display is
the info screen. On the Jam the eight touch strips are dials 1 to 8, turned by
sliding a finger along them, and the encoder is dial 9. The info screen shows
its key's label in one colour and has no touch, so run its action with Test.
Brightness dims the lights. Quit Native Instruments' Maschine software and its
background services (NIHardwareAgent and NIHostIntegrationAgent) first, since
Ryu cannot open the controller while they hold it. The original Maschine (MK1)
is not supported.

On a Native Instruments Traktor Kontrol F1, the 16 pads and 12 buttons are keys:
the buttons named as printed, the pads Pad 1 to Pad 16 row by row. The pads light in their key's colour; the other buttons have
a light of one fixed colour that glows brighter for a brighter key colour. The
browse encoder is dial 1, and pushing it presses the dial; the four knobs are
dials 2 to 5 and the four faders dials 6 to 9, each turned a step at a time as
you move it. The two-digit display stays dark. Brightness dims the lights. Ryu
takes Pad 1 as the top-left pad, which has not been checked on an F1. Quit
Traktor and Native Instruments' background services (NIHardwareAgent and
NIHostIntegrationAgent) first, since Ryu cannot open the controller while they
hold it.

On a Native Instruments Komplete Kontrol S25, S49, S61 or S88 (the first
generation, MK1), the 23 buttons around the displays are keys, named as printed.
Each has a light of one fixed colour that glows brighter for a brighter key
colour, and the deck light colours the light guide over the piano keys. The
browse encoder is dial 1, and pushing it presses the dial; the eight encoders
above the displays are dials 2 to 9 (touching one does nothing). The displays
stay dark. The piano keys, wheels and touch strip stay MIDI, for your music
software, and are not keys in Ryu. Brightness dims the lights. Quit Komplete
Kontrol and Native Instruments' background services (NIHardwareAgent and
NIHostIntegrationAgent) first, since Ryu cannot open the keyboard while they
hold it. The MK2 and later keyboards are not supported.

The Stream Deck panel on a Corsair Galleon 100 SD keyboard has 12 keys, two
dials and a wide screen between the dials and the keys. Decks splits that
screen into two keys, Screen 1 and Screen 2, one under each dial. They show
their label on their colour; it is not confirmed that the screen takes touch,
so run their actions with Test. The ring of lights round each dial takes a
colour under **Dial lights**. Ryu uses only the panel: the keyboard keeps
typing as usual.

Each Cooler Master MasterHub module is a deck of its own in Decks. The Keypad's
15 keys show their label on their colour. The Knob's three knobs, the Slider's
five faders, the Roller's two rollers (numbered left to right) and the 1xDial
work as dials: moving a fader turns its dial by how far it moved. The 1xDial's
round screen is one more key that shows its label on its colour; pushing the
dial is a dial press, so run that key's action with Test. The knob and fader
lights take a colour under **Dial lights** and the base's light under **Deck
lights**. Quit Cooler Master's MasterHub software first. The rollers are not
known to work on a Mac.

### Logitech gaming keyboards

On a Logitech G13, the G-keys, M1 to MR, the round button and L1 to L4 under
the screen, the two thumb buttons and the stick click are keys, named as
printed. The stick is four more keys, Stick up, down, left and right. The LCD
is the info screen: it shows that key's label in one colour, lighting the bright
parts of its face, and has no touch, so run its action with Test. Any colour on
M1, M2, M3 or MR turns its light on. The colour under **Deck lights** sets the
backlight, which also lights the LCD; with none set it stays dark. Brightness
dims the backlight. The backlight button stays with the G13. Quit Logitech G HUB
or Logitech Gaming Software first if Ryu cannot open it.

On a Logitech G15, G15 v2, G11, G510 or G110 keyboard, Ryu takes only the
G-keys, M1 to MR, the buttons under the LCD and the backlight button (Light);
the G510 adds its game-mode switch, which reads as held while it is on, and its
headphone and mic mute keys. The rest of the keyboard still types. On the G15,
G15 v2, G11 and G510, Ryu also stops the G-keys typing F-keys or numbers. On the G15, G15 v2 and
G510 the LCD is the info screen: it shows that key's label in one colour, with
the bright parts of its face as dark pixels on the lit glass, and has no touch,
so run its action with Test. Any colour on M1, M2, M3 or MR turns its light on.

Brightness steps the G15, G15 v2 and G11 backlights between off, low and high and sets
the G110's backlight level; pressing Light on the keyboard takes the backlight
back until you change the brightness again. On the G510 the colour under **Deck
lights** sets the backlight of the keys and the LCD, and brightness dims it;
with none set it goes back to the colour it had. Ryu puts the backlight back as it found
it when it lets go. Quit Logitech G HUB or Logitech Gaming Software first if Ryu
cannot open the keyboard.

On a Logitech G915, G815 or G910 keyboard, Ryu takes only the G-keys (G1 to G5,
or G1 to G9 on the G910) and the lights; the rest of the keyboard still types,
and the G-keys stop doing their own actions while Ryu has them. The G513 and G
Pro have no G-keys, so Ryu sets only their backlight. A G-key's colour is its
light, the colour under **Deck lights** colours the backlight, and brightness
scales both. Ryu leaves the lights alone until one of those colours is set.
Every G-key you give an action has a colour (the default one unless you change
it), so binding a G-key takes the lights too: the whole backlight then shows
the **Deck lights** colour, and goes dark when none is set. Pick a backlight
colour under **Deck lights** if you want it lit.

On the G915 and G815, once none is set any more (and when Ryu lets go), the
keyboard goes back to its own effect; the G910, G513 and G Pro cannot be handed
back, so they go dark until they are replugged or Logitech's software takes
them. Quit Logitech G HUB or Logitech Gaming Software first, or it takes the
lights back. Only the G915 on its USB cable works, not over LIGHTSPEED or
Bluetooth, and M1 to MR are not read.
