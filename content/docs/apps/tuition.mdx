## What it does

A tutor for one learner - you. Point it at your own syllabus, chapter or notes and it builds a mastery model you can read: every skill carries a Bayesian Knowledge Tracing posterior, its prerequisite edges, and a spaced-repetition schedule computed in whole days so a review due tomorrow does not move because you studied at 23:58. "What should I study next" is a topological answer - the weakest skill whose prerequisites you have already mastered - not a guess, and "0.62 mastery, three more correct at this difficulty reaches 0.9" is a projection you can check by hand. Multiple-choice, cloze, numeric-with-tolerance and exact answers are graded by comparison with no model in the loop at all; only free-response answers are marked by a model, and those always show the rubric they were graded against. Given an exam date it projects, from your observed rate over the last ten sessions, whether the syllabus will be covered in time, and says `unknown` rather than extrapolating off two data points. A model is used at exactly two edges - proposing skills from a document, and writing practice items and rubric marks - and both are reviewed by a person before they count.

### MCP server

MCP (Model Context Protocol) is a standard way for an AI host to find and call tools. An MCP server provides those tools.

- **`tuition`** - Your own mastery model as tools, so any agent or workflow can tutor you and record what happened: `due` lists the skills whose review is owed and why, `quiz` hands back stored practice items for a skill, `grade` marks an answer (by comparison for objective kinds, against the written rubric for free response), `log` records an attempt so the posterior and the schedule actually move, and `mastery` reports the current posterior per skill with the projection of how many more correct answers reach the target. `due`, `quiz` and `mastery` are pure reads of computed state - a workflow branching on them takes the same path for the same data.

### Hook events

- **Reviews are due** - The scheduler's tick found at least one skill whose `due_at` has passed since the last emit. Fires once per tick per subject carrying every newly-due skill, not once per skill, and does not fire again for the same skill until it is reviewed and rescheduled. A skill that is merely weak is NOT due - the SM-2 interval decides due-ness, so a low-mastery skill reviewed an hour ago stays quiet. Pending review candidates from Study mode do NOT fire this either: they are not yet part of the deck and have their own accept/reject queue.
- **A skill fell back below mastered** - A graded attempt moved a skill's Bayesian Knowledge Tracing posterior from at-or-above the ready threshold to below it. It is a CROSSING, not a decrease: an attempt that lowers 0.55 to 0.48 does not fire, because the skill was never mastered, and neither does a wrong answer that leaves the posterior above the threshold. An attempt whose BKT denominator was degenerate is recorded as non-informative and leaves the posterior unchanged, so it cannot fire this. A free-response answer fires it only once the rubric grade lands, never while it is still awaiting marking.
- **The exam date is at risk** - The trajectory projection - remaining mastery work divided by the observed gain per session over the trailing ten sessions - needs more than 15% more sessions than the days remaining to the exam allow. Deterministic from stored state, so the numbers in the payload reproduce. It does NOT fire with fewer than three sessions of history (the trajectory reports `unknown` instead of extrapolating off one data point), does NOT fire when the subject has no exam date set, and fires at most once per day per subject so a run of bad sessions is one warning rather than five.
