This page lists the decks in this family that Decks supports and how each one maps to Ryu keys, dials, and lights. To give keys labels, colours, and actions, start with [Decks](/docs/apps/decks). Before Ryu opens a deck, [close other deck software](/docs/apps/decks#close-other-deck-software), and on Linux add the [udev rules](/docs/apps/decks-linux).

## Device notes

### Behringer

On a Behringer X-Touch Mini, keys 1 to 8 are the top row of buttons and keys 9
to 16 the bottom row, left to right; Layer A and Layer B are keys 17 and 18.
Each button's light has one colour and is only on or off: any colour except
black or near-black turns it on. The eight encoders are dials 1 to 8, and
pushing one presses its dial; its ring lights in full for any dial colour
except black. The fader is dial 9 and has no light: moving it turns its dial
by how far it moved, from where it was first touched. There is no brightness
setting. Ryu switches the deck to its MC (Mackie Control) mode, so Layer A and
Layer B no longer switch layers while Ryu drives it.

Close other music apps using it first: on Windows only one app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing
it draws over Ryu's lights or changes its mode. When Ryu lets go, the lights go
off and the deck returns to its Standard mode. On Linux, Ryu reaches it
through ALSA, like the Launchpads.

On a Behringer X-Touch One, first select MIDI CC mode in the deck's setup: in
its Mackie Control mode Ryu cannot read it. Keys 1 to 35 are its buttons in
the order the deck numbers them, from Segment Solo, BPM and Master to the Up,
Left, Enter, Right and Down keys, so their places in the Companion are not
where they sit on the deck. Each button's light has one colour and is only on
or off: any colour except black or near-black turns it on. Key 36 is touching
the fader and has no light. The encoder, the jog wheel and the fader are dials
1 to 3 with no light; moving the fader turns its dial by how far it moved,
from where it was first touched.

The deck light colours the display's
backlight in the nearest of its eight colours (white when none is set; black
turns the display off), and the display shows Ryu's name. There is no
brightness setting. Close other music apps using it first: on Windows only
one app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it draws over Ryu's lights.
When Ryu lets go, the lights and displays go off. On Linux, Ryu reaches it
through ALSA, like the Launchpads.

On a Behringer BCR2000 or BCF2000, Ryu writes its own preset into the deck's
edit buffer when it connects, which takes a few seconds. It is never stored, so
your saved presets are untouched; choosing one on the deck brings it back, and
Ryu's keys and dials stop matching until the deck reconnects. Keys 1 to 16 are
the two rows of buttons and keys 17 to 20 the four buttons at the lower right
(top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right). Each button's light has one
colour and is only on or off: any colour except black or near-black turns it
on. Dials 1 to 8 are the top push encoders, whichever of the four encoder
groups is selected, and pushing one presses its dial.

On the BCR2000, dials 9 to 32 are the three rows of encoders below, row by row from the top (an order
not yet confirmed); on the BCF2000, dials 9 to 16 are the motor faders, which
Ryu never moves, each turning its dial a step for every two units it moves.
Encoders are put back to the middle of their range after each turn, and their
rings show only that. There is no brightness setting. Both models report the
same USB id, so Ryu tells them apart by their MIDI port name. On Linux, Ryu
reaches them through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Support is untested on
hardware.

### PreSonus

On a PreSonus ATOM, keys 1 to 16 are the pads, row by row from the top left,
and keys 17 to 36 the buttons, listed four to a row below the pads rather than
where they sit on the deck: Shift, Zoom, Note Repeat, Full Level, Bank, Preset,
Show/Hide, Nudge, Editor, Set Loop, Setup, Up, Down, Left, Right, Select,
Click, Record, Play and Stop. Shift and Zoom are plain keys. Ryu switches the
deck to its native mode when it connects. Each pad's light takes the key's
colour; the buttons' lights are only on or off, and any colour except black or
near-black turns one on. The deck has no brightness command, so Ryu dims the
pad colours itself, and 0% turns every light off. The four knobs are dials 1
to 4, with no light.

Close Ableton Live, Studio One and other music apps using
it first: on Windows only one app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it
changes its mode or draws over Ryu's lights. When Ryu lets go, the lights go
off and the deck leaves native mode. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA,
like the Launchpads. Support is untested on hardware.

On a PreSonus ATOM SQ, keys 1 to 16 are the upper row of pads and keys 17 to
32 the lower row, left to right. Keys 33 to 63 are the buttons, listed on the
last two rows rather than where they sit on the deck: Bank A to H, the six
buttons under the display, Shift, Song, Instrument, Editor, User, Display Left
and Right, Up, Down, Left, Right, Click, Record, Play, Stop, Plus and Minus.
Ryu switches the deck to its native mode when it connects. The lights of the
pads, Bank A to H, Play, Plus and Minus take the key's colour; the other
buttons' lights are only on or off, and any colour except black or near-black
turns one on. The deck has no brightness command, so Ryu dims the colours
itself, and 0% turns every light off.

The display shows Ryu and, above each of
the six display buttons, that key's label. The eight knobs are dials 1 to 8,
the display knob dial 9 and the touch strip dial 10, with no light. Close
Ableton Live, Studio One and other music apps using it first: on Windows only
one app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it changes its mode or draws
over Ryu's lights. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off and the deck leaves
native mode. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads.
Support is untested on hardware.

On the original PreSonus FaderPort, Ryu asks the deck who it is when it
connects and then switches it to its native mode. Keys 1 to 24 are its buttons
in the order the deck numbers them, from User to Off, so their places on the
page are not where they sit on the deck; each light is only on or off, and any
colour except black or near-black turns one on. Key 25 is the footswitch and
key 26 touching the fader, with no light. The encoder is dial 1 and the fader
dial 2; moving the fader turns its dial by how far it moved, and Ryu never
moves it. There is no brightness setting.

On a PreSonus FaderPort 2, FaderPort 8 or FaderPort 16, first power the deck up
in its native (Studio One) mode: in its Mackie Control or HUI mode Ryu cannot
read it. On the FaderPort 8 and 16, the first four rows of keys are each
channel's Select, Mute and Solo buttons and touching its fader, left to right;
the other buttons follow, from Arm to the footswitch, rather than where they
sit on the deck. The FaderPort 2's 28 keys are its buttons in a fixed order,
from Arm to Record, then the footswitch and the fader touch. Select, Bypass,
Link and some others take the key's colour; the other lights are only on or
off, and the encoder pushes, footswitch and fader touches have none. The
encoders are the first dials and the faders the dials after them; Ryu never
moves the faders. On the FaderPort 8 and 16, Ryu clears the scribble strips.
There is no brightness setting.

For every FaderPort, close Studio One, Ardour and other music apps using it
first: on Windows only one app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it draws
over Ryu's lights. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off. On Linux, Ryu reaches
it through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Support is untested on hardware.

### iCON and Mackie control surfaces

The iCON Platform M+ and Platform M must be powered up in their Mackie Control
mode (chosen on the deck as it starts): in the other mode Ryu cannot read the
knobs and says so in its log. Columns 1 to 8 are the channel strips, top down
Select, Mute, Solo, Rec and touching the fader; column 9 is Mixer, Read, Write,
Cycle and touching the master fader; the bottom row is Channel left and right,
Bank left and right, Rewind, Forward, Stop, Play and Record. Each light is only on or off, and the
fader touches have none; Zoom and Lock send nothing to Ryu. Dials 1 to 8 are
the knobs (pressing one presses its dial), dial 9 the jog wheel and dials 10 to
18 the faders, master last; Ryu never moves the motor faders. There is no
display or brightness setting.

When Ryu lets go, the lights go off. The
Platform M is assumed to match the M+. No USB id is known for either, so on
Linux add a `sound` udev line with the ids `lsusb` shows if your desktop does
not already allow sound devices. Support is untested on hardware.

The iCON Platform X+ extender must be powered up in its Mackie Control mode
(chosen on the unit as it starts): in its other modes it sends other messages.
Each X+ is its own deck, found by its port name (Platform X+1, Platform X+2
and so on). Each column is a channel strip, top down pushing the knob, Select,
Mute, Solo, Rec and touching the fader. Only Select, Mute, Solo and Rec have
lights, each only on or off. Dials 1 to 8 are the knobs and dials 9 to 16 the
faders; Ryu never moves the motor faders. There is no display or brightness
setting. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off. No USB id is known, so on Linux
add a `sound` udev line with the ids `lsusb` shows if your desktop does not
already allow sound devices. Support is untested on hardware.

The iCON QCon Pro X and its QCon Pro XS1 extender must be in their Mackie
Control (MCU) mode; in HUI or another DAW mode their buttons send other notes,
and Ryu says so in its log. Each unit is its own deck with its own MIDI port.
The top rows are each strip's Rec, Solo, Mute, Select and V-Pot push. On the
Pro X the global buttons follow in Mackie note order (Track to User B) with
their Mackie names, because where each sits on the deck is not known, then the
fader touches; on the XS1 the last row is the fader touches. Rec, Solo, Mute,
Select and the Pro X's Track to Scrub lights are only on or off. The display
shows each strip's Select label over its V-Pot push label, six characters
each.

Dials 1 to 8 are the V-Pots; on the Pro X dial 9 is the jog wheel and
dials 10 to 18 the faders, master last, and on the XS1 dials 9 to 16 the
faders. Ryu never moves the motor faders; whether a fader moves back when you
let go of it is not known. There is no brightness setting. When Ryu lets go,
the lights go off and the displays are blanked. No USB id is known for
either, so on Linux add a `sound` udev line with the ids `lsusb` shows if your
desktop does not already allow sound devices. Support is untested on hardware.

The iCON QCon Pro G2 and its QCon EX G2 extender work the same way, in their
Mackie Control (MCU) mode, each as its own deck. On the Pro G2 the global
buttons follow the strip rows in Mackie note order (Track to Scrub) and the
fader touches come last; on the EX G2 the last row is the fader touches. Keys
carry the Mackie function names: the legends printed on the G2 differ on many
buttons, and the references Ryu read disagree on them, so press a button to
find its key. Dials 1 to 8 are the V-Pots, and each V-Pot ring lights its
centre dot while the dial's light is on; on the Pro G2 dial 9 is the jog wheel
and dials 10 to 18 the faders, master last, and on the EX G2 dials 9 to 16 the
faders. Ryu never moves the motor faders. There is no brightness setting.

No USB id is known for either, so on Linux add a `sound` udev line with the ids
`lsusb` shows if your desktop does not already allow sound devices. Support is
untested on hardware.

The iCON P1-Nano works the same way in its Mackie Control (MCU) mode. The top
rows are each strip's Rec, Solo, Mute, Select and V-Pot push; the global
buttons follow in Mackie note order (Track to User B) under their Mackie
function names, then the fader touches. Which of them the P1-Nano has is not
known, so press a button to find its key; one it lacks never fires. Its D-5
touchscreen is not read. Button lights are only on or off, and the display
shows each strip's Select label over its V-Pot push label. Dials 1 to 8 are the
V-Pots: a V-Pot's dial light colours the backlight of the scribble strip above
it (white with no colour set, off for black). Dial 9 is the jog wheel and dial
10 the fader, without a light.

The fader is read only: moving it turns its
dial on whichever strip it is set to, and Ryu never moves the motor fader.
There is no brightness setting. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off, the
display is blanked and the backlights turn white. The P1-M and the P1-X
extender are not supported, because their MIDI port names are not known.
Support is untested on hardware.

Boot a Mackie MCU Pro in Mackie Control mode (Logic Control mode works too;
in HUI or another emulation Ryu cannot read it). Ryu writes nothing until the
deck answers its query, except that on Windows and Linux the MCU Pro also
takes over its lights and display as soon as it sends a button, V-Pot or fader
message. The MCU Pro's three MIDI jack ports always show as XT Pro decks (on a
Mac, as more MCU Pro decks), even with nothing chained; each sends only the
Mackie query every two seconds until an XT Pro chained there answers, and that
XT Pro becomes that deck. On a Mac a chained XT Pro uses the MCU Pro's key
layout. A stand-alone XT Pro on USB is not found.

The keys are each strip's Rec, Solo, Mute, Select and V-Pot push, the global buttons
under their Mackie function names and the fader touches. Button lights are
only on or off. The V-Pots, the jog wheel and the faders are dials; each
V-Pot's LED ring lights as its dial light, and Ryu never moves the motor
faders. The display shows Ryu and the timecode digits are blanked. There is no
brightness setting. Support is untested on hardware.

### Zoom recorders

A Zoom R16 or R24 recorder works as a control deck once you choose USB, then
AUDIO I/F, then EXECUTE on the recorder and set its control surface to Mackie
Control; in storage mode it has no MIDI port. The R16 and R24 look the same to
Ryu. The top row is the eight track status keys: the recorder's MASTER status
key chooses whether they arm, solo or mute, and each presses the same key
either way. Below are AUTO PUNCH I/O, A-B REPEAT, `MARKER |<<`, `MARKER >>|` and
MARK (F1 to F5) and the 1-8Tr and 9-16Tr bank keys, then the transport and
cursor keys. Only the status keys may light, on or off, and that is not
confirmed. Dials 1 to 8 are the track faders, dial 9 the master fader and dial
10 the jog dial; the faders have no motor, so the first move of each only sets
where it is. There is no display or brightness setting. Support is untested on
hardware.
