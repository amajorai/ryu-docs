This guide covers turning speech in imported audio or video into transcripts
and timed captions in [Video Studio](/docs/apps/video-studio). Transcription and
translation use the services configured on the Ryu node. Review the words and
timing before export. The [timeline editing guide](/docs/apps/video-studio-editing)
and [media generation guide](/docs/apps/video-studio-generation) cover related work.

## Transcribe source media

In **Transcript**, choose a source video or audio file and, optionally, its
**Spoken language** (automatic detection or a specific language). **Transcribe
with Ryu** uses the node's speech-recognition service and saves progress in
20-second windows; you can stop after the current window and resume later. The
language is sent to the engine as a hint and stored with the transcript;
engines that do not accept a hint ignore it. Engine segment timestamps are kept
when available, and Video Studio never claims word timing the engine did not
supply. **Transcribe again** replaces an older transcript; existing timeline
captions stay until you apply the new one.

In Desktop, **Transcribe with Ryu** uses the engine you choose in
Ryu's Voice Recognition settings, the same one chat voice input uses. With
Parakeet, Whisper, or audio.cpp, the audio is transcribed on your node and stays
there. With Gateway, your node sends the audio to the speech-to-text provider
configured for your Gateway.

## Apply measured captions

When the engine supplies word timestamps, **Apply word captions** places
individually timed words on the timeline, following each clip's trim and speed.
**Apply highlighted captions** groups the words into short lines and highlights
the word being spoken. Editing a line's text or timing, or translating it,
removes its word timing; apply the measured captions again to restore it. MP4
exports keep highlighting; SRT and WebVTT downloads contain line text and
timing.

**Download subtitles** in the Captions tab writes the active caption track as
SRT, WebVTT, or plain text. Pick the format in **Subtitle format**. Plain text
(TXT) has one caption per line in playback order and no timestamps, which suits
a transcript or show notes.

## Edit by transcript

**Timeline transcript** in the Transcript area shows the words of every
transcribed clip in playback order, with an optional track filter. Click a word
to seek; the word being played is highlighted. Search with **Next** and
**Previous** (or Enter and Shift+Enter). Shift-click, Cmd/Ctrl-click, or drag to
select words, then press Delete to cut them: clips, captions, and timed layers
ripple on every sync-locked track. Clips with imported captions but no word
timing show whole caption lines.

**Repeated phrases and retakes** lists phrases of two to six words repeated
straight away (with up to two fillers between, within ten seconds) and
sentences of three or more words that the next sentence restarts. Each
suggestion removes the earlier take, along with up to a second of pause before
the take you keep. **Remove all** and large selections work in batches of 100
ranges.

## Style captions

**Caption style** in the Captions area offers 12 presets (Classic, Punch,
Karaoke, Boxed, Spotlight, Minimal, Broadcast, Glow, Reveal, Marker, Cinema, and
Headline) and lets you set the font (a bundled font, or an imported or
installed system font under **Imported or system font**), size, weight, uppercase, text, highlight,
outline, and box colors, outline and shadow size, box opacity, horizontal and
vertical position, maximum lines, and a **Word animation** (none, highlight,
pop, bounce, box, karaoke, or reveal). **Apply style** saves it for the selected
caption track. The preview and the export match; boxed reveal styles grow the
box word by word. Once speakers are detected, captions are tinted by speaker.
An imported or system font falls back to the chosen bundled **Font** when it is
missing, as it does for titles.

**Caption timing** regroups the captions: **Keep sentences**, **Short
phrases** (with a maximum word and character count), or **One word at a time**, with a
minimum duration and closing of small gaps. **Profanity** can leave words as
spoken, mask them (`f***`), or remove them, and **Also filter** adds your own
words (remembered in this browser). Choose **Rechunk captions** to apply.
**Suggest emoji** asks Ryu's language model for emoji; each one is added only
after you tick it.

## Import subtitle files

**Import captions** reads SRT, WebVTT, ASS, and SSA files. **Skip malformed cues
and report them** imports what it can and lists each skipped cue with its line
number; **Add to the existing captions** appends instead of replacing. A file
can hold up to 5000 captions.

You can also import a source SRT or WebVTT file in **Transcript**. **Apply to
timeline captions** maps its cues onto every use of that source, following
trims and speed.

## Fix names and terms

Speech recognition often misspells names, brands, and jargon. **Fix names and
terms** in the Captions tab keeps a list of fixes for the project: type what
the captions say in **Heard as** and the right spelling in **Correct
spelling**, then choose **Add fix**. **Suggest fixes with Ryu** asks Ryu's
language model for likely misheard proper nouns and technical terms, using the
project title and brief; review the suggestions and choose **Add suggestions**
to keep them.

**Fix captions and transcripts** replaces every whole-word or whole-phrase
match, ignoring case, in the source captions and in the saved transcripts of
media on the timeline, as one undoable step. Word timing is kept, so
highlighted captions still follow the speech. Turn on **Also fix every caption
language track** to include translated tracks. The list is saved with the
project, so you can apply it again after a new transcription.

## Detect speakers

**Speakers** in the Transcript area labels caption words by speaker. Choose
**Loudest mic** when each speaker has their own microphone (needs two or more
audible sources, such as linked or multicam mics) or **Voice clustering** for
one microphone with 2 to 6 speakers. Detection needs timed captions, and
word-level timing works best. Captions take the speaker with the most word
time, and clips get a colored stripe for their speaker; with clustering a clip
is tagged only when one speaker has at least 60% of its speech. Clips on locked
tracks are not re-tagged.

Rename, recolor, and merge speakers; running detection again keeps their ids,
colors, and names. Detection reads up to two hours of source audio per run. It
is a heuristic, not true diarization, so review the labels.

## Translate a reviewed track

In **Captions**, select a target language and choose **Translate with Ryu**.
Translation uses the node's language model and keeps caption IDs and timing in a
separate language track; malformed or incomplete responses leave the original
captions intact. The **Caption track** selector switches between the source
captions and each translation, and playback, burned-in captions, subtitle
downloads, dubbing, and agent timeline reads all use the selected track. Review
grammar and specialized terms before exporting.

## Create narration from captions

Use **Dub this caption track** to create a spoken audio track through Ryu's
narration engine, with an optional language code, voice, and speaking rate.
**Dubbing voice** lists the voices of the speech engines installed on your Ryu
node, grouped by engine and language, and picking one fills in its language.
The caption timings stay unchanged; the saved WAV is added to the timeline for
review and can be trimmed or ducked. Long tracks must be split before dubbing.
To narrate a title instead, see
[Speak a title](/docs/apps/video-studio-graphics#speak-a-title).

## Agent routes

Agents edit captions without sending the whole project through
`POST /api/video-studio/projects/{id}/captions` (`append`, `replace`, or
`remove` with the current `revision`; `trackId` is `null` for the source
captions). Style, regrouping, import, and name fixes use `caption-style`,
`caption-rechunk`, `caption-import`, and `caption-glossary` under the same
project path; `captions/rechunk` and
`captions/import` are accepted aliases. `language` on
`POST /api/video-studio/assets/{id}/transcript` sets the transcription hint, and
`POST /api/video-studio/projects/{id}/speakers/detect` runs speaker detection.
The full list is in [agent media and export](/docs/apps/video-studio-agents-media#captions-and-speakers).
