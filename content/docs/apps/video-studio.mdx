Video Studio is a separate editing workspace. Use [Clips](/docs/apps/clips) to
capture recordings, then import your footage into Video Studio alongside music,
voice recordings, and still images.

Video Studio is a multi-track editor with the tools of a desktop editing app:
razor, ripple, roll, slip, and slide edits, a clip clipboard, snapping, markers,
keyframes with easing curves and a graph editor, blend modes, masks, keys, color
wheels, curves, LUTs, an effects and transitions library, adjustment layers,
nested sequences, templates, a track mixer, audio cleanup and effects, styled
captions and titles, and exports from MP4 to ProRes 4444, WebM, GIF, audio, and
image sequences. Analysis and assisted tools add beat detection, voice-activity
cleanup, speaker labels, transcript editing, shot search, automatic J-cuts and
L-cuts, auto reframe, motion tracking, AutoCut, vertical shorts, and script to
video. Editing and analysis run on your Ryu node; only the generation features
call a model.

## Limits and requirements

- A project holds up to 5000 clips on 32 tracks, 1000 titles, and 1000
  graphics, and runs for up to two hours. A custom canvas can be up to 8192
  pixels on a side; aspect and quality presets stay within 3840.
- Supported frame rates are 23.976, 24, 25, 29.97, 30, 48, 50, 59.94, 60, and
  120. NTSC rates use exact 1000/1001 timebases in exports and handoff files.
- Clip speed runs from 0.1× to 100×, and each imported file can be up to 2 GB.
- The node needs FFmpeg and ffprobe. Some features need optional FFmpeg filters
  or encoders: `lut3d` for LUTs, `arnndn` for voice isolation, `backgroundkey`
  and `prores_ks` for background cutout, `drawtext` for text masks,
  `minterpolate` for optical-flow interpolation, and libvpx-vp9, libmp3lame, or
  10-bit libx265 for those exports. When one is missing, the matching control is
  disabled and names what is missing.
- Asking an agent about the timeline and recording a voiceover each need an
  optional permission that is granted separately from enabling the app.

## Media operations and concurrency

Exports, local upscales, nested timeline rendering, frame capture and inspection,
color inspection, and audio sync share two active media-operation slots. Busy
requests start no work and enter no waiting queue. Captures, inspections, and
audio sync have a one-minute deadline. Other operations have a thirty-minute
deadline, including their nested work and cleanup.
Canceling an export or stopping the app cancels its active media work.

A nested-timeline operation uses one snapshot and reuses each completed source
revision. It supports up to 64 source projects, 512 distinct sequence bindings,
and 10 GB of working data. Separate sequence clips keep their own bindings.
The project duration and output-dimension limits above still apply.

Frame capture and inspection seek directly to the requested time, including
nested timelines, and render a single composed frame. Disconnecting from an
inspection cancels its active work. Separate clips keep their own source times.
Capture reserves media-library storage before rendering and rejects a changed
source timeline instead of saving a stale frame.
Active textured meshes and animated rigs are sampled at the chosen frame time,
including inside nested timelines.

Audio sync compares the selected trims at the measured waveform's resolution
and runs in the background so editing stays responsive. A short trim from a long,
coarsely measured recording may not contain enough samples: use a longer shared
sound or a manual offset. If the timeline changes during sync, reload before
trying again; the newer edit is preserved.

Media analysis limits probe metadata to 8 MB, including file-backed output.
Oversized metadata is rejected before JSON parsing; probe output files are removed
after success or failure.

Local soundtracks can be up to two hours long. Video Studio runs at most two
soundtrack operations at once across its connected clients, including generation,
import, and temporary-file cleanup. Additional requests receive a busy response
without starting work or entering a waiting queue; try again after a soundtrack
finishes. Background analysis retains its separate operation limit.

Stock imports share media-operation admission and honor the media library's
asset and storage limits before downloading. NASA metadata is read only from
trusted HTTPS NASA hosts; redirects are rejected.

Mesh imports limit expanded scene nodes, animation channels, and joint data.
Mesh export runs in a background worker and honors export cancellation. If a
mesh exceeds the supported rendering work or temporary-image limit, reduce its
geometry, export resolution, or duration before retrying.

## Editing safeguards

Deleting a linked clip checks every affected track, including an unselected
partner's track. Unlock those tracks before deleting the linked group.

Partial settings updates preserve an omitted export preset. Change that preset
explicitly when switching between master, review, and social delivery.

Production decisions are appended through the decision log. Ordinary project
edits and undo preserve existing entries; the log cannot be rewritten or cleared
through a project update.

## Scene review and approval

Undo and history restore recover editorial changes, not reviewer authority.
Reopened or changed scenes remain unapproved when an older version is restored.
An unchanged scene keeps its current approval; restored scene content needs a
fresh review before an approval-required export.

Changing **Require scene approval** requires scene-approval permission. Ordinary
editing and undo preserve that requirement and locked notes on approved scenes.
Reopen review before changing those notes. An unavailable scene preview stops
retrying automatically; choose **Retry unavailable previews** to try it again.

Agents update the requirement through
`POST /api/video-studio/projects/{id}/approval-policy` with the current `revision`
and a boolean `requireApproval`. Imported production packages retain their review
requirement; imported approvals still need fresh review on the new project.

## Production readiness

The **Production readiness** card in **Plan** reports the brief, scene plan,
scene assets, creative approval, timeline, and delivery review separately.
**Export gate ready** means the project can be rendered. **Delivery ready**
appears only after the current saved revision passes a post-render review.
See [production review](/docs/apps/video-studio-finishing#production-readiness).

## Gateway budget snapshot

Generation history can show a read-only snapshot of the node's Gateway spend
and, with the optional audit permission, exact receipts matched by generation
request. Video Studio never estimates a cost from that snapshot or attributes
node-wide spend to a project. See
[agent access](/docs/apps/video-studio-agents#gateway-budget-snapshot).

Gateway receipts require the separate administrative audit permission. An existing installation
keeps its current approvals until a node administrator permits and approves that permission.
Until then, generation budgets use conservative estimates rather than claiming confirmed costs;
ordinary usage summaries remain available. After approval, receipts can confirm actual costs by
matching the generation request identifier.

The production activity ledger shows the newest 200 events. Project budget
admission includes older generation requests even when the visible history shows
only recent entries. Pending and unsettled requests remain counted against the cap.

## Data at rest

Video Studio encrypts its project records on disk with a key only this app receives from Ryu: projects and their undo history (titles, scripts, captions, scene prompts), transcripts, the app's transcript search indexes, generation requests and their prompts, and media analyses. The media library list (names, kinds, durations), export jobs, and timestamps stay unencrypted. Imported and generated media, imports in progress, and rendered exports are ordinary video, audio, and image files in the app's private data folder, readable only by your account, because the video tools read them directly; they are not encrypted.

Cached project poster frames, imported fonts and LUTs, and shot analysis results (including shot descriptions used by media search) are also files in that folder, readable only by your account, and are not encrypted. While an export, frame capture or transcription runs, its temporary work files, such as the caption file, are unencrypted copies; they are deleted when the job ends, and any left by a crash are deleted the next time the app starts. Records saved by an earlier version are encrypted the first time the updated app starts. If the app is updated before Ryu itself, new records stay unencrypted until Ryu delivers its key, and the app reports that it is waiting for a Ryu update.
