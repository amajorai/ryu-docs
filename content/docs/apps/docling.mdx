## What it does

Document parsing via IBM's MIT-licensed Docling - the highest-fidelity `document.parse` backend. Where a text extractor hands a model a stream of characters, Docling runs real layout analysis: a page-segmentation model finds the reading order, a table-structure model rebuilds merged cells and spanning headers, and the result is exported as markdown whose headings, lists and tables mirror the document's actual structure. That is what makes a multi-column paper or a financial statement survive chunking instead of interleaving into nonsense. The cost is weight: the first parse downloads its models (hundreds of MB) and can take minutes, and every parse after that is CPU-bound inference rather than a string copy. Everything runs locally - Docling explicitly supports air-gapped operation once the models are cached - and no document ever leaves the node. Parses run as jobs: submit returns 202 + a job_id, the caller polls.
