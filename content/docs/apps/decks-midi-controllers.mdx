This page lists the decks in this family that Decks supports and how each one maps to Ryu keys, dials, and lights. To give keys labels, colours, and actions, start with [Decks](/docs/apps/decks). Before Ryu opens a deck, [close other deck software](/docs/apps/decks#close-other-deck-software), and on Linux add the [udev rules](/docs/apps/decks-linux).

## Device notes

### KORG

On a KORG nanoKONTROL2, keys 1 to 24 are the S, M and R buttons of the eight
channel strips (S1 to S8, then M1 to M8, then R1 to R8), followed by Track ◀
and ▶, Cycle, Marker Set, Marker ◀ and ▶, Rewind, Forward, Stop, Play and
Record. Each button's light has one colour and is only on or off: any colour
except black or near-black turns it on. The Track and Marker buttons have no
light. The knobs are dials 1 to 8 and the sliders dials 9 to 16, with no
light; moving one turns its dial by how far it moved, from where it was first
touched. There is no brightness setting. Ryu puts the deck in KORG native mode
while it drives it, so it needs no KONTROL Editor setup whatever scene, MIDI
channel or LED mode is set, and when Ryu lets go the deck returns to its
scene.

Close other music apps using it first: on Windows only one app can hold
it. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads.

A KORG nanoKONTROL Studio works the same way over USB (not Bluetooth), found
by its MIDI port name: keys 1 to 32 are the Solo, Mute, Rec and Select buttons
of the eight strips, followed by Track ◀ and ▶, Cycle, Marker Set, Marker ◀
and ▶, Return to Zero, Rewind, Forward, Stop, Play and Record, and key 45 is
the Scene button, which has no light and no longer changes scenes while Ryu
drives the deck. The knobs are dials 1 to 8, the sliders dials 9 to 16 and the
jog wheel dial 17. Its lights use the nanoKONTROL2's commands, which KORG does
not document for the Studio, so they are not yet confirmed on a real deck.

A KORG padKONTROL is driven in KORG native mode through its second MIDI port
(Port 2: PORT A in, CTRL out), found by its port name; close KORG's padKONTROL
Editor/Librarian and any music app holding that port first. Keys 1 to 16 are
the pads, pad 1 top left; keys 17 to 35 are the buttons from Scene to Hold;
key 36 is touching the X-Y pad and key 37 a momentary pedal switch, such as
the PS-1, in the PEDAL jack. Each pad and button light has one colour and is
only on or off; the touch and the pedal have none, and there is no brightness
setting.

The data-entry wheel is dial 1, Knob 1 and 2 dials 2 and 3, and the
X-Y pad's X and Y dials 4 and 5, with no light; a knob or the X-Y pad turns its
dial by how far it moved, from where it was first touched (the X-Y pad again on
each touch). The display is blank, and the deck's scenes and Setting mode do
nothing while Ryu drives it: it shows the native-mode lights only while Ryu
runs, and returns to its scene when Ryu lets go. Holding Scene and
Message/Exit and pressing Setting/Enter leaves native mode by hand. Support is
untested on hardware.

### Arturia

On an Arturia BeatStep, keys 1 to 16 are the pads, top row then bottom row,
and keys 17 to 19 are Shift, Stop and Play, shown to the left of the pads.
Each pad's light is red, blue or both (pink), with no other colours or
dimming: red, orange and yellow keys glow red, green, cyan and blue keys glow
blue, and purple, magenta, white and grey keys glow both; black or near-black
turns it off. Shift, Stop and Play have no light, and there is no brightness
setting. The 16 encoders are dials 1 to 16, top row then bottom row, and the
large encoder is dial 17, which may stay a volume knob on some firmware.
Holding Shift, Chan, Recall or Store and pressing a pad still runs the deck's
own functions.

About a second after it connects, Ryu writes its own mapping
into the deck's working memory, never into a saved preset: when Ryu lets go,
the pads go dark, and pressing Recall brings back your own mapping. The
BeatStep Pro is not supported. Close Ableton Live, Arturia's MIDI Control
Center and other music apps using it first: on Windows only one app can hold
it, and elsewhere one sharing it changes its mapping or draws over Ryu's
lights. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads.

On an Arturia MiniLab 3, Ryu uses the deck's DAW program through its main MIDI
port, never the MCU/HUI, DIN THRU or ALV ports. When it connects, the screen
shows "Ryu" and "Connected". Keys 1 to 8 are the eight pads in bank A and keys
9 to 16 the same pads in bank B: the deck's own bank button switches between
the two rows. Key 17 is Shift, with no light, and keys 18 to 22 are Loop, Stop,
Play, Record and Tap. Every key but Shift shows its colour as its light, dimmed
by the brightness setting. The eight encoders are dials 1 to 8, the main
encoder dial 9 (dial 10 while Shift is held; pushing it presses that dial), and
the four faders dials 11 to 14, with no light.

While another program is picked
on the deck, Ryu ignores it; going back to the DAW program sends every light
again. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off and the deck is handed back. Close
Ableton Live, Analog Lab and other music apps using it first. On Linux, Ryu
reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Support is untested on hardware.

On an Arturia KeyLab Essential mk3 (49, 61 or 88 keys), Ryu uses the deck's
DAW program through its main MIDI port. When it connects, the screen shows
"Ryu" and "Connected". Keys 1 to 8 are the pads in bank A (top row, then
bottom row) and keys 9 to 16 the same pads in bank B: the deck's own Bank
button switches between them. Keys 17 to 20 are Context 1 to 4, keys 21 to 24
Save, Punch, Undo and Redo, keys 25 to 32 Loop, Rewind, Fast-forward,
Metronome, Stop, Play, Record and Tap, key 33 Part and key 34 Bank, which has
no light. Every other key shows its colour as its light, dimmed by the
brightness setting. The nine knobs are dials 1 to 9, the display encoder dial
10 (pushing it presses that dial), and the nine faders dials 11 to 19, with no
light.

While another program is picked on the deck, Ryu ignores it; going back
to the DAW program sends every light again. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off
and the deck is handed back. Close Ableton Live, Bitwig Studio, Analog Lab and
other music apps using it first. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like
the Launchpads. Support is untested on hardware.

### DJ TechTools

On a DJ TechTools Midi Fighter Twister, the 16 knobs are dials 1 to 16, row by
row from the top left, and pushing one presses its dial. Keys 1 to 4 are the
side buttons at the left top, right top, left bottom and right bottom, with no
light; the two middle side buttons still switch the deck's banks, and every
bank works the same. Each dial light takes the nearest colour on the knob's
colour wheel (white and grey show the knob's own active colour), and the
brightness dims both the light and the white ring. Ryu puts a knob back to the
middle of its ring once you stop turning it, so pause briefly during a long
turn: the knob stops at either end.

Ryu needs the deck's factory settings: if
you changed its knobs, side buttons, MIDI channels or palette in the Midi
Fighter Utility, restore the defaults there first. Holding both bottom buttons
can switch newer firmware to its native mode, and both middle buttons start the
step sequencer on four-bank firmware. On 2026 firmware the deck blanks its
lights after an hour untouched until you touch it, unless you change its sleep
setting. When Ryu lets go, the knobs show your own colours again. Close the
Utility, your DJ software and other music apps using it first: on Windows only
one app can hold it. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the
Launchpads.

On a DJ TechTools Midi Fighter Spectra, keys 1 to 16 are the arcade buttons,
row by row from the top left. The six side buttons still switch the deck's
banks, and every bank works the same: Ryu lights each button in all four. Each
light takes the nearest of the deck's palette colours (white and grey show
white); there is no brightness command, so below 50% the buttons use the
palette's dim colours (white has none, so white and grey stay bright), and 0%
turns them off. The deck light sets the white
ground-effect lights under the case to one level from its brightness. Ryu
needs the deck's factory settings: if you changed its notes or MIDI channels
in the Midi Fighter Utility, restore the defaults there first. When Ryu lets
go, every light goes off.

Close the Utility, your DJ software and other music
apps using it first: on Windows only one app can hold it. On Linux, Ryu
reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Support is untested on hardware.

On a DJ TechTools Midi Fighter 64, keys 1 to 64 are the arcade buttons, row
by row from the top left. Each light takes the nearest colour of the deck's
128-colour palette, and the brightness sets the lights' own brightness in 16
steps. Ryu reads the buttons in both banks (holding a corner button for 2
seconds switches bank, and that hold also counts as a key press), but its
lights assume the factory channel 3, the upper-left bank. Ryu needs the deck's
factory settings: if you changed its notes or MIDI channels in the Midi
Fighter Utility, restore the defaults there first. Ryu finds it by a MIDI port
name containing "Fighter 64". When Ryu lets go, every light goes off.

Close the Utility, your DJ software and other music apps using it first: on Windows only
one app can hold it. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the
Launchpads; its USB product id is not known, so add it (from `lsusb`) to the
`2580` `sound` line in the [Decks Linux udev rules](/docs/apps/decks-linux). Support is untested on hardware.

### Intech Studio Grid

An Intech Studio Grid module (EN16, PO16, BU16, EF44, PBF4 or TEK2) needs its
factory default profile: if you remapped its controls in Grid Editor, restore
the default first. Every module type looks the same to Ryu, so all of them
share one 4 x 4 layout of 16 keys and 16 dials, numbered by the module's own
control order: a key is a button or the push of an encoder or endless knob,
and a dial is an encoder, endless knob, knob or fader. On an EN16, keys and
dials 1 to 16 are its encoders, row by row from the top left; on a PO16, dials
1 to 16 are its knobs; on a BU16, keys 1 to 16 are its buttons. On a PBF4, dials
1 to 4 are the knobs, dials 5 to 8 the faders and keys 9 to 12 the buttons. On
an EF44, keys and dials 1 to 4 are the encoders and dials 5 to 8 the faders.

On a TEK2, keys 1 to 8 are the buttons, upper row first, and keys and dials 9 and
10 the left and right endless knobs. Only the module plugged into USB is read,
not modules chained to it, and each page its utility button selects keeps its
own positions. Ryu sets no colours or brightness: each light follows its own
control on the module. Each dial turns by how far its control moved, from
where it was first touched: each time Ryu opens the module, the first move of
every encoder, knob and fader on each page only sets that starting point and
is not counted. An encoder turned on past its end keeps turning its dial. Close Grid
Editor and other music apps using it first: on Windows only one app can hold
it. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Support is
untested on hardware.

### Faderfox, Livid, Allen & Heath and Intuitive Instruments

The Faderfox EC4 needs firmware 2.0 for Shift, the User keys and the encoder
names; on older firmware Ryu reads only the encoders and their pushes. Ryu
reads the setup you loaded on the deck and never changes it: factory setups 1
to 14 in any group, or the DrivenByMoss template. Sending DrivenByMoss's .syx
file rewrites all 16 setups, with the template in setup 1 and the others back
to factory. Setups 15 and 16 (Ableton's) are not read. Keys 1 to 16 are the
encoder pushes and dials 1 to 16 the encoders, as they sit on the deck; keys 17
to 20 are User 1 to 4 (hold FUNC and press encoder 1, 5, 9 or 13) and key 21 is
Shift, which the deck also uses for fine turning and in its menus.

The first four characters of each push key's label become its encoder's name on the
display, only where the setup's own names are still `----`; this is expected
to work in setups 1 to 14 but is untested on hardware. When Ryu lets go the
names go back to dashes. There are no lights or brightness setting. Under the
deck's default routing, MIDI arriving at its DIN input is passed on to USB, so
Ryu reads only the channel of the setup the deck is on. No USB id is known for
the EC4, so on Linux add a `sound` udev line with the ids `lsusb` shows if your
desktop does not already allow sound devices. Support is untested on hardware.

The Livid Instruments Base and Base II are found by their MIDI port name:
`Base8` for the Base, and `Base2`, `Base2 MIDI 1` or `Base2 Base_Controls` for
the Base II. Ryu uses only the first port and ignores the second. Keys 1 to 8
are the touch buttons across the top, keys 9 to 40 the 32 pads as they sit,
and keys 41 to 48 the eight function buttons, placed in the bottom row as a
layout choice. The 9 touch faders turn dials 1 to 9 (9 is the master fader);
their own lights are left alone. Each key's light shows the nearest of the
deck's seven colours (white, red, yellow, green, cyan, blue or magenta), or
off for black, with no brightness setting.

At start Ryu puts the pads in Live
mode and turns local control off; when Ryu lets go every light goes off, but
local control is left off until you power-cycle the deck or reset it in
Livid's editor. Close other apps using its MIDI port first. No USB id is known
for the Base, so on Linux add a `sound` udev line with the ids `lsusb` shows if
your desktop does not already allow sound devices. Support is untested on
hardware.

The Allen & Heath Xone:K2 is found by `XONE:K2` in its MIDI port name. Keys
1 to 4 are the top encoder pushes, keys 5 to 16 the three button rows under
the knobs and keys 17 to 32 the bottom 4 x 4 matrix; Layer and Exit Setup sit
at the ends of the last row. Each button's light shows red, amber or green, or
off for black, near-black or pure blue, with no brightness setting; Exit
Setup's is only on or off. The top encoders turn dials 1 to 4, the knobs dials
5 to 16, the faders dials 17 to 20 and the bottom encoders dials 21 and 22
(their pushes press them). A knob or fader turns its dial from its second
move, since the deck cannot report where it is. Keep Latching Layers off (the
factory default).

Ryu uses the MIDI channel the deck was set to at power-on
(15 by default), learnt from the first control you touch, so on another
channel the lights appear only after that. With X-Link-chained units, touch
the USB-attached K2 first: Ryu follows whichever unit it hears first and
ignores the other channels. The Xone:K1 and K3 are not recognised. When Ryu lets go
every light goes off. Close other apps using its MIDI port first. Support is
untested on hardware.

The Intuitive Instruments Exquis is found by a MIDI port whose name contains
`Exquis`, and Ryu puts it in Developer Mode, so the pads play no notes while
Ryu holds it. Keys 1 to 61 are the hexagonal pads, from the bottom left to the
top right, shown row by row with the top row first (the half-pad offset of
every other row is not shown). Below them come Record, Loop, Clips, Play/Stop,
Down and Up, then Undo and Redo, then the slider's six portions; these rows
are a logical arrangement, not where the controls sit. Every key lights in its
colour, and the deck has no brightness of its own, so Ryu dims the colours.
The four encoders turn and press dials 1 to 4, and each dial's colour lights
its encoder.

Settings and Sound stay the device's own, so its settings menu
still works, and Ryu redraws the lights when you leave it. When Ryu lets go
the lights go off and the Exquis leaves Developer Mode. Close other apps
using its MIDI port first. Support is untested on hardware.
