This guide covers preparing source media for a
[Video Studio](/docs/apps/video-studio) project before you edit it. Use
[timeline editing](/docs/apps/video-studio-editing) to place the prepared media
and adjust its timing, tracks, and playback. Sound-specific tools, such as
cleanup, ducking, and waveform video, are in the
[audio guide](/docs/apps/video-studio-audio).

## Organize the library

Expand **Organize** on a library item to rename it or move it into a relative
folder such as `B-roll/Sunset`; use the **Media folder** filter beside search to
browse one folder. **Delete media** removes the app-owned file and thumbnail and
removes its clips from saved projects after checking their revisions. Deleting
several items at once keeps media that a project still uses and names those
projects. Use **Preview** (or **Preview and mark** on video and audio) to look
at a source before adding it. Sorting, skimming, and marking a source range are described in the
[workspace guide](/docs/apps/video-studio-workspace#the-media-library).

## Analyze source footage

Choose **Analyze source** on an imported video or audio file to measure its
scene changes and audio envelope on your node. Analysis can be canceled and its
results are kept with the library. Each detected video range can be added to
the timeline as a clip; review the suggested boundaries before using them as
cuts. **Add loudness markers** turns separated loudness peaks into timeline
markers, and **Remove detected silence** ripple-deletes the silent ranges of
every matching audio clip.

**Detect beats** measures a music source's tempo, beats, and bar starts; see
[beats and cutting to music](/docs/apps/video-studio-assisted-editing#beats-and-cutting-to-music).
When no steady pulse is found, **Add estimated beat markers** offers a loudness
estimate instead. A project holds up to 500 markers.

## Find footage by look

Open **Find footage** and use **Find footage by look**. Choose a video with a
completed source analysis to measure its shots: dominant colors, brightness,
framing, and motion for each shot (similar neighboring cuts merge into one
shot). Then describe a look, such as "dark blue motion", "low light", or "not
dark". The search understands synonyms (dim, navy, cyan, closeup), phrases
("dimly lit", "well lit"), hyphenated words ("dark-blue"), and negation ("not
dark", "without blue"); words it does not know are listed as **Not a visual
term**. Drag a result to the timeline or choose **Add source range**.

Labels are measured heuristics, not image recognition. A source can have up to
400 shots, sampled at up to 4 frames per second. **Index shots in Space** adds
the shot descriptions to a Space so meaning search finds them too.

**Describe shots with AI** adds what a vision model sees: Video Studio sends one
small keyframe per shot, a few at a time, to the model you name (or the node
default) and stores a one-sentence caption and short tags with each shot. Then
words such as "dog on a beach" find described shots, combined with look words
("bright beach"). Shots without a description are still found by their
measured look. If the model cannot read images, Video Studio says that the
selected model cannot read images; choose a vision model and try again.

## Find footage by meaning

In **Find footage**, choose a completed source transcript and a Space name,
then choose **Index transcript**. This copies transcript ranges into that
Space; video and audio files stay in Video Studio. The Space's access settings
apply to the copied text, and Ryu Core performs indexing and semantic search.

Describe a moment in the search field, review the matching transcript ranges,
and choose **Add source range** to add one to the timeline. Newly saved
documents may take time to become searchable. After changing a source
transcript, index it again; **Rebuild index** creates a fresh projection and
preserves documents edited outside Video Studio. Search requires a Ryu host with
Spaces access and does not fall back to cached results when access is denied.

## Find open media

Open **Stock** to search Archive.org, Wikimedia Commons, NASA Images and Video,
Openverse images and audio, and, when the node has access configured, Unsplash
images and Pexels, Pixabay, and Coverr catalogs. Each result shows its source
page, attribution, rights text, format, and size before anything is downloaded.
**Import to library** accepts only the catalog identifier; the node resolves a
supported file, applies a 512 MB limit, probes it, and stores it with its
provenance. Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay, and Coverr require operator-provisioned
API keys on the node. Without a key, Stock reports that provider as unavailable.
Credentials stay on the node. Review the recorded rights and attribution before
publishing. NASA media is generally public domain in the United States, but
check item metadata for credited third-party material.

## Preview proxies

Video wider than 1920 pixels or above 30 Mb/s gets a hidden 960-pixel H.264
preview copy, made in the background after import. Turn on **Use proxies** in
the preview toolbar to play from these copies; at **Half** or **Quarter**
preview quality, Video Studio asks for lighter copies as needed. Proxies never
appear in the library or on the timeline, and exports always use the original
media. Agents can create proxies, list them, sweep missing ones, and turn the
automatic import-time proxy off.

## Stabilize, interpolate, and enhance

The inspector's media tools make new library media from a video clip; the
original is never changed.

- **Stabilize** (low, medium, or high, zooming 4%, 6%, or 8% on each edge)
  uses FFmpeg's `vidstab` filters when available. Otherwise a built-in tracker
  corrects camera shift but not rotation. Choose **Stabilize clip**.
- **Convert frame rate** resamples a video to a new rate with **Optical flow**
  (needs FFmpeg's `minterpolate`) or **Frame blending**, keeping its length.
- **Frame interpolation** with **Render smooth slow motion** renders smooth
  slow motion for a clip slower than 1×, keeping its speed and in and out points.
- **Enhance** denoises, upscales with Lanczos, and sharpens, up to 8192 pixels
  on the long edge. It is not an AI upscaler.

Stabilizing, interpolation, and enhanced video are limited to 10-minute sources.
Every derivative checks the library's storage limit. Leaving the default name
reuses an earlier identical result; a custom name always creates new media.
Visual library items also offer plain **2× upscale** and **4× upscale**
(Lanczos, up to 3840 pixels on the long edge).

## Color mattes and Lottie

**New color matte** creates a solid-color image (2 to 8192 pixels on a side,
the canvas size by default) and places it as a five-second still that holds for
any length you trim it to.

**Import Lottie** renders a Lottie `.json` animation (up to 5 MB) at the
project frame rate, scaled to fit 1920 pixels and up to 600 frames, into a
ProRes 4444 video with transparency, plus a lighter VP9 copy for the preview when
FFmpeg has VP9. Embedded images are kept, text is drawn as shapes, and
expressions do not run. You can cancel until encoding starts.

It also accepts dotLottie (`.lottie`) files up to 20 MB: Video Studio unpacks
the first animation in the file, with its images, and renders it the same way.
Lottie `.json` and `.lottie` files you drop on the window, or pick with
**Import media**, go through the same import.

## Offline media and relink

When a media file is missing or damaged, it shows as offline, and its timeline
clips get a hatched **Offline** badge. Exports are refused while any clip,
canvas background image, lip-sync audio, or nested sequence is offline, and the
message lists the files.

Choose **Relink offline media** in the Media tab to open the relink dialog.
Relink one file, or point it at a folder: files are matched by name (ignoring
extension, case, and copy suffixes) and then by duration. A replacement must be
the same kind of media and, for video and audio, within half a second of the
original's length; media already used in a project cannot be a replacement.
Clips, captions, and analysis stay attached. **Check for damaged files** scans
the library again.

## Study a reference video

Open **Reference** and choose an analyzed video. **Build reference profile**
saves its measured pacing (duration, dimensions, shot count, average shot
length, audio, caption coverage) and a sampled visual style. The generated
brief describes the observed rhythm so you can adapt the structure to an
original production; it does not copy the source media, transcript, or text.
Three editable concepts (preserve the rhythm, slow the reveal, or lead with a
faster hook) can be copied into **Plan** with **Use concept in Plan**.
