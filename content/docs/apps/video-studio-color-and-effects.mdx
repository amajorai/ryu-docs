This guide covers how a clip looks and moves: transform and framing, blend
modes, masks, keys, color, effects, adjustment layers, the canvas background,
layouts, transitions, and keyframes. Start with the
[Video Studio overview](/docs/apps/video-studio) and
[timeline editing](/docs/apps/video-studio-editing); agents reach the same
settings through the routes in [agent access](/docs/apps/video-studio-agents).

Every setting on this page is saved with the clip and drawn by both the editor
preview and the export. Where the browser cannot match the export exactly, the
inspector says so. Agents can set any clip field described here with
`POST /api/video-studio/projects/{id}/clip-properties`.

Some features need an optional FFmpeg filter on your node. When the filter is
missing, the control is disabled and names what is missing. Agents can read the
node's filters and encoders from `GET /api/video-studio/capabilities`.

## Transform, crop, and framing

Select a visual clip to set its position, scale, rotation, opacity, and crop
edges (top, right, bottom, left), or drag it directly in the
[preview](/docs/apps/video-studio-workspace#edit-on-the-canvas). **Edge shape**
rounds the corners (a percentage of the short edge) and feathers the alpha edge.

- **Flip horizontal** and **Flip vertical** mirror the source before rotation.
- **Scale X** and **Scale Y** (0.1 to 4) resize the clip's frame on one axis.
- **Fit** chooses how the picture fills that frame: **Fit** letterboxes (the
  default), **Fill** crops the center to cover the frame on top of any manual
  crop, and **Stretch** distorts the picture to the frame. Only Stretch
  distorts.

Rotated clips stay rectangular with Fill or Stretch. When Scale X or Scale Y is
keyframed under Fill, the crop keeps its first-frame shape and never distorts.
A freeze frame keeps the clip's flip and fit and bakes its Scale X and Scale Y
at the frozen moment.

## Blend modes

**Blend mode** combines a clip with the layers below it using one of 20 modes:
normal, multiply, screen, overlay, darken, lighten, color dodge, color burn,
hard light, soft light, difference, exclusion, add (plus lighter), subtract,
linear light, pin light, hue, saturation, color, and luminosity. Shapes take
blend modes too. Exports are exact.

When a clip on screen uses a blend mode and your browser supports WebGL2, the
preview composites the canvas background and every visible video and image
clip into one picture with the same maths as the export, while playing as
well as paused. This covers position, scale, fit, fill and stretch, rotation,
flips, crops (including keyframed crops), opacity, fades and entry motion,
masks, keys, and color grades. A few stages are not composited this way yet:
basic brightness, contrast and saturation changes without a grade, legacy and
CSS effects, vignettes, rounded or soft edges, shaped reveals and fades
through a color, blur during a transition or animation, fill with keyframed
horizontal or vertical scale, media whose pixel size is unknown, per-clip
backgrounds, mask strokes, adjustment layers on screen, and image or blur
canvas backgrounds.

While playing, an animated mask (keyframed position, size, rotation or
feather) can trail the picture by the moment it takes the browser to draw
each new mask shape (if it falls further behind, those frames use the
browser's blending); the paused frame uses the exact mask. When a clip's
media is not ready yet, for example a video clip that is still buffering as
playback reaches it, the preview shows the browser's blending for those
frames rather than holding an old picture.

Titles, graphics, and other overlays stay above the composited picture and
keep the browser's own blending. When a title uses a blend mode, or a shape
uses one the browser only approximates, the paused preview still shows the
exact rendered frame (see below); during playback those overlays use the
browser's approximation.

When the stack uses one of those stages, or without WebGL2, the preview falls
back to the browser's blending. Where that differs (subtract is shown as
difference, linear and pin light as hard light, and add brightens more while
the clip is partly transparent), the inspector says so and explains when the
exact frame appears: whenever playback is paused and your edits are saved,
the preview shows the exact rendered frame at the playhead, a moment after
you stop scrubbing. During playback, while edits are still unsaved, and when
the frame cannot be rendered on this node, the fallback uses the browser's
approximation.

Hue, saturation, color, and luminosity mix whole pixels rather than single
channels. Exports work in YCbCr: **Color** keeps the brightness of the layers
below and takes the clip's hue and saturation, **Luminosity** does the
reverse, **Hue** takes only the clip's hue, and **Saturation** takes only the
clip's saturation. A gray clip has no hue, so Hue and Saturation with a gray
clip turn the layers below gray. The browser uses its own RGB version of these
modes during playback; while paused, the preview shows the exact frame.

## Masks

Add up to four masks per clip: rectangle (with corner radius), ellipse, linear,
mirror, polygon (3 to 12 sides), star (points and inner radius), heart, text
(13 fonts), or freeform. Each mask has a position, size, rotation, feather,
opacity, and invert switch; overlapping masks intersect. Masks apply to the
clip's own picture after crop and flip and before scale and rotation.

Drag a mask in the preview: the center moves it, the corners resize it about
its center, the handle above rotates it, the handle on the right sets feather,
and freeform points move one at a time. Arrow keys nudge it, Shift by ten.
Mask position, size, rotation, and feather can be keyframed; removing a mask
removes its keyframes. Text masks need FFmpeg's `drawtext` filter, and their
text size does not animate. Static masks cost almost nothing to export;
animated and very detailed freeform masks take longer.

Give any mask except a text mask an outline **stroke**: a width (a fraction of
the clip's height, up to 0.1; 0 turns it off), a color, and an alignment.
**Center** straddles the mask edge, **Inside** lies within the shape the mask
keeps, and **Outside** surrounds it. The stroke is drawn over the masked clip
with round corners, follows keyframed mask position, size, and rotation, and
is not softened by feather, invert, or mask opacity. Very detailed freeform
paths are stroked along a simplified outline within about a pixel of the edge.

## Keys and background cutout

**Key** removes a color or brightness range: **Chroma key**, **Color key
(RGB)**, **Hue key (HSV)**, or **Luma key**. **Pick from preview** samples the
key color with an eyedropper (Escape or the same button cancels). Adjust
**Similarity**, **Edge blend** (or **Softness** for luma), **Luma low** and
**Luma high**, and **Green spill** or **Blue spill** removal. A hue key matches
hue only, so it never removes grey, white, or black, and accepts a hue range of
up to ±60°. The preview keys live with WebGL2 and matches the export; without
WebGL2 the preview shows the clip unkeyed, and the export still keys it. A key
type your FFmpeg build lacks is listed as unavailable.

**Background cutout** removes a static background from a locked-off shot
without a green screen. Choose a clean-plate frame (**Use playhead frame**) and
a scene threshold, then **Swap clip to cutout**. Video Studio writes a new
ProRes 4444 video with transparency and swaps the clip to it; the original
media is never changed. It needs FFmpeg's `backgroundkey` filter and the
`prores_ks` encoder. The longest supported clip depends on resolution and frame
rate: up to ten minutes, about two minutes at 1080p30, and about 28 seconds at
4K30. Longer clips are refused up front with the limit. This is a static
background difference key, not AI person segmentation.

## Color

The basic grade sets exposure, brightness, contrast, saturation, temperature,
tint, and vibrance. **Advanced color** adds:

- **Tone:** whites, highlights, shadows, and blacks.
- **Color wheels:** lift, gamma, and gain wheels with a master value each.
  Drag a wheel or use the arrow keys; Delete or a double-click resets it.
- **Curves:** master and RGB curves, plus hue versus saturation. Click to add a
  point, drag or use the arrows to move it, and press Delete to remove it.
- **HSL:** eight hue bands with their own hue, saturation, and lightness.

The basic grade applies before the advanced grade, in the preview and the
export alike.

### LUTs and looks

Import a 3D `.cube` LUT (sizes 2 to 65, up to 2 MB) and apply it to a clip with
an intensity. LUTs need FFmpeg's `lut3d` filter. A LUT that a current project
uses cannot be deleted. A LUT used only by undo history leaves the library but
is kept until nothing refers to it, so undo can bring it back.

The **Looks** tab offers 12 looks: Warm film, Teal-orange, Bleach bypass, Noir,
Faded, Vivid, Cool night, Golden hour, Clean, Matte, Cross-process, and Mono
contrast. Each thumbnail shows the look on the playhead frame of the saved
project. Apply a look with an intensity to up to eight selected visual clips;
a clip keeps its own LUT, and a manual grade change clears the look's name.

To check the result, **Inspect color** on a library item shows a luma
histogram, an RGB parade, and a vectorscope for a source frame. Agents can read
the same measurements for the composited timeline frame.

## Effects

The effect stack holds up to 16 effects per clip, each with an on/off switch,
an amount, and an order. The library has 24 effects in five groups:

| Group | Effects |
| --- | --- |
| Basic | Blur, Sharpen, Grayscale, Sepia, Vignette, Invert |
| Clean up | Denoise, Clarity, Dehaze, Skin smooth, Relight, Letterbox |
| Light | Glow, Light leak, Film grain, Hue cycle |
| Stylize | RGB split, Glitch, Mosaic, Mirror, Edge detect, Shake |
| Motion | Motion blur, Echo trails |

Film grain, glitch, shake, and light leak take an optional seed for a
repeatable pattern. Effect amounts can be keyframed. Effects apply after the
crop, so letterbox bars and mirror lines sit relative to the cropped frame.
Motion blur and echo trails appear only in exports, and the GPU-drawn effects
need WebGL2 in the preview. Skin smooth and Relight are filter-based retouching,
not face detection.

**Relight** adds a soft light to the clip. Pick the light in **Relight light**:
facial lights (**key light** from the upper left, **front fill**), an ambient
**overhead** light, a **rim** light from the left or right edge, or a creative
colored light (**warm sunset**, **cool moonlight**, **neon magenta**). Each
preset brings its own position; **Light position X** and **Y** move the light
across the frame. The amount sets how strong the light is.

## Adjustment layers

Open **Adjustments** in the Vector graphics area to add an adjustment layer: a
grade, effects, and masks applied for a time range to every clip on its track
and the tracks below. Adjustment layers take a blend mode and a keyframable
opacity, and they appear in their own timeline lane where you can move and
trim them. An adjustment layer can also apply a LUT (**LUT** in the layer
editor) with an intensity, to everything below it for its time range; a LUT an
adjustment layer uses cannot be deleted. The preview approximates advanced
grades, LUTs, blend modes, and some effects during playback; when playback is
paused with no unsaved edits, it shows the exact exported frame.

## Canvas and clip backgrounds

The project canvas, set in the Vector graphics area, can be the style color, a
solid color, a gradient (0° points up, 90° right), a blur fill, or an image or
video scaled to cover the frame (a video canvas loops and follows the playhead).
The blur fill (amount up to 100% and a dim control) fills the space around video
and image clips on the bottom track with a blurred copy of the clip; it fades,
crossfades, and wipes with its clip. It is not drawn around keyed or masked
clips or around waveforms.

A clip can also have its own color, gradient, blur, or image fill under **Clip
background**, shown only while that clip plays and through its key or mask
holes; **Project** clears it. An image fill is a library image, or the first
frame of a library video, scaled to cover the frame behind the clip. Audio
clips have no clip background. Deleting a library image or video used as a
canvas resets that background.

## Layouts

Open **Layouts** to arrange selected visual clips in a composition: single
frame, side by side, stacked, 2 × 2, 3 × 3, or 4 × 4 grid, picture in picture
(including one preset per corner, a 25% inset with a 4% margin), **Main and
sidebar** (70/30), **Three up**, and **Three stacked**. Selection order sets the
slot order, and an inset clip must be on a higher track than the main clip.
**Cell framing** chooses **Keep** (leave each clip's framing), **Fit**
(letterbox in the cell), or **Fill** (crop to cover the cell, replacing the
clip's crop). Applying a layout replaces the selected clips' position and scale
keyframes; unlock any locked tracks first.

**Auto reframe**, also in Layouts, converts the whole project to another
aspect; see [assisted editing](/docs/apps/video-studio-assisted-editing#auto-reframe).

## Transitions and clip animation

Drag a transition from the gallery onto a cut between two clips on the same
track (it lands on the cut within a few pixels of the drop, or explains why
not), or choose it under **Transition in** in the incoming clip's inspector.
There are 24 transitions in four groups:

- **Dissolves:** dissolve, blur dissolve, fade through black, and fade through
  white.
- **Wipes:** from each side, two diagonals, and horizontal and vertical barn
  doors.
- **Shapes:** circle open, circle close, box open, iris (diamond), and clock
  wipe.
- **Motion:** four slides, push left, push right, and zoom in.

Durations run from 0.04 to 5 seconds, and wipes and shapes take a softness
from 0 to 1. Adding a transition extends the incoming clip under the previous
one so the program keeps its length (or uses the outgoing clip's tail when the
incoming clip has no spare media); removing it keeps the overlap. Linked audio
stays at the original cut. A push also slides the previous clip out. Every
changed clip, including linked visual clips, must be on an unlocked track.

The inspector also sets an **Entry transition** (crossfade, zoom, or a slide in
from any side) and an **Exit transition** (fade, **Shrink (zoom in)**, **Shrink
and fade (zoom out)**, or a slide out to any side; 0.5 seconds by default,
never longer than the clip). **Animation** adds an in and an out move (fade,
pop, bounce, spin, zoom, four slides, or blur) and a loop (pulse, wobble,
float, spin, or shake, with a period of 0.2 to 10 seconds).

## Keyframes

Use **Animation** in the clip inspector to add keyframes at the playhead for
position, scale, Scale X and Scale Y, rotation, opacity, volume, pan, crop
edges, brightness, contrast, saturation, exposure, temperature, blur, effect
amounts, and mask geometry. Each keyframe has one of six curves: linear, hold, ease in,
ease out, ease in and out, or a custom bezier that works like CSS
`cubic-bezier`. Adding a keyframe in the middle of a curve splits it without
changing playback.

With the keyframe list focused, Cmd/Ctrl+C and Cmd/Ctrl+V copy and paste the
selected keyframes. Paste keeps their relative timing, replaces keyframes of the
same property at the same time, clamps values, and skips (and counts) keyframes
past the target clip's end or for effects and masks the target lacks.

Choose **Show keyframe lanes** on a selected clip to open one lane per animated
property under it, in **Lanes** or **Graph** view (your choice is remembered).
Drag diamonds in time, or in value in Graph view; they snap to the playhead,
clip edges, markers, and the clip's other keyframes, and cannot pass their
neighbours. **Curve** opens a graph editor with two draggable handles, seven
presets (smooth, ease in, ease out, ease in-out, pop overshoot, linear, hold),
and curves you save by name. A pop overshoot can briefly pass a property's
range between keyframes. Clips on locked tracks show their lanes read-only.

A few animated properties are approximated: temperature changes in steps of
about two frames, an animated crop stops at 90% and leaves the cropped edges
transparent, and with many bezier curves the export approximates between
keyframes while staying exact at each keyframe.
