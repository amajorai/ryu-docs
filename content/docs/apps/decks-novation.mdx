This page lists the decks in this family that Decks supports and how each one maps to Ryu keys, dials, and lights. To give keys labels, colours, and actions, start with [Decks](/docs/apps/decks). Before Ryu opens a deck, [close other deck software](/docs/apps/decks#close-other-deck-software), and on Linux add the [udev rules](/docs/apps/decks-linux).

## Device notes

### Novation

On a Novation Launchpad Mini MK3, Launchpad X or Launchpad Pro MK3, keys 1 to 64
are the pads, row by row from the top left, followed by the buttons: on the Mini
and X the top row, then the right-hand column from the top; on the Pro the top
row (Shift first), the left-hand column, the right-hand column, then the two
rows under the pads. Every pad and button glows in its key's colour, the logo is
the deck light, and brightness dims the colours Ryu sends without changing the
Launchpad's own brightness setting. A pad presses on any hit, however soft. Ryu
drives the Launchpad in Programmer mode through its MIDI port, so close Ableton
Live, Novation Components and other music apps using that port first; Ryu picks
the Launchpad up once they let go.

The Pro's Setup button sends nothing in
Programmer mode, so it is not a key. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off and the
Launchpad returns to its usual mode. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA.
The Decks app itself needs the `libasound2` (or `alsa-lib`) package to start
on Linux, for every deck, not just the MIDI ones; most desktops already have
it.

On a Novation Launchpad MK2, keys 1 to 64 are the pads, row by row from the
top left, keys 65 to 72 the round top row from the left, and keys 73 to 80 the
right-hand column from the top. Every pad and button glows in its key's colour,
and brightness dims the colours Ryu sends; the MK2 has no logo light. Ryu
switches it to its Session layout, so close Ableton Live and other music apps
using the Launchpad first: on Windows only one app can hold it, and elsewhere
one sharing it draws over Ryu's lights. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off. On
Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the other Launchpads.

On a Novation Launchpad Pro (the 2015 model, before the MK3), keys 1 to 64 are
the pads, row by row from the top left, keys 65 to 72 the top row from the
left, keys 73 to 80 the left-hand column and keys 81 to 88 the right-hand
column from the top, and keys 89 to 96 the bottom row from the left. Every pad
and button glows in its key's colour, the logo is the deck light, and
brightness dims the colours Ryu sends. A pad presses on any hit, however soft;
the Setup button is the Launchpad's own, so it is not a key. Ryu switches it to
its Programmer layout through its Standalone MIDI port, so close Ableton Live
and other music apps using the Launchpad first. When Ryu lets go, the lights
go off and the Launchpad returns to its Session layout. On Linux, Ryu reaches
it through ALSA, like the other Launchpads. Untested on hardware.

On a pair of Novation Dicers, keys A1 to A5 are buttons 1 to 5 of the Dicer
set to deck A (MIDI channels 11 to 13) and keys B1 to B5 those of the one set to
deck B (channels 14 to 16); which unit is which is set on the hardware. A button
is the same key in every mode and with or without shift. Each button glows
green or orange, dim or bright, in every mode: keys with more green than red
glow green, other colours orange, and blue is dropped. Brightness picks the dim
light or turns it off. Close Mixxx, Traktor and other apps using the Dicers
first. When Ryu lets go, it resets both Dicers. On Linux, Ryu reaches them
through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Untested on hardware.

On an original Novation Launchpad, Launchpad S or Launchpad Mini (MK1 or MK2),
keys 1 to 64 are the grid, row by row from the top left, keys 65 to 72 the
right-hand scene buttons from the top, and keys 73 to 80 the top row from the
left. Each light mixes red and green at four levels, so a key shows the nearest
of red, green, amber, orange, yellow or a dimmer one of them; blue is dropped,
and brightness steps the levels down. Close Ableton Live, Novation Automap and
other music apps using the Launchpad first: on Windows only one app can hold
it, and elsewhere one sharing it draws over Ryu's lights. On Windows, the
original Launchpad and the Mini MK1 need Novation's USB driver before they
show up. When Ryu lets go, the Launchpad resets and its lights go off. On
Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the newer Launchpads.

On a Novation Launch Control XL (MK1 or MK2), keys 1 to 8 are the Track Focus
buttons, keys 9 to 16 the Track Control buttons, keys 17 to 20 Up, Down, Left
and Right, and keys 21 to 24 Device, Mute, Solo and Record Arm. The knob rows
are dials 1 to 8, 9 to 16 and 17 to 24, top to bottom, and the faders dials 25
to 32; each knob's light takes a dial colour, and the faders have none. On a
Launch Control, keys 1 to 8 are the pads and keys 9 to 12 the arrows, and the
top and bottom knobs are dials 1 to 8 and 9 to 16, without lights. Moving a
knob or fader turns its dial by how far it moved, from where it was first
touched.

The track buttons and pads mix red and green at four levels each
(blue is dropped); the arrows and side buttons are only on or off, and
brightness steps the levels down. Ryu uses factory template 1 and follows you
to another factory template; on a user template it reads nothing and leaves
the lights alone. Close Ableton Live, Novation Components and other music apps
using it first: on Windows only one app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing
it draws over Ryu's lights. When Ryu lets go, its lights go off. On Linux, Ryu
reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads.

On a Novation Launch Control XL 3 or Launch Control 3, Ryu uses the buttons,
encoders (and the XL 3's faders) and screen in DAW mode through the deck's DAW
port. On the XL 3, keys 1 to 16 are the two button rows, top row first, then
Solo/Arm, Mute/Select, the small logo button (Function), Page Up and Down,
Track Left and Right, Record, Play and Shift; the encoder rows are dials 1 to
8, 9 to 16 and 17 to 24, top to bottom, and the faders dials 25 to 32. On the
Launch Control 3, keys 1 to 8 are the buttons and key 9 is Shift (its Page,
Track, Function and Mode buttons are not keys), and the encoder rows are dials
1 to 8 and 9 to 16. The encoders turn step by step; moving a fader turns its
dial by how far it moved, from where it was first touched.

Each button glows in its key's exact colour and each encoder's light takes its dial colour;
Shift and the XL 3's logo button have no light Ryu sets, and the faders none.
The screen is the info screen, in one colour. Brightness dims the colours Ryu
sends, and 0% blanks the screen. Switching the deck to a custom mode pauses Ryu
until you switch back to a DAW mode. Close Ableton Live, Bitwig, Novation
Components and other music apps using the DAW port first: on Windows only one
app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it draws over Ryu's lights. When
Ryu lets go, the deck leaves DAW mode. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA,
like the Launchpads. Support is untested on hardware.

On a Novation Launchkey MK3 (Mini, 25, 37, 49 or 61), Ryu uses the pads,
buttons, knobs and faders in DAW mode through the keyboard's DAW port; the
piano keys stay free for your music apps. Keys 1 to 16 are the pads, top row
first. On the Mini, keys 17 to 23 are Scene Launch, Stop/Solo/Mute, Track
Left, Track Right, Shift, Play and Record. On the 25 to 61, keys 17 to 33 are
Scene Launch, Stop/Solo/Mute, Up, Down, Track Left, Track Right, Shift, Play,
Stop, Record, Loop, Capture MIDI, Quantise, Click, Undo, Device Select and
Device Lock, and on the 49 and 61 keys 34 to 42 are the eight fader buttons and
Arm/Select. The knobs are dials 1 to 8; on the 49 and 61 the faders are dials 9
to 16 and the master fader dial 17.

Moving one turns its dial by how far it
moved, from where it was first touched. The pads and the scene, arrow and
fader buttons show the nearest of the Launchkey's 128 colours; the other lit
buttons are off, half or full, and Shift and Device Select have no light.
Brightness dims the colours Ryu sends. Ryu keeps the pads in their session
layout and the knobs and faders in volume mode, and switches them back if you
change them on the keyboard. Close Ableton Live, Bitwig, Novation Components
and other music apps using the DAW port first: on Windows only one app can
hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it repaints the pads over Ryu's. On Windows
every full-size Launchkey shows as the 25, so a 49's or 61's faders and fader
buttons are not read there. When Ryu lets go, the Launchkey leaves DAW mode.
On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads.

On a first-generation Novation Launchkey Mini, Ryu uses the pads, buttons and
knobs in InControl mode through its InControl port (the second one); the piano
keys stay free for your music apps. Keys 1 to 16 are the pads, top row first,
keys 17 and 18 the round Scene Launch and Stop All Clips buttons, and keys 19
to 22 the Track Left and Right and Scene Up and Down arrows, which have no
light. The knobs are dials 1 to 8; moving one turns its dial by how far it
moved, from where it was first touched. The pads and round buttons mix red and
green at four levels each, so they show red, green, amber or yellow; blue is
dropped, and brightness steps the levels down. If the Launchkey leaves
InControl mode, Ryu switches it back and repaints.

Close Ableton Live, Bitwig and other music apps using the InControl port first: on Windows only one app
can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it repaints the pads over Ryu's. When
Ryu lets go, the lights go off and the Launchkey leaves InControl mode. On
Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Untested on hardware.

On a Novation Launchkey MK2 (25, 49 or 61), Ryu uses the pads, buttons and
knobs (and the 49's and 61's faders) in InControl mode through the keyboard's
InControl port; the piano keys stay free for your music apps. Keys 1 to 16 are
the pads, top row first, keys 17 and 18 the round Scene Launch and Stop All
Clips buttons, and keys 19 to 26 Track Left and Right, Scene Up and Down, Stop,
Play, Loop and Record; on the 49 and 61, keys 27 to 34 are the fader buttons
and key 35 the master button. The knobs are dials 1 to 8; on the 49 and 61 the
faders are dials 9 to 16 and the master fader dial 17. Moving one turns its
dial by how far it moved, from where it was first touched.

The pads and round buttons show the nearest of the Launchkey's 128 colours (the round buttons'
colour may be approximate), the fader buttons are on or off, and the track,
scene and transport buttons stay unlit. Brightness dims the colours Ryu sends.
If you switch a section out of InControl with its InControl button, Ryu
switches it back and repaints. The port name rarely says the size, so a
Launchkey MK2 usually shows as the 25, and a 49's or 61's faders and fader
buttons are then not read. A first-generation Launchkey has a port of the same
name and shows as an MK2 too, but Ryu cannot drive it. Close Ableton Live,
Bitwig and other music apps using the InControl port first: on Windows only
one app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it repaints the pads over
Ryu's.

When Ryu lets go, the Launchkey leaves InControl mode. On Linux, Ryu
reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Untested on hardware.

On a Novation Launchkey MK4 (Mini 25 or 37, 25, 37, 49 or 61), Ryu uses the
pads, buttons and knobs (and the 49's and 61's faders) in DAW mode through the
keyboard's DAW port; the piano keys stay free for your music apps. Keys 1 to
16 are the pads, top row first, then Pad Up and Down, Scene Launch, Function,
Encoder Up and Down, Shift, then Play and Record on the Minis, or Track Left
and Right, Capture MIDI, Undo, Quantise, Metronome, Stop, Loop, Play and
Record on the others; the 49 and 61 add Fader Buttons 1 to 8 and Arm/Select.
The pads glow in their key's exact colour; Scene Launch, Function, the fader
buttons and Arm/Select show the nearest of the Launchkey's 128 colours; the
other buttons have one colour whose brightness follows the key's, and Shift
has no light.

Brightness dims the colours Ryu sends. The knobs are dials 1 to
8 and turn step by step; the faders are dials 9 to 16 and the master fader
dial 17, and moving one turns its dial by how far it moved, from where it was
first touched. Ryu keeps the pads in their DAW layout and the faders in volume
mode, switching back if you change them on the keyboard, and takes the knobs
back from a custom mode. The screen keeps showing the keyboard's own
parameters. Close Ableton Live, Bitwig, Novation Components and other music
apps using the DAW port first: on Windows only one app can hold it, and
elsewhere one sharing it repaints the pads over Ryu's. When Ryu lets go, the
Launchkey leaves DAW mode. The Launchkey 88 MK4 is not supported. On Linux,
Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Support is untested on hardware.

On a Novation SL MkIII (49 or 61; the two show as one model), Ryu uses the
pads, buttons, knobs and faders through the keyboard's InControl port; the
piano keys stay free for your music apps. Press the InControl button on the
keyboard to use them with Ryu: until then it sends Ryu nothing and may not
show its lights. Keys 1 to 16 are the pads, top row first, and keys 17 to 61
the buttons: Scene Launch top and bottom, Soft buttons 1 to 24, Screen Up and
Down, Pads Up and Down, the right soft buttons' Up and Down, Grid, Options,
Shift, Duplicate, Clear, Track Left and Right, then Rewind, Fast Forward,
Stop, Play, Loop and Record. The grid is folded to fit, so the soft buttons
right of the screens appear above the ones under them.

Every key but Shift glows in its colour, and brightness dims the colours Ryu sends. Soft buttons 1
to 8 also show the first nine characters of their label on the screen above
them. The knobs are dials 1 to 8, each lit as the knob colour on its screen,
and the faders dials 9 to 16, lit by their LEDs; moving a fader turns its dial
by how far it moved, from where it was first touched. Close Ableton Live,
Bitwig, Novation Components and other music apps using the InControl port
first: on Windows only one app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it draws
over Ryu's lights. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off and the screens are
cleared. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Support is untested on hardware.
