This page lists the decks in this family that Decks supports and how each one maps to Ryu keys, dials, and lights. To give keys labels, colours, and actions, start with [Decks](/docs/apps/decks). Before Ryu opens a deck, [close other deck software](/docs/apps/decks#close-other-deck-software), and on Linux add the [udev rules](/docs/apps/decks-linux).

## Device notes

### 3D mice, game controllers and other inputs

A 3Dconnexion 3D mouse's buttons are keys, named as printed (Menu, Fit, Esc
and so on). Its puck is six dials: dials 1 to 3 slide it right, toward you and
down, and dials 4 to 6 tilt and twist it. Pushing further turns a dial by more
steps, and letting the puck go turns it back. On the models with an LED, any
colour under **Deck lights** turns it on and Off turns it off. The SpacePilot
PRO's and SpaceMouse Enterprise's screens are not drawn, and the Enterprise
offers 22 of its 31 keys. The CadMouse, Keyboard Pro, Numpad Pro and Universal
Receiver 2 are not supported. Quit 3DxWare (or stop spacenavd on Linux) if Ryu
cannot open the mouse.

On a Yamaha CC121MK2, keys 1 to 12 are the keys behind its screen, in rows of
four. The buttons below are named by their printed numbers (Button 1, Button 2,
Button 15 to Button 29); all but 1 and 2 have a light that shows the nearest of
ten colours (blue, orange, yellow, purple, sky blue, pink, red, green,
yellow-green and white) at full strength, and black turns it off. Dials 1 to 6
are RC1 to RC6, with RC6 the jog wheel, and pushing one presses it. The fader
is dial 7: touching it presses the dial, and moving it turns the dial. Ryu asks
its motor to hold it where you leave it. Brightness dims the screen and the
lights. Quit Yamaha's ControlCenter first, and on Linux add the [Decks Linux udev rules](/docs/apps/decks-linux).

On an Xbox controller, keys 1 to 8 are A, B, X, Y, LB, RB, LT and RT; keys 9
to 16 are the d-pad (Up, Down, Left, Right), View, Menu, Xbox and Share; keys 17
to 24 are each stick's four directions (LS up to RS right). A trigger or stick
direction counts as pressed past half way, and a little play around the centre
is ignored. Dials 1 and 2 are the left stick across and up, 3 and 4 the right
stick: holding a stick off centre keeps turning its dial, faster the further you
push, and clicking a stick presses dial 1 or 3. Over a USB cable it works on a
Mac; on Linux, Ryu sees the controller over Bluetooth only, and on Windows over
USB it is not recognised yet. Share works over Bluetooth only, and the Elite's
back paddles, rumble and the Xbox button's light are not used. On a Mac, Ryu
asks to hold the controller to itself, so games stop reacting to it while Ryu
has it.

On a DualShock 4, DualSense or DualSense Edge, keys 1 to 8 are Cross, Circle,
Square, Triangle, L1, R1, L2 and R2; keys 9 to 16 are the d-pad (Up, Down,
Left, Right), Create (Share on the DualShock 4), Options, PS and the touchpad
click; keys 17 to 24 are each stick's four directions. The DualSense adds Mute
as key 25, and the Edge adds its two Fn buttons and two back paddles as keys 26
to 29. Triggers, sticks and dials work as on an Xbox controller. On the
PlayStation Access Controller, keys 1 to 8 are its eight buttons, then Center,
PS and Profile; Ryu reads the buttons as pressed, whatever the active profile
maps them to. Its stick is dials 1 (across) and 2 (up), and clicking it presses
dial 1. Pressing Profile still switches the controller's profile.

The deck light colours the light bar (the Access controller's light), and brightness
dims it; it goes dark when Ryu lets go. Connect the controller with a USB cable:
Ryu does not use it over Bluetooth. Rumble is not used. Setting a deck light
turns the DualSense's player lights and mute light off (so the mute light no
longer shows whether the microphone is muted), and the Access controller's
player indicator off. On a Mac, Ryu asks to hold the controller to itself, so
games stop reacting to it while Ryu has it; on Linux, games still see it.
Steam Input may also set the light bar.

On Sony Buzz! quiz buzzers, each handset is one row of five keys, from the top
of the handset down: the red Buzz! dome, then blue, orange, green and yellow, so
keys 1, 6, 11 and 16 are the four domes. A dome lights when its key has a
colour, and goes dark when the key has none or a very dark one; it is only on
or off, and the coloured buttons have no light. A wireless handset that is
switched off simply never presses anything, so all four rows always show. The
domes go dark when Ryu lets go.

On a GameSir Cyclone 2, keys 1 to 8 are A, B, X, Y, LB, RB, LT and RT; keys
9 to 16 are the d-pad (Up, Down, Left, Right), View, Menu, Home and Capture;
keys 17 to 19 are the back buttons L4, R4 and M; keys 20 to 27 are each
stick's four directions. Sticks and dials work as on an Xbox controller, and
the triggers count as pressed when the controller says so. Ryu reads the
buttons as pressed, whatever GameSir Connect remaps them to. The deck light
replaces the light animation the controller is playing with one colour, and
brightness dims it. The controller saves that in its own light settings, so
Ryu first keeps a copy of the animation and puts it back when you clear the
deck light or Ryu lets go of the controller.

That copy is kept only while Ryu
has the controller open: if the controller is unplugged, its dongle loses it,
or Ryu stops before it can put the animation back, the controller keeps the
single colour, and the next time Ryu opens it that colour is what it keeps and
restores. Only GameSir Connect can bring the original animation back then. If
you switch animations on the
controller (M and the left stick), Ryu puts the old one back and colours the
new one. With no deck light set, Ryu leaves the lights alone. The column of
LEDs between the sticks keeps showing the game profile number. Connect the
controller with a cable or its 2.4 GHz dongle: Ryu does not use it over
Bluetooth. Rumble is not used, and on Linux games still see the controller.

On a SteelSeries Apex keyboard with an OLED, Ryu draws only the small screen:
it is the deck's info screen, with one key's label and action. Bright parts of
the face light up and dark parts stay off. The keyboard types as usual, Ryu
reads none of its keys and leaves the key lights alone, and there is no
brightness setting. Run the screen's action from Decks with Test. Quit
SteelSeries GG first, or it may draw over the screen. The Apex Pro TKL Wireless
Gen 3 is a different deck by cable and through its 2.4 GHz dongle, each with
its own layout. The screen goes dark when Ryu lets go.

On a Griffin PowerMate, the knob is dial 1: pushing it presses the dial and
turning it turns the dial, about 94 steps a full turn. The deck light sets how
bright the blue base light glows (it is always blue, so only how bright the
colour is matters), and brightness dims it. The light stays steady while Ryu has
the knob, keeps its slow pulse while the computer sleeps, and goes dark when Ryu
lets go. Only the USB PowerMate works, not the later Bluetooth one. On Linux add
the [Decks Linux udev rules](/docs/apps/decks-linux).

### DJI remotes

On a DJI RC-N1 drone remote, keys 1 to 4 are the left stick's up, down, left
and right, keys 5 to 8 the right stick's, and keys 9 and 10 the gimbal wheel up
and down. A direction counts as pressed past half way, and a little play around
the centre is ignored. Dials 1 and 2 are the left stick across and up, 3 and 4
the right stick, and 5 the wheel: holding one off centre keeps turning its dial,
faster the further you push. The remote's buttons (return to home, the shutter,
Fn, C1, C2 and the flight-mode switch) are not read, and so far only the left
stick has been checked on a real remote. Switch the remote on and connect its
bottom USB-C port with a data cable, then quit DJI Assistant and any simulator
using it. On Linux add the [Decks Linux udev rules](/docs/apps/decks-linux).

### monome

On a monome grid, the keys are numbered row by row from the top left. Each key's
light has one colour: on grids from 2011 it glows at one of sixteen levels,
brighter for a brighter key colour, and on the older 64, 128 and 40h it is on
for any colour but black or near-black. On a monome arc, each encoder is a dial,
turning 64 steps a turn on 2012 and later arcs (16 on the 2011 arc), and only the
2011 arc's encoders push. Each ring lights whole under **Dial lights**, brighter
for a brighter colour. Brightness sets the grid's or rings' intensity.

Ryu finds a monome by its USB serial number, the way monome's own serialosc does, so quit
serialosc (and anything using the grid or arc through it) first; Ryu picks the
device up once serialosc lets go. 16 x 16 grids (the 256, grid zero and the older
256s and kits) have more keys than Ryu can number, so they are not supported. On
Linux add the [Decks Linux udev rules](/docs/apps/decks-linux).

### PrehKeyTec POS keyboards

Ryu cannot program a PrehKeyTec keyboard, so set it up first in PrehKeyTec's
own tool (WinProgrammer or MapMyKey): give every key you want in Ryu its own
POSKey macro, POSKey001 to POSKey128, and turn on the OPOS/JavaPOS POSKey HID
output. Keys left as ordinary keys never reach Ryu. Ryu listens only to that
POSKey channel and leaves the keyboard's normal typing alone. Each key is named
by its POSKey number, and POSKey 1 is the bottom-left key, counting left to
right and then upwards. When the keyboard is plugged in, Ryu asks it which
model it is, so the right layout appears; one that does not answer is shown as
an MCI 84 or MCI 128, by its USB id.

The MCI 30, 60, 84 and 128 have been tried
on hardware by others; the MCI 96 has only answered which model it is, and the
MCI 128 Alpha, 3000 and 3100 POS sections and the MSI 60 follow their published
layouts only. There are no screens, lights or brightness to set.

### QMK keyboards and macropads on Vial

Any keyboard or macropad running Vial firmware shows up in Decks under its own
name, with its keys laid out as in its Vial layout and named by their
switch-matrix position (Row 1 col 3 and so on). Keyboards on stock QMK or VIA
firmware without Vial are not supported, and a keyboard whose switch matrix is
too large for Vial to report is listed with that reason instead of its keys.
Ryu learns a keyboard's layout from the keyboard, so after Ryu restarts it is
listed again once it is plugged in; its saved keys are kept.

Vial keeps a keyboard's keys locked from other programs until you unlock it on
the keyboard itself. Decks says when it is locked and which keys unlock it.
Press **Unlock**, then hold those keys together for about ten seconds until the
bar fills; letting go starts the count again. Once started, an unlock ends only
when the keys have been held long enough or the keyboard is unplugged, and while
it runs the keyboard ignores its lights. It stays unlocked until you unplug the
keyboard or lock it from the Vial app.

Each key still types what its keymap says when you press it: Ryu cannot stop
that. Map the keys you use in Decks to nothing (`KC_NO`) or to F13 to F24 in the
Vial app. Ryu never changes the keymap. Knobs and small screens on the keyboard
are not reachable this way.

On a keyboard built with VialRGB, the colour of a key lights the LED under it,
and **Deck lights** colours the rest (underglow and indicators). While any light
is set, Ryu drives them and **Brightness** dims them; when none is set, or Ryu
lets go of the keyboard, its own lighting effect comes back. Nothing is saved to
the keyboard. Its lighting timeout and sleep still turn the lights off.

### Wooting keyboards

On a Wooting keyboard every key is a deck key: Ryu reads the keyboard's analog
channel alongside your computer, and lights each key in its colour. Because it
only reads alongside, a key you bind in Decks still types as usual, so bind keys
you rarely type with. A key presses at the actuation point you set in Wootility;
on firmware that only sends the older analog report (the One and Two on their
first firmware among them), Ryu presses it at 40% of the travel and releases it
under 25%. **Brightness** dims the lights, and when Ryu lets go the keyboard
returns to its own lighting. Quit Wootility and other RGB tools first, or they
and Ryu take turns at the lights.

Decks shows the keyboard's whole switch matrix, six rows by 21, 17 or 14
columns, so a few keys are cells with no switch that never press (one of the six
rows is empty on a 60HE). On the 21-column Two family each key is named after
its legend. Ryu has been checked only against Wooting's published SDKs, not on
hardware yet.

### Dynamic Lighting devices (HID LampArray)

Keyboards, mice, light strips and DIY boards that support Windows Dynamic
Lighting describe their lights in a standard way, so Decks can light them
whoever made them. Each one shows up under its own name with one key per lamp,
placed where the device says the lamp sits and named after the key it lights
(or Lamp 1, Lamp 2 and so on). Devices with more than 255 lamps have the rest
as one deck light. Ryu learns the lamps from the device, so after Ryu restarts
it is listed again once it is plugged in; its saved keys are kept.

These keys are lights only. Pressing them does nothing in Ryu, and a keyboard's
keys keep typing as usual, so run a key's action with **Test** or from a
workflow. Each lamp glows in its key's colour, and lamps with a fixed colour
only get brighter or dimmer. **Brightness** dims them all. On Windows, turn off
**Use Dynamic Lighting on my devices** in Settings for the device first;
otherwise Windows and Ryu take turns at its lights. When Ryu lets go, the
device's own lighting comes back. Ryu has been checked only against the
standard and published examples, not on hardware yet.

### MacBook Pro Touch Bar

On a MacBook Pro with a Touch Bar, Decks lists the Touch Bar as a deck with eight
keys. Once you save a key, a **Ryu** button appears in the Touch Bar's Control
Strip (the part on the right). Tap it to show your keys across the Touch Bar;
the close button on the left brings the usual Touch Bar back. A tap on a key
works like a press on any other deck.

An app that replaces the whole Touch Bar, such as MTMR or Pock, can cover
Ryu's keys while it is showing. The Touch Bar is not supported on Linux.
