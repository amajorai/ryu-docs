This guide covers the parts of Video Studio around the timeline: projects,
panels, the preview, saving, and the media library. Start with the
[Video Studio overview](/docs/apps/video-studio); the edits themselves are in
[timeline editing](/docs/apps/video-studio-editing), and every key is listed in
[keyboard shortcuts](/docs/apps/video-studio-shortcuts).

## Projects

Open **Projects** in the Media area for the project dashboard. Search by title,
sort by last modified, date created, or title, and switch between a grid and a
list. Each project shows a poster frame taken from its first visible content
(hidden clips and tracks are skipped). You can rename a project, read its
details, or select several and delete them after a confirmation.

Deleting a project removes it with its saved history, export records, exported
files, and posters. Media stays in the library. When the project was used as a
nested sequence elsewhere, that sequence clip becomes an ordinary video clip.
A delete is refused while the project is exporting or while another project
still nests it.

The strip under the toolbar shows up to eight recently opened projects for this
viewer. Choose one to switch to it; deleted projects drop out of the strip.

## Arrange the workspace

Drag the dividers between panels to resize them; the sizes are remembered for
this viewer. The layout menu offers **Edit**, **Color**, **Audio**, and
**Review** presets. **Save current layout** stores your arrangement as **My
layout**, and a layout you have changed since choosing a preset shows as
**Custom (unsaved)**. Press the backtick key or use the **Maximize** button in
the workspace bar to maximize the focused panel; **Restore panels** brings the
others back.

The first time you open the editor, a short tour points out the preview,
timeline, inspector, layouts, and shortcuts. Choose **Skip tour** or press
Escape to close it; **Take the tour** opens it again. At phone width the panels stack in
one column and the layout presets are hidden.

### Timeline colors

**Timeline colors** in the workspace bar sets the default fill for each kind
of timeline item: video, audio, and image clips, titles, captions, graphics,
avatars, and adjustment layers. Pick a swatch to change a kind's color, or
**Reset all** to return every kind to its default. A clip with its own color
tag keeps that tag. The colors are remembered for this viewer and do not
change the project or an export.

## Language

Video Studio follows the language you choose for Ryu. In Arabic, Chinese
(Simplified), French, German, Hindi, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese
(Brazil), Russian, and Spanish, the workspace appears in that language: panel
tabs and the overflow button that lists hidden tabs, every feature panel
(Generate, Soundtrack, Multicam, Find footage, Sync, Tempo & Beat, Split
edits, Cleanup, Highlights, Transcript, Titles, Avatar, dubbing, recipes,
production and the rest), the toolbar, the timeline and its clip menu, marker
editing and **Ask agent**, the library, the preview and source tabs, the
inspector sections, export settings, status messages, the tour, and the
keyboard shortcuts dialog and printable reference.

Messages that include a value are translated too, such as "Imported 3 captions.", a marked range, or
an error that names a clip or track; the value itself is shown as it is.
Switching Ryu's language updates the workspace without a reload. Your own
project, clip, and media names stay as they are, as do the names of codecs and
file formats.

Video Studio also includes machine-drafted translations of the whole
workspace for Czech, Danish, Dutch, Finnish, Greek, Hebrew, Hungarian,
Indonesian, Norwegian Bokmål, Polish, Romanian, Swedish, Thai, Traditional
Chinese, Turkish, Ukrainian, and Vietnamese. They cover the same strings and
value-filled messages as the official languages, with each language's own
plural forms, but they were machine-drafted and have not yet been reviewed by
native speakers, so some wording may read unnaturally. They appear only when
Ryu itself runs in that language, which needs a Ryu language pack for it;
without the pack Ryu cannot select the language, and Video Studio stays in
English.

## Save, autosave, and history

Video Studio saves your edits automatically two seconds after the last change
and when you switch away from the tab. The toolbar shows **Saved**,
**Saving…**, **Unsaved**, or **Save failed**, and the browser warns before you
leave with unsaved changes. If the project was saved somewhere else in the
meantime (another editor or an agent), the status reads **Changed elsewhere**,
autosave pauses, and a banner offers to reload the newer version. **Save changes** and Cmd/Ctrl+S save immediately, waiting for an
autosave that is already in progress.

Changes an agent (or another window) saves to the open project appear in the
editor within a few seconds, without a reload. Each one is added to your
history as an **Agent edit** step, so Cmd/Ctrl+Z reverts the agent's change
like any of your own edits, and redo brings it back. If you have unsaved edits
at the time, Video Studio merges the two: clips, tracks, titles, captions,
markers and the other timeline items are matched one by one, and a change to a
different item, or to a different setting of the same item, is combined with
yours. Your edits stay unsaved on top of the newer version and autosave writes
them a moment later. Only when both sides changed the same setting of the same
item, one side deleted an item the other edited, or both moved clips onto each
other, does the **Changed elsewhere** banner appear, listing those items, and
offer to reload the newer version.

Open **History** in the Exports area to see this session's edits, each with a
label and a time. Choose any step to return to it; steps you undo stay
available to redo until you make a new edit. The first entry reads **Opened**,
or **Oldest kept step** once more than 100 edits have been made. The same panel lists saved revisions, including
those from earlier sessions, and restoring one is itself an undoable edit.

## The preview

The preview toolbar changes only how you watch the project; none of it affects
the saved project or an export.

- **Zoom:** **Fit** shows the real fit percentage, or pick a level from 25%
  to 400%. With
  the preview zoomed in, pan by dragging with the middle mouse button or by
  holding Space while you drag.
- **Guides:** action safe (90%), title safe (80%), 9:16, 4:5, 1:1, 1.85, and
  2.39 aspect guides, rule of thirds, a center cross, generic vertical-video
  interface zones, and three generic vertical layouts (caption-heavy, right
  action rail, and bottom sheet) that approximate where short-form apps put
  their buttons and text. **Grid** draws a grid, and **Custom guide** draws
  your own margins and aspect box. While either is on, the menu shows **Custom
  aspect** (W:H, such as 2.35:1), **Side margins**, **Top and bottom
  margins**, **Grid columns**, and **Grid rows**; they are remembered on this
  device. **Hide all guides** clears them. Guides never
  appear in exports, and your last set is remembered.
- **Playback rate:** play the timeline from 0.25× to 10×.
- **Quality:** **Full**, **Half**, or **Quarter** resolution. Lower quality
  makes heavy timelines play more smoothly.
- **Use proxies:** play video from lighter preview copies when they exist (see
  [preview proxies](/docs/apps/video-studio-media-workflows#preview-proxies)).
  Exports always use the original media.
- **Save frame** downloads the frame at the playhead as a PNG; **Copy frame**
  puts it on the clipboard, or explains when the browser cannot copy images.
- **Fullscreen** fills the screen with the preview.

### Preview tabs

The preview has a fixed **Timeline** tab plus one tab for each library item
you open. Double-click a library tile, or choose **Open in viewer**, to open
it in a source tab. A source tab plays the media in real time with its sound:
Space plays or pauses, J and L shuttle backward and forward (press again for
2×, 4×, and 8×), K pauses, and the position slider scrubs. **Mark in (I)** and
**Mark out (O)** set the source range, the same range the library tile shows,
so **Add to timeline**, **Insert at playhead**, and **Drag to timeline** place
only the marked part. Opening a source tab pauses timeline playback.

The arrow buttons before the tabs go back and forward through the tabs you
viewed. Close a tab with its × button or a middle click. Open tabs are
remembered for this viewer and project, and tabs for deleted media close.

### Edit on the canvas

Select a clip, title, or graphic and drag it in the preview to move it. Corner
handles scale it evenly (hold Alt to scale from the center). Clips and graphics
also have edge handles that stretch one axis; on a clip they set **Scale X** or
**Scale Y**. Clips, titles, and graphics have a rotation knob above their box,
and holding Shift snaps rotation to 15° steps. Arrow keys nudge the selection by one pixel,
or ten with Shift.

Items snap to the canvas center and edges and to other items. Hold Cmd/Ctrl to
skip snapping for one drag, or turn snapping off with N. Each drag is one undo
step; dragging a property that already has keyframes adds a keyframe at the
playhead. Clips on locked tracks cannot be changed on the canvas.

Press C with a clip's box focused, or Enter, to open crop mode. It shows the
whole source with the crop window over it. Choose a free crop or a 16:9, 9:16,
1:1, 4:5, or 4:3 aspect (a fixed aspect leaves only the corner handles), then
**Done**; **Reset** removes the crop and Escape leaves crop mode. The kept area
refits the canvas exactly as it will in the export. Crop mode also ends when you
select something else, the playhead leaves the clip, or its track is locked.

Double-click a title, or press Enter on its box, to edit its text in place.
Enter commits, Shift+Enter adds a line, and Escape cancels.

During a slip, roll, or ripple drag, the preview shows two source frames side
by side with their source timecodes: the first and last frames for a slip, and
the outgoing and incoming frames at the edit point for a roll or ripple.

### Number fields

Number fields in the clip inspector, the Titles and Graphics panels (timing,
position, size, fades, stroke width, and shape geometry), the title style
controls (letter spacing, line height, outline, shadow, box, rotation, and
animation durations), effect seeds, and title and graphic keyframe values
accept arithmetic such as `1920/2-10` or `+0.1`, with `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`,
parentheses, and decimals. Enter applies the value, Escape reverts it, and
values are clamped to the field's range. An expression that cannot be
evaluated stays marked with its reason, which screen readers announce, and
reverts when you leave the field. Drag a field's label to scrub it one step per
pixel; hold Shift for finer steps or Alt for larger ones, and press Escape to
cancel the scrub.

ArrowUp and ArrowDown nudge by one step with the same
modifiers. A title style field or effect seed that shows a placeholder is unset:
scrubbing starts from the placeholder's default, and emptying the field
returns it to the default.

## The media library

Sort the library by date, name, type, duration, or frame size, in either
direction, and show it as a list or a grid; both choices are remembered. Select
several items with their checkboxes, a Shift range, or Cmd/Ctrl-click, then
**Select all**, **Clear**, or **Delete selected**. Media that a project still
uses is kept, and the message names those projects.

Move the pointer across a video tile to skim through it. **Preview and mark**
lets you scrub a source and mark a range with I and O or the buttons; that range
is then used by **Add**, **Insert at playhead**, source-analysis ranges, and
dragging. Without a marked range the whole clip is used. For real-time
playback with sound, open the item in a
[preview tab](#preview-tabs); its marks are shared with the tile.

### Bring media in

Drag library media onto a timeline lane to overwrite what is there, or hold
Cmd/Ctrl while dropping to insert it and ripple later content. A ghost shows
where the media will land and shades the clips, including linked audio, that it
will cut. Drops snap to edit points unless you hold Shift. Dropping below the
lanes picks a free track of the matching kind, and locked tracks refuse drops.

Media files dropped anywhere on the window are imported; unsupported files are
reported as skipped. With no text field focused, Cmd/Ctrl+V imports an image or
video from the system clipboard, or pastes copied clips when the clipboard holds
none. Imports run in chunks, and each file can be up to 2 GB.

The "Media imported" notice in the status line goes away on your next timeline
edit, such as adding the new media, or after six seconds.
