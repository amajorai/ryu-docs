This page lists the decks in this family that Decks supports and how each one maps to Ryu keys, dials, and lights. To give keys labels, colours, and actions, start with [Decks](/docs/apps/decks). Before Ryu opens a deck, [close other deck software](/docs/apps/decks#close-other-deck-software), and on Linux add the [udev rules](/docs/apps/decks-linux).

## Device notes

### Akai

On an Akai APC mini MK2, keys 1 to 64 are the pads, row by row from the top
left, keys 65 to 72 the scene buttons down the right (Clip Stop to Stop All
Clips), keys 73 to 80 the track buttons along the bottom (Volume to Right) and
key 81 Shift. The pads glow in their key's colour; the scene buttons (green)
and track buttons (red) are only on or off, and Shift has no light. Brightness
dims the colours Ryu sends. The eight faders are dials 1 to 8 and the master
fader dial 9; moving one turns its dial by how far it moved, from where it
was first touched. Ryu keeps the pads in session mode and switches them back if you
pick note or drum mode with Shift.

Ryu uses the deck's Control port, so close
Ableton Live and other music apps using it first: on Windows only one app can
hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it draws over Ryu's lights. When Ryu lets
go, the lights go off. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the
Launchpads.

On the first Akai APC mini (MK1), the keys are placed as on the MK2: keys 1 to
64 are the pads, row by row from the top left, keys 65 to 72 the scene buttons
down the right, keys 73 to 80 the track buttons along the bottom and key 81
Shift. Each pad glows green, red or yellow, whichever is nearest to its key's
colour (white and greys show yellow, blue and purple green); the scene buttons
and the red track buttons are only on or off, and Shift has no light. The APC
mini has no brightness setting. The eight faders are dials 1 to 8 and the
master fader dial 9; moving one turns its dial by how far it moved, from where
it was first touched. Close Ableton Live and other music apps using the APC
mini first: on Windows only one app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it
draws over Ryu's lights. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off. On Linux, Ryu
reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads.

On an Akai APC40 MKII, keys 1 to 40 are the clip pads, row by row from the top
left, and keys 41 to 45 the scene buttons down their right. Below them, row by
row: the eight clip stop buttons and Stop All Clips (46 to 54), the eight track
select buttons and Master (55 to 63), then mute (64 to 71), solo (72 to 79),
arm (80 to 87) and the A/B crossfade buttons (88 to 95), the eight device
buttons (96 to 103) with Bank (111), Pan, Sends, User, Metronome, Play, Record
and Session (104 to 110), and the bank arrows, Shift, Tap Tempo, Nudge and the
foot pedal (112 to 120). The pads and scene buttons glow in the nearest of the
deck's 128 colours; the other buttons are only on or off, except the crossfade
buttons, which show A, or B for a blue key.

Stop All Clips, the arrows, Shift,
Tap Tempo, Nudge and the pedal have no light, and there is no brightness
setting. Dials 1 to 8 are the track knobs, 9 to 16 the device knobs, 17 Tempo,
18 Cue, 19 to 26 the channel faders, 27 the master fader and 28 the
crossfader; a fader turns its dial by how far it moved, from where it was
first touched. Ryu switches the deck to its Ableton Live mode when it connects.
Close Ableton Live and other music apps using the APC40 MKII first: on Windows
only one app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it draws over Ryu's lights
and changes its mode. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off. On Linux, Ryu
reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads.

On the first Akai APC40 and the APC20, keys 1 to 40 are the clip pads, row by
row from the top left, and keys 41 to 45 the scene launch buttons down their
right; each pad glows green, red or amber, the nearest to its key's colour.
Below them on the APC40, row by row: the eight clip stop buttons and Stop All
Clips (46 to 54), the eight track select buttons and Master (55 to 63), then
activator (64 to 71), solo (72 to 79) and arm (80 to 87), the eight device
buttons (88 to 95), Pan, Send A, B and C, Play, Stop and Record (96 to 102),
and the arrows, Shift, Tap Tempo, Nudge and the two footswitches (103 to 112).

Its dials 1 to 8 are the track knobs, 9 to 16 the device knobs, 17 Cue, 18 to
25 the channel faders, 26 the master fader and 27 the crossfader; the knob
rings stay off. On the APC20: clip stop (46 to 53), track select (54 to 61),
Master (62), activator (63 to 70), solo (71 to 78), arm (79 to 86), Shift (87)
and the foot pedal (88); its dials 1 to 8 are the channel faders, in any
slider mode, 9 the master fader and 10 Cue. The scene launch and clip stop
buttons are green and only on or off, like every other lit button; Stop All
Clips, the arrows, Shift, Tap Tempo, Nudge and the pedals have no light, and
there is no brightness setting. A fader turns its dial by how far it moved,
from where it was first touched.

Ryu switches the deck to its Ableton Live
mode when it connects, so close Ableton Live and other music apps using it
first. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through
ALSA, like the Launchpads. Support is untested on hardware.

On an Akai APC Key 25 or APC Key 25 MK2, keys 1 to 40 are the clip pads, row
by row from the top left, keys 41 to 45 the scene buttons down their right
(Clip Stop, Solo, Rec Arm or Mute, Mute or Rec Arm, Select), keys 46 to 53 the
track buttons below them (Up, Down, Left, Right, Volume, Pan, Send, Device),
then Stop All Clips (54), Play (55), Record (56) and Shift (57), which have no
light. On the first APC Key 25 each pad glows green, red or amber, the nearest
to its key's colour, and there is no brightness setting; on the MK2 the pads
glow in the nearest of the deck's 128 colours, and brightness shows them at
full or half. The scene buttons (green) and track buttons (red) are only on or
off. Dials 1 to 8 are the knobs.

The piano keys are not keys in Ryu: the MK2's
come through a separate port Ryu leaves alone, and the first APC Key 25's on
another MIDI channel, which Ryu ignores. Close Ableton Live and other music
apps using the deck first: on Windows only one app can hold it, and elsewhere
one sharing it draws over Ryu's lights. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off. On
Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads.

On an Akai MIDImix, keys 1 to 8 are the Mute buttons and key 9 Bank Left,
keys 10 to 17 the Mute buttons pressed while SOLO is held (Solo 1 to 8) and
key 18 Bank Right, and keys 19 to 26 the Rec Arm buttons and key 27 SOLO. Each
button's light has one colour and is only on or off: any colour except black
or near-black turns it on. SOLO has no light, and the Solo keys' lights are
meant to show on the Mute buttons while SOLO is held, which is not confirmed
on hardware. There is no brightness setting.

Dials 1 to 8 are the top knobs,
9 to 16 the middle knobs, 17 to 24 the bottom knobs, 25 to 32 the channel
faders and 33 the master fader; moving one turns its dial by how far it moved,
from where it was first touched, and pressing Send All after connecting seeds
every dial without turning any. Close Ableton Live and other music apps using
it first: on Windows only one app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it
draws over Ryu's lights. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off. On Linux, Ryu
reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads.

On an Akai LPD8 (the original; the LPD8 MK2 is not supported), keys 1 to 4 are
PAD5 to PAD8 (the top row) and keys 5 to 8 are PAD1 to PAD4. Keep the deck on
PROG 1 with its factory settings and the pads in PAD mode: in CC or PROG CHNG
mode the pads send nothing Ryu reads, and a reprogrammed PROG 1 may not match.
The PROG and mode buttons are not keys. Each pad's light has one colour and is
only on or off: any colour except black or near-black turns it on. The deck
forgets the lights when you switch program or mode, so Ryu sends them again a
few times a second. There is no brightness setting. Knobs K1 to K8 are dials 1
to 8; moving one turns its dial by how far it moved, from where it was first
touched.

Close Ableton Live and other music apps using it first: on Windows
only one app can hold it, and elsewhere one sharing it draws over Ryu's
lights. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through
ALSA, like the Launchpads.

On an Akai MPK mini IV or MPK mini Plus II, Ryu uses the deck's DAW port, never
its MIDI port, so the keys still play in your music apps. When it connects, the
screen shows "Ryu", the model and "Connected", and the pads stop sending notes
to the MIDI port until Ryu lets go. Keys 1 to 8 are the eight pads in one bank
(top row, then bottom row) and keys 9 to 16 the same pads in the other bank:
the deck's own bank buttons switch between them. Keys 17 to 25 are Shift, which
has no light, -, +, Tap Tempo, Undo, Loop, Play, Record and Overdub. Each pad
shows the nearest colour on the deck's palette; each button's light is only on
or off. The brightness setting picks one of the deck's six levels, and 0% turns
every light off.

The eight knobs are dials 1 to 8, top row first, and the main
encoder dial 9 (pushing it presses that dial), with no light. While the deck is
in its plugin mode, Ryu ignores it; back in DAW mode every light is sent again.
When Ryu lets go, the lights go off and the screen and pads are handed back.
Close Ableton Live, Bitwig Studio and other music apps using the DAW port
first. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Support is
untested on hardware.

On a first-generation Akai MPK mini Plus (not the MPK mini Plus II), Ryu uses
only the five transport buttons: keys 1 to 5 are Rewind, Fast Forward, Stop,
Play and Record (assumed to be their order left to right). Each button's light
is only on or off, and Ryu sends every light again a few times a second. There
is no brightness setting. The pads, knobs and keybed
send whatever the deck's program says, so Ryu leaves them to your other MIDI
software. Close Ableton Live, Bitwig Studio and other MIDI apps using the deck
first: on Windows only one app can hold its port. When Ryu lets go, the lights
go off. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Support is
untested on hardware.

On an Akai Fire, keys 1 to 64 are the pads, row by row from the top left, and
keys 65 to 84 the buttons: Mute/Solo 1 to 4 (left of the pad rows), Pattern
Up and Down, Browser, Grid Left and Right, the knob-mode (Bank) button, Step,
Note, Drum, Perform, Shift, Alt, Pattern/Song, Play, Stop and Rec. The pads
take any colour. Each button only shows its own fixed colours, dim or bright:
Pattern, Browser and Grid red, Mute/Solo green, Alt and Stop orange, Pattern/Song
and Play green or orange, and the others red or orange, the nearest to the
key's colour; Bank has no light. The OLED is the info screen, in one colour:
bright parts of the face light up. Ryu writes it again every couple of seconds
so it does not go to sleep.

The deck has no brightness command, so Ryu scales
the pads, dims the buttons and at 0% blanks the screen. Volume, Pan, Filter
and Resonance are dials 1 to 4 and the Select encoder is dial 5; pushing it
presses dial 5, and touching a knob does nothing. Close FL Studio and other
music apps using it first: on Windows only one app can hold it, and elsewhere
one sharing it draws over Ryu's lights and screen. When Ryu lets go, the
lights and screen go off. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the
Launchpads.

### Ableton Push

On the first Ableton Push, keys 1 to 64 are the pads, row by row from the
top left, and keys 65 to 127 the buttons around them; the User button is
never a key, since with Live running it takes the Push back. The pads and the
lower row of buttons under the display glow in the nearest of the Push's
colours, the upper row and the scene buttons in red, amber, yellow or green,
and the other buttons in one colour, dim or full. There is no brightness
setting. The Tempo and Swing encoders are dials 1 and 2, the eight track
encoders dials 3 to 10, Master dial 11 and the touch strip dial 12; touching
an encoder does nothing. The display shows only "Ryu".

Ryu uses the Push's User port and never changes its mode, so Ableton Live can keep its Live
port; leave the User port off in Live's MIDI settings, since on Windows only
one app can hold it. When Ryu lets go, the lights go off and the display is
cleared. On Linux, Ryu reaches it through ALSA, like the Launchpads. Support is untested on hardware.

On an Ableton Push 2, keys 1 to 64 are the pads, row by row from the top left,
and keys 65 to 128 the buttons around them; the User button is never a key.
The pads and the coloured buttons (the two rows above and below the display,
the scene buttons, Mute, Solo, Stop Clip, Automate, Record and Play) glow in
the nearest of the Push 2's colours; the other buttons are white and only
brighter or dimmer. Brightness sets the Push 2's own light level, but on USB
power alone, without its power supply, the Push 2 keeps its lights dim
whatever the setting. The Tempo and Swing encoders are dials 1 and 2, the
eight track encoders dials 3 to 10, Master dial 11 and the touch strip dial
12; touching an encoder does nothing. The display stays dark.

Ryu uses the Push 2's User port and switches it to User mode, so Ableton Live can keep its
Live port; leave the User port off in Live's MIDI settings, since on Windows
only one app can hold it. Pressing the User button hands the Push 2 back to
Live, and Ryu ignores it until User mode is picked again. When Ryu lets go,
the lights go off and the Push 2 returns to Live mode. On Linux, Ryu reaches
it through ALSA, like the Launchpads.

### M-Audio

On an M-Audio Oxygen Pro 25, 49, 61 or Mini or a Hammer 88 Pro, Ryu uses the
deck's third MIDI port, the DAW port (`Mackie/HUI` on macOS, `MIDIIN3` and
`MIDIOUT3` on Windows), never the keybed's port, so your music software keeps
the keys. Ryu puts the deck in DAW mode. The pads (16, or 8 on the Mini) come
first, top row first, and light in the nearest of the deck's 64 palette
colours; the track buttons follow, with lights that are only on or off. They
latch on the deck, so each press reaches Ryu as one tap. The transport, Back,
bank, Preset, DAW, Scene (not on the Mini), Shift and mode buttons fill the
remaining rows in a logical order and have no light Ryu sets.

The knobs, the faders (Oxygen Pro 49 and 61 and Hammer 88 Pro), the master fader (not on the
Mini) and the main encoder are dials; a knob's or fader's first move only
tells Ryu where it is, and the encoder also presses. The deck has no
brightness command, so Ryu dims the pad colours. When Ryu lets go, the lights
go off and the deck takes its LEDs back. Close other apps using the DAW port
first. Support is untested on hardware.
