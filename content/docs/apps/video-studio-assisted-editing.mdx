These tools analyze your media and propose or make larger edits. Start with the
[Video Studio overview](/docs/apps/video-studio) and
[timeline editing](/docs/apps/video-studio-editing); the agent routes behind
each tool are listed in [agent media and export](/docs/apps/video-studio-agents-media#assisted-edits-for-agents).

Analysis results (beats, voice activity, speakers, shots, highlights, drift,
and subject tracking) are measurements and heuristics. Treat them as cues to
review, not guarantees. Every edit these tools make is one undoable step.

Some assisted tools can also use a cloud model you choose: shot descriptions for
[finding footage](/docs/apps/video-studio-media-workflows#find-footage-by-look),
and video-to-video edits, subject mattes, motion gap fills, generated music,
and photoreal lip-sync in [generation](/docs/apps/video-studio-generation). The
measured and local versions stay the defaults, and every cloud request names
the model it used.

## Automatic J/L cuts

Open **J/L cuts** in the side panel, or press Shift+Alt+J, and choose
**Analyze cuts**. For each linked cut, Video Studio proposes a J-cut, an L-cut,
or no change:

- **Measured word timings come first.** When both sides have a saved transcript
  with word timings, a sentence that the picture cut clips at the end of the
  outgoing shot suggests an L-cut. Speech that starts in the incoming shot's
  unused media just before its picture suggests a J-cut. The audio being
  shortened must stay quiet over the proposed offset; if both sides are
  speaking, the cut stays straight.
- **The waveform is the fallback.** Without transcripts, completed source
  analysis supplies a loudness envelope, at lower confidence. When the measured
  waveform is too coarse for the offset range, no edit is proposed.
- **Nothing is fabricated.** Offsets are clamped to available source media, and
  cuts that continue the same take, already have a split edit, or lack spare
  media are skipped with a reason.

Each row shows the cut time, J or L, the offset, the reason, and a confidence
score; rows at 0.6 or above are preselected. Edit an offset, use **Seek** to
review the cut, and choose **Apply N split edits**. **Detach camera audio when
analyzing** first detaches embedded audio from unlinked video clips as a
separate undoable edit, because proposals only cover cuts between linked audio
and video. **Replace existing split edits** re-plans cuts that already have one.

## Freeze, reverse, and speed curves

**Freeze frame** (video clips): **Insert at playhead** holds the source frame
under the playhead for 0.1 to 30 seconds and shifts later layers right; **Hold
the last frame** appends the final frame. The still comes from the source media,
so overlays are not baked into it. It keeps the clip's crop, grade, effects,
and framing, plays silently, and is not available on multicam clips.

**Reverse** plays a video or audio clip backwards. Video Studio renders a
reversed copy of the source on your node once, in short chunks, and reuses it.
Sources up to 10 minutes long can be reversed, and linked partners reverse with
the clip. A clip with a J-cut or L-cut against a neighbour must be returned to a
straight cut first. **Restore forward** returns to the original.

**Speed curves** in **Speed & beat** change a clip's speed along a curve
between 0.1× and 10× (**Montage**, **Hero**, **Bullet time**, **Flash in**,
**Flash out**, **Jump cut**, or **Custom**). **Jump cut** plays fast, then slow,
then fast with hard changes. **Custom** opens a curve editor that starts from
the curve on screen: drag a point (left to right is the position in the clip,
bottom to top is the speed on a 0.1× to 10× scale), or select it and type its
position and speed. **Add point** and **Remove point** keep 2 to 12 points, and
the first and last points stay at the start and end of the clip.

Choose how the curve is applied:

- **Smooth** (the default) keeps one clip and remaps its time continuously:
  every point plays at its own speed and the speed glides between points in the
  preview and the export, with no steps. The clip becomes as long as the curve
  needs, and the later clips move as **Later clips** says. Its audio follows
  the curve and keeps the clip's exact length: tape-style audio lands within a
  sample of the curve, and pitch-kept audio within about 5 ms, the small
  leeway that keeps it seamless. With **Preserve pitch** on (the default) the
  tempo follows the curve and the pitch stays the same; turn **Preserve pitch**
  off in the Speed curve section to play the curve tape-style, so the audio
  rises in pitch as it speeds up and falls as it slows down, in the preview and
  the export alike. The clip inspector's own **Preserve pitch** applies to
  constant speeds.

  Keyframes stay on the moments of the source they animated.
  Splitting a curved clip gives each part its piece of the curve, so the parts
  play exactly as the whole clip did.
- **Steps** replaces the clip with 4 to 24 constant-speed pieces, each starting
  exactly where the previous one ended in the source.

On a curved clip, **Bake to clips** turns the curve into **Ramp steps**
constant-speed clips (each at the curve's average speed over its piece, so the
length stays the same), and **Reset to constant** returns it to one constant
speed with the same length. Choosing a speed in the inspector also replaces the
curve. On stepped pieces, **Reset to constant** merges them back. Linked audio
follows the picture in every mode. A curve or ramp is refused when a linked
partner is offset (after a J-cut or L-cut), a fade is longer than the first or
last step, or the clip is a still or multicam clip.

Premiere XML and Final Cut XML exports carry the curve as variable-speed time remapping; OpenTimelineIO
can only play one constant speed, so it writes the curve's average and keeps
the keyframes in a custom `Ryu.SpeedCurve.1` effect and metadata that other
tools do not play (see [handoff files](/docs/apps/video-studio-export#handoff-files)).

## Beats and cutting to music

Choose **Detect beats** on an analyzed music source to measure its tempo (60 to
180 BPM), beats, and bar starts with a confidence score. Detection covers up to
10 minutes of the part of the source a clip plays. Sources with no steady pulse
fall back to a loudness estimate, and the panel says which was used. **Add N
beat markers** and **Add N bar markers** turn the results into timeline markers
(up to 500).

In **Speed & beat**, choose an analyzed music clip, the track to cut, and
**Cut on**: **Every beat**, **Every 2 beats**, **Every 4 beats**, or **Bar
starts (downbeats)**.

- **Snap existing edits to the nearest beat** rolls each edit point on the track
  to a beat within the **Snap tolerance**; edits without spare media stay put.
- **Lay clips one per beat** places the chosen clips back to back, one per beat
  slot, using the middle of each clip or its loudest part. A clip shorter than
  one slot is refused, and the target span must be empty.

If you re-trim the music clip while its beats load, the panel asks you to **Cut
to beat again**.

## Remove filler words and pauses

Open **Cleanup** after transcribing the sources:

- **Filler words** lists fillers (such as "um", "uh", "you know", and "I mean"),
  immediate repeats, and stutters. "Like" counts only between pauses. Edit the
  word list if needed, and review repeats, since some doubles are intentional.
- **Dead air** lists pauses longer than **Shortest pause to remove** (0.6
  seconds to start), keeping **Pause to keep** as breathing room and **Padding
  beside speech** around each word. **Confirm pauses with voice activity**
  checks each pause against the measured audio, using **Speech threshold above
  noise floor** and **Shortest speech**. **Show non-speech on clips** draws the
  detected non-speech spans in red on clips with audio.

Only clips you can hear count: muted, hidden, and zero-volume clips, muted
tracks, and tracks silenced by solo are ignored. Clear any row you want to keep,
then choose **Remove** to cut the selected ranges. Every track ripples by
default; turn off a track's sync lock (for a music bed, say) to keep it in
place.

## Find highlights

Open **Highlights** to rank moments for short clips. Choose how many, the
shortest and longest length, and optional keywords. Candidates follow sentence
boundaries and are scored on speech density, loudness changes and peaks,
opening questions or exclamations, keyword matches, and scene cuts at their
edges. Without a usable transcript, analyzed loudness is used instead. Only
clips you can hear count. Use **Seek** to review a candidate, **Mark** to add a
range marker, or **Make short** to create an alternate cut that keeps only that
highlight.

## AutoCut and vertical shorts

**AutoCut** in **Highlights** builds a new project from 1 to 20 video or image
sources, with optional music, a length from 5 to 600 seconds, a style, an
aspect (16:9, 9:16, 1:1, or 4:5), and optional captions. Energetic cuts on every
beat, smooth and minimal on bar starts, and cinematic on every other bar. Cuts
land on detected beats when **Detect beats** has run on the music, otherwise on
the loudness estimate, or on a steady grid without music. Source audio is muted
under the music, which fades out over the last two seconds. Clips play at
normal speed when the source is long enough; shorter sources are slowed (never
below 0.25×), and nothing is sped up. Your current project is not changed.

**Vertical shorts** turns this project's best highlights into separate short
projects (1 to 10, 15 to 60 seconds by default, 9:16 by default), each captioned
one word at a time in the Punch style and storing a matching publish preset.
Turn on **Export each short** to queue their exports. Track locks are kept.

In both, a clip whose aspect differs from the target pans to keep its subject
(detected from motion and detail) in frame; a clip that cannot be tracked, or
one with its own crop or animated framing, keeps a centred crop. Created
projects get **Open** buttons.

## Script to video

**Script to video** in **Plan** turns a pasted script into an edit. Plan scenes
with Ryu's language model, or choose **Split into scenes** to split locally
without a model. Edit each scene's narration, stock search words, and image
prompt, choose open stock media or generated images, an optional narration
voice, a caption style, and a pause between scenes (0 to 2 seconds).

You can start without a script. Describe the video in **Topic**, set **Script
length**, and choose **Write script with Ryu**: Ryu's language model writes
plain narration of about that length, using the project title and brief, and
fills **Script** for you to edit. **Use storyboard scripts** instead joins the
per-scene scripts that **Draft scenes with Ryu** wrote in the Plan tab, in
scene order. Either way, the script then goes through the same steps as a
pasted one.

Production narrates each scene with Ryu's text-to-speech, transcribes the
narration for word timing, then finds a stock visual or generates an image.
Every narration and image counts against the project budget and goes through
Ryu's approval prompts. Use **Stop after this scene**, retry a failed scene (only
the failed step is redone), or remove it. Without speech or image generation
the scene shows why it failed and assembly stays disabled; without
transcription it reads **Ready · estimated caption timing**.

Assembly places the narration back to back on the first free track you can
hear, by default after the current edit, and sizes each visual to its
narration. Stills get a gentle zoom and drift; long videos are trimmed and
shorter ones slowed (to half speed at most) or repeated. Captions overlapping
the assembled span are replaced. Stock media keeps its attribution; review its
rights before publishing.

Two optional extras are added in the same step:

- **Music bed** scores a local soundtrack over the assembled span and places it
  on a free audio track, faded in and out and set to duck under the narration.
  Choose a style, or **Match the description** to pick one from words such as
  calm, epic, lo-fi, or upbeat. When Ryu's language model is available, a
  description goes to the soundtrack planner, which picks the style, tempo,
  instrument layers, and how the energy changes at the scene cuts; a style you
  chose still wins. Without a model, the local plan changes intensity at each
  scene and builds towards the end. A long score keeps composing in the
  background before it is placed.
- **Presenter avatar** adds a 2.5D avatar for each scene (on the right, on the
  left, or small in the bottom-right corner) for the length of that scene's
  narration, with its mouth lip-synced to the narration. Edit or restyle each
  avatar afterwards in **Avatars**.

## Sync clips and correct drift

Open **Sync** to align audio-bearing clips from cameras and recorders. Choose a
reference clip and targets, then an **Alignment method**: embedded SMPTE
timecode, **Audio correlation** (after source analysis on both), or a manual
offset in seconds. Correlation reports a confidence score and moves targets on
frame boundaries.

With **Correct clock drift** on, Video Studio also measures drift between the
start and end of the shared audio (at least 20 seconds of overlap). Drift up to
±0.5% is corrected with a tiny speed change; larger drift is reported and left
alone. **Measure drift** reports drift without changing anything.

## Multicam

Open **Multicam** to create a named session from two or more camera angles,
a program audio source, and optional **More microphones**. **Switch camera over
range** replaces the selected range with another angle, another program
microphone, or a split-screen layout (fit or fill) showing several angles.
Extra angles go on the lowest free unlocked tracks above the session, and a
layout is refused when none are free. Press 1 to 9 to cut to an angle, or Alt+1
to 9 to switch the microphone, from the playhead to the end of the current
switch. **Ungroup session** keeps the clips and removes the session.

## Auto reframe

**Auto reframe** in **Layouts** converts the project to 9:16, 1:1, 4:5, 16:9,
4:3, 3:4, 2.35:1, or 21:9, keeping the short edge. Choose **Track subject** (with **Smoothing**),
**Center crop**, or **Fit with blur fill**, and optionally **Selected clips
only**. By default only video and image clips that cover the whole canvas
change; picture-in-picture insets, split-screen cells, and clips on locked
tracks are left alone.

Tracking follows motion and detail, not object recognition. At a scene cut the
framing jumps with the cut, and it trails a fast subject slightly to avoid
jitter. The frame can pan across the whole source width, so a subject near the
edge of a 16:9 shot stays in a 9:16 frame. Clip position (**Horizontal
position** and **Vertical position**) reaches ±3 to allow this. Nothing is generated outside the
picture; blur fill is the way to keep the whole frame. A clip that cannot be
tracked keeps a centred crop, and the result says so.

**Image stabilization** (Off, Low, Medium, or High) steadies shaky footage
before the reframe. Each video clip is moved onto a stabilized copy of its
media, the same copy **Stabilize** in Media tools makes and reuses, so the
subject tracking and the crop follow the subject instead of the camera shake.
Stabilizing zooms in slightly (4 to 8% per edge, depending on the level).
Stills, nested sequences, preview proxies, media longer than 10 minutes,
multicam clips, and clips already on stabilized media keep their media; the
result lists them.

## Motion tracking

Open **Motion tracking** in the Vector graphics area, select a video clip, and
park the playhead on a frame showing the subject. Set the region in percent of
the canvas, or choose **Draw region on preview** and drag a box (drag the
dashed box or use the arrow keys to move it). Pick a **Follower**: a title
(position and scale), a graphic (position), another clip such as a sticker
(position and scale), or a new blur or mosaic region, which becomes an
adjustment layer. Then choose **Track motion**.

Tracking runs from the playhead to the clip's end, for at most 120 seconds, and
needs a textured region. A lost subject keeps its last position, and the status
line reports lost frames and the match percentage. Tracking replaces the
follower's existing position and scale keyframes. Clips on locked tracks
cannot be followers.
