## What it does

The project-level view over many chats: recent sessions and what each one accomplished, per-day activity, the files several chats keep returning to, and the to-dos left outstanding in threads nobody reopened. Declares the out-of-process ryu-mission-control sidecar (kind:local), which owns the digest store and the cross-chat rollup and is served to the desktop through the generic ext-proxy public_mount. The digests themselves are computed in the desktop from each chat's message parts and PUT here, because a manifest sidecar has no conversation read and the parts column is sealed at rest - the same function feeds the in-chat Mission Control panel, so a chat's summary is identical in both places.

## Data at rest

Mission Control encrypts the digests it keeps with a key only this app receives from Ryu: digest titles and folders, headlines and summaries, the turn and file lists, and your open to-dos. Counts, times, statuses and agent ids stay readable so the per-day activity and ordering can still run.

Data saved by an earlier version is encrypted when the updated app starts. If the app is updated before Ryu itself, it keeps working unencrypted until Ryu delivers its key. Its health status says it is waiting for a Ryu update.

## Who sees which chats

Mission Control shows, re-indexes, narrates, and removes only chats you can currently open yourself.
On a shared node, the dashboard, its daily activity and open to-dos, and each chat's detail cover
only conversations that:

- you own;
- are shared with your organization or one of your teams;
- you collaborate on.

When you lose access to a chat, or it is deleted, it disappears from your dashboard, even if someone
else indexed it earlier. A deleted chat's summary is never shown for a new chat that later reuses its
ID. The new chat has no summary until it is indexed.

If you can open a very large number of chats, the dashboard covers the most recently active several
thousand of them.
