This guide covers building a [Video Studio](/docs/apps/video-studio) timeline:
placing, selecting, cutting, trimming, and arranging clips, and managing tracks,
markers, nested sequences, and templates. The [workspace guide](/docs/apps/video-studio-workspace) covers projects,
the preview, and the library. [Assisted editing](/docs/apps/video-studio-assisted-editing)
covers the tools that propose edits for you, and
[keyboard shortcuts](/docs/apps/video-studio-shortcuts) lists every key.

## Place clips

Create a project and choose **Import media** for a local file, or paste an HTTPS
URL into **Import media URL** and choose **Import URL**. Imported media is probed
and copied to your Ryu node before it appears in the library. Add items from the
library, drag them onto a lane, or drop files on the window (see
[bring media in](/docs/apps/video-studio-workspace#bring-media-in)). Higher
tracks draw above lower tracks.

Select a clip to edit its source in and out points, start, track, speed,
volume, framing, color, and fades in the inspector. Projects hold up to 5000
clips on 32 tracks and run for up to two hours; titles and graphics are limited
to 1000 each.

## Select, copy, and paste

Click a clip to select it, Shift-click to add clips, or drag on empty lane
space to draw a selection box (hold Shift or Ctrl to add to the selection; a
plain click clears it, and Escape restores the selection you started with).
A selects every clip from the playhead forward on the selected track, Shift+A
forward on every track, and Cmd/Ctrl+A everything. **Select all on track** is in
the clip menu.

Cmd/Ctrl+C, X, and V copy, cut, and paste clips with their linked partners.
Paste lands at the playhead on the same tracks when they are free, otherwise on
the next free track. Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+V pastes and ripples later content right; cut
ripples when ripple mode is on. D duplicates the selection right after its end.
Each paste, cut, or duplicate is one undo step.

**Copy settings** in the inspector's **Clip settings** group copies a clip's
treatment. Tick the groups to paste (transform, color, effects, audio, masks,
and animation), then choose **Paste settings** or press Cmd/Ctrl+Alt+V with the
target clips selected. Pasting never copies the clip's label and color, and
mute, hide, pitch preservation, speaker, timing, and multicam grouping are never
copied. A reveal transition is dropped when the target has no clip before it to
cover.

## Cut, trim, and ripple

- **Split:** S or Cmd/Ctrl+K splits the selected clips at the playhead, or every
  unlocked clip under it when nothing is selected. The **Razor** tool (C) cuts
  where you click; Shift-click cuts every unlocked track.
- **Trim to the playhead:** Q and W ripple-trim the start or end of those clips
  to the playhead, closing the gap. Alt+Q and Alt+W trim and leave a gap. The
  trim moves every sync-locked track, plus titles, captions, and markers, by the
  same amount; a clip still inside the trimmed span on a moving track limits the
  ripple, and the status line says a gap was left. Alt+[ and Alt+] trim only the
  selected clip.
- **Ripple delete:** Shift+Delete deletes the selection and closes the gap.
  Right-click empty lane space and choose **Ripple delete gap** to close a gap
  on that track. It is refused when the track is locked, there is no gap, the
  gap is shorter than 0.04 seconds, or another sync-locked track has clips in
  the gap. Deleting the clip a reveal transition plays over, with Delete or
  ripple delete, removes that reveal from the next clip, which then starts
  with a hard cut.
- **Ripple mode** in the timeline toolbar makes Delete, Alt+[ / Alt+], and
  pointer edits ripple. Dragging a clip edge works like the Ripple tool: later
  clips on the track (and on sync-locked tracks) move with the edge, so no gap
  opens and the edge can extend into the next clip's place. Dragging a clip
  closes the gap it leaves and inserts it at the nearest edit point on the
  track it lands on, pushing later clips right; dropped past the track's last
  clip, it lands right after it. Each drag is one undo step.
  It does not change clip drags; use the Ripple tool (B) for that.

Splitting keeps a clip's entry transition on the first piece and its exit
transition on the last, each shortened to fit. Linked partners are always cut
and trimmed together.

### Drag and trim on the timeline

Dragging any selected clip moves the whole selection; linked audio and video
stay together in time, and only the clips you drag change track. Dragging an
edge trims every selected clip, each stopping at its neighbor and at its source
media. Hold Alt while trimming to leave the linked partner alone (a split edit
by hand). Alt-drag a clip body to drop a copy; a copy that would overlap the
originals is refused. Shift turns snapping off during a drag, and Escape cancels
it. Trims remove animation keyframes that fall outside the shortened clip.

The handles at a clip's top corners set its fade in and fade out and show the
length in seconds. Focus one to adjust it with the arrow keys (one frame, ten
with Shift) or Home and End.

### Edit tools

| Tool | Key | What a drag does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pointer | V | Moves clips with their linked partners; dragging an edge trims. |
| Ripple | B | Trims an edge and shifts later clips on the track by the same amount. |
| Roll | R | Moves the shared edit point between two touching clips. |
| Slip | Y | Changes which part of the source plays without moving the clip. |
| Slide | U | Moves a clip between its neighbors; their touching edges follow. |
| Razor | C | Cuts the clip where you click. |

Every trim snaps to whole frames, respects playback speed, and stops at the
source's limits, the 0.04-second shortest clip, and any clip it would run into.
Ripple trims also shift later markers, captions, titles, and other timed layers.
Slip is not available on multicam clips or still images. During a slip, roll, or
ripple drag the preview shows the frames on both sides of the edit.

### Snapping

Dragged clips and edges snap to clip edges on every track, the playhead (which
wins over other targets), the marked range, markers (both ends of a range
marker), the selected clip's keyframes, and title and graphic bounds, otherwise
to the nearest frame. A highlighted line shows the snap. N or the magnet button
in the timeline toolbar turns snapping off and on; holding Shift skips it for
one drag.

## Zoom and navigate

Zoom with Ctrl+wheel, Alt+wheel, or a pinch around the pointer, with `+`, `=`,
and `-`, or with the zoom slider (5 to 600 pixels per second); Shift+Z fits the
whole timeline. Shift+wheel or Cmd/Ctrl+wheel pans. Drag on the ruler to scrub
frame by frame, or click empty lane space to move the playhead there. With the
playhead handle focused, arrows step one frame (Shift one second), Page Up and
Page Down five seconds, and Home and End jump to the ends.

The timecode field accepts `HH:MM:SS:FF`, `MM:SS:FF`, `;` for drop-frame, a
frame count (`120` or `120f`), seconds (`4.5s`), and relative `+` and `-`
offsets. Up and Down jump between edit points; Shift+Up and Shift+Down between
markers.

Cmd/Ctrl+L cycles loop playback between off, the marked range, and the whole
timeline. `/` plays from two seconds before the playhead to two seconds after,
then returns; J, K, L, or seeking cancel it. Pressing J or L again speeds the
shuttle up to 2×, 4×, and 8×; K stops and resets it.

## Tracks

Each track header has a name, **Hide** (picture and audio, in the preview and
the export), **Mute**, **Solo**, **Lock**, and **Sync lock**, plus a height
(**Small**, **Medium**, or **Large**), gain and pan controls, and up and down
arrows to reorder tracks. **Track options** adds a track above or below, or
removes the track; removing a track that has clips asks first, and a locked
track must be unlocked before it can be removed. Shift+M mutes the selected clip and Shift+H hides it.

Lock prevents selecting, moving, trimming, and inspector edits on a track.
**Sync lock** decides whether a ripple edit on another track shifts this one;
it is on by default. Turn it off on a music bed so range deletes, inserts,
transcript removals, filler and dead-air cleanup, and ripple trims leave it in
place. The edited track always ripples.

Hidden, muted, and zero-volume clips, and clips faster than 16× without **Keep
audio above 16x**, count as silent everywhere the editor listens: dead-air
detection, speaker detection, highlights, and lip-sync sources skip them.

Select an overlapping audio and visual clip and choose **Link overlapping
audio/video** to keep them together; **Unlink audio/video** separates them.

### Clips on the timeline

Video and still clips show filmstrip thumbnails, and clips with sound show a
waveform that follows trim, speed, and volume and dims when muted. Give a clip a
label and a color tag from the inspector's **Label** section or the clip menu.
Clips whose media is missing show an **Offline** badge (see
[relink offline media](/docs/apps/video-studio-media-workflows#offline-media-and-relink)).

Right-click a clip, long-press it, or press Shift+F10 on a focused clip for its
menu: split at the playhead (or **Split here** at the pointer), clipboard
actions, **Select all on track**, mute and hide, **Rename…**, **Color** (with
**No color**), **Detach audio** or **Recover audio**, freeze frame, reverse,
**Speed and ramps…**, **Reveal in library**, **Replace media** (keeping trims,
transform, and grade), **Move to new track**, **Duplicate to new track**,
**Nest**, and **Ask agent**. Unavailable items say why. New tracks for moved or
duplicated clips are the first free tracks above that hold the same kind of
media.

### Titles, graphics, and caption lanes

Titles, graphics, captions, adjustment layers, and avatars have collapsible
lanes above the clip tracks. Drag an item to move it or change lane, drag its
edges to trim it, and use snapping as with clips. Delete removes it, Cmd/Ctrl+D
duplicates it, and Alt+Left/Right nudges it by a frame (Shift for one second).
Every item keeps at least one frame, and trims keep fades, keyframes, and
caption word timing consistent.

## Detach and recover audio

Select a video clip with sound and choose **Detach audio**. Video Studio
extracts the source's audio once into an app-owned audio file, places a linked
audio clip with the same position, trim, speed, volume, pan, audio cleanup,
ducking, and volume and pan animation, and mutes the video clip. The audio clip
goes on the first free unlocked track without visual clips. Reversed clips and
nested sequences keep their audio embedded.

On either clip of a detached pair the inspector offers **Recover audio**. It
removes the extracted audio clip, restores its settings to the video clip
(moving keyframes with it if the audio was moved), and removes the link; the
extracted file stays in the library. Both tracks must be unlocked.

## J-cuts and L-cuts

A J-cut starts the next shot's audio before its picture; an L-cut lets the
previous shot's audio continue after the picture changes. Both need linked
audio and video pairs back to back on the same video track, so link or detach
each clip's audio first.

Use the inspector's **Split edit** group: **J-cut** and **L-cut** roll the
audio edit point by the **Split offset** (up to four seconds, in whole frames)
while the picture cut stays put; **Straight cut** puts it back. **Audio
crossfade** (0 to 0.5 seconds, 0.08 by default) overlaps the two audio clips
around the new edit point where media allows; otherwise a 0.01-second fade
removes clicks. Alt+J and Alt+L apply the same edit at the selected clip's cut,
or the cut nearest the playhead.

Offsets never invent audio: when a clip has less spare media than you ask for,
Video Studio applies the largest offset that fits and reports it. Audio that
would overlap another clip moves to a free unlocked audio track and stays
linked. An offset badge on the audio clip shows how far its edit point is from
the picture cut. For proposals across a whole edit, see
[automatic J/L cuts](/docs/apps/video-studio-assisted-editing#automatic-jl-cuts).

## Markers

Press M to add a marker at the playhead, or to remove the one there; Alt+M turns
the marked range into a range marker. Drag a ruler marker to move it.
Double-click, right-click, or press Shift+Enter on it to edit its label, color,
status, range length, and comment. `[` and `]` jump between markers.

The **Markers** panel in the Exports area lists every marker with a status
filter (**All**, **Unresolved**, **Resolved**) and a comment field per marker
that saves when you leave it; undo and agent edits do not overwrite what you are
typing. **Ask agent** on a marker row (or **Ask agent about this marker** in
the ruler marker's editor) hands that marker, with its status and comment, to
the agent. It also has a ripple-markers switch (when off, ripple edits leave
markers in place) and a switch to show marker labels over the preview. Markers
never change the exported length.

## Range edits and inserts

Press I and O to mark a range, or hold Shift and drag across the ruler. The
marked range is shaded across the ruler and every track. Drag its In or Out
edge on the ruler to adjust it (edges snap to clip edges, markers, and the
playhead; hold Alt to place an edge without snapping), or focus an edge and use
the arrow keys (Shift moves a second). **Clear range** (Alt+X) hides it.

The range bar offers actions on the marked range:

- **Ripple delete range** removes it. Later clips shift left on every
  sync-locked track, captions and timed layers keep their relative timing, and
  markers inside the cut are removed.
- **Export range** opens the export settings with **Only the marked range**
  on; **Save range to library** also turns on saving the result to the media
  library.
- **Ask agent** hands the range (and any selected clips) to the agent.
- **Score this range** creates music for the range and places it at the range
  start on a free audio track (see [local soundtracks](/docs/apps/video-studio-audio#local-soundtracks)).

**Insert at playhead** splices a copy of the selected clip into its track at the
playhead, splitting a crossing clip and shifting later clips, captions, titles,
graphics, avatars, meshes, and markers right.

## Nested sequences

The **Nested sequences** section of the Media tab renders another project as a
sequence clip; **Refresh sequence** re-renders it after source edits. A
sequence clip always renders as H.264 MP4 with picture and audio at the nested
project's full length and canvas size, whatever that project's own export
settings are. Cycles are refused.

Select clips and press Cmd/Ctrl+Alt+G, or choose **Nest selection**, to move
them (with titles and graphics fully inside their span) into a new project with
this project's canvas, frame rate, and codec, replaced by one sequence clip.
Nesting never changes the output: the nested project keeps each source track's
hide, mute, solo, gain, and pan result. It is refused when other pictures sit
below or between the selected clips, an adjustment layer sits between their
tracks, or audible clips would land on a muted track, and for multicam clips,
template slots, lip-sync sources, locked tracks, and audio-only selections.
**Open nested** opens the child project in a tab.

Select a sequence clip and press Cmd/Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G, or choose **Decompose**,
to put its clips back. Only the part inside the clip's trimmed range returns,
moving up to free tracks when needed. Restored clips take the parent tracks'
settings; clips from muted or hidden nested tracks come back muted or hidden.
Speed, transform, effects, and keyframes set on the sequence clip itself are
not carried over, and transitions on a trimmed-away edge are dropped. Reset the
sequence clip's speed to 100% and unlock its track before decomposing.

## Templates

Open **Templates** in the Recipes area. Select clips and choose **Save N
selected clips as template**: those clips become replaceable slots, ordered by
start and track and labeled by kind ("Video 1", "Image 2"), while titles,
effects, timing, and other clips stay fixed. Eight original templates are built
in: vlog intro, product promo, recap montage, quote card, podcast clip,
slideshow, event highlight, and tutorial step. Their slots are whole bars of a
locally generated music bed, which you can replace.

Choose **Use template**, pick media in order, add an optional title (and, for a
built-in, turn **Generate music** on or off), and create the project. Visual
slots take video or stills and audio slots take audio. Stills hold for the slot;
longer video is trimmed and shorter video slowed to fill a fixed slot (never
below 0.1×); audio is trimmed but never retimed. Unfilled slots keep their
placeholder and extra media is reported as unused. The new project opens and
reports how many slots were filled.

## Project settings

Project settings set the canvas, the frame rate (23.976, 24,
25, 29.97, 30, 48, 50, 59.94, 60, or 120), the export codec, and a **Visual
style** (**Clean professional**, **Flat motion**, **Minimalist diagram**, or
**Cinematic**) that sets the canvas color and the default accent for new shapes.
When the first video you add differs from the project in size or frame rate, a
banner offers **Match project to media**, which keeps the aspect with even edges
and changes the rate only to a supported project rate.

**Format** lists canvas presets: 16:9 at 720p, 1080p, 1440p, 4K UHD, and 8K
UHD; 9:16 vertical; 1:1 square; 4:5 portrait; 4:3 classic; 21:9 ultrawide;
1.85:1 flat and full 2K DCI; DCI 4K; and a 360p preview size. Choose **Custom
size…** to type **Canvas width** and **Canvas height** in pixels. Each edge is
rounded to an even number between 128 and 8192, and the shorter edge grows if
needed to keep the aspect between 1:10 and 10:1. A project whose canvas matches
no preset shows its size as **Custom**.

Choose **Duplicate project** to make an independent alternate cut that shares
the same media. Edits use revision checks: if another editor or agent saved a
newer revision, Video Studio pauses and offers to reload. The
[agent access guide](/docs/apps/video-studio-agents) lists the matching routes,
and the [workspace guide](/docs/apps/video-studio-workspace#save-autosave-and-history)
covers autosave and history.
