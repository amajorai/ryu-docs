## Paid access skips the waitlist

Completing a paid Teams or Business checkout approves the account when payment is confirmed.
Enterprise approvals are handled through the organization conversation and
contract.

## Choose the organization before checkout

When signed in, select the organization that should own Teams or Business. The selection is
carried through checkout and checked again when payment completes. Billing is
organization-scoped; it is not a collection of individual seat toggles.

Only an organization owner or admin can buy Teams or Business for that organization. The local
desktop license is separate from organization billing and does not grant hosted
agents or a shared organization wallet.

## Personal workspaces and business email

Every account starts with a default one-person workspace. Accounts using a
public mailbox (Gmail, Outlook, Live, Yahoo, Proton, and similar) stay in the
personal boundary: they cannot invite, add, or accept another member. A
company-domain account is stamped as a Teams boundary instead, but its owner
still has one member until the business email is verified and a paid Teams or Business
subscription is active. The one-member rule for personal workspaces is enforced
by the server, so it also applies to direct API calls and older invitation links.

Shared organizations are Teams boundaries. Creating one, joining one, and
starting its Teams or Business checkout require a verified business-domain email. Public
mailbox domains such as Gmail, Outlook, Live, Yahoo, and Proton are not accepted.
The default company-domain bootstrap may exist while verification is pending so
the owner has a home; Ryu then requires both the domain and Better Auth's
`emailVerified` state for shared membership, invitation acceptance, and checkout.
This is an admission heuristic; Enterprise can add stronger domain, SSO, or DNS
verification requirements.

If the owner started with a personal email, do not create a second Ryu account.
Change and verify the email on the same account, then return to **Organizations
→ Billing**. The completed business-email change moves the same workspace into
the Teams boundary; the Teams purchase then activates paid shared access after
Polar confirms the subscription. For a legacy personal workspace whose owner
already has a company email, the paid webhook performs that same conversion.
The organization id, agents, workflows, wallet, and audit history remain intact.
No email change or checkout can add a second member to the personal boundary.

## Invite organization members

Organization owners and admins can open **Members → Invite a member** and enter
email addresses or usernames. Invitees receive an accept-invitation link, and
people without an account receive a sign-up link that returns them to the
invitation.

Each pending invitation reserves one organization seat. The Members page shows
active members as `active / capacity`; the invite control is disabled when all
effective seats are active or reserved. Cancel a pending invitation, remove a
member, or buy more seats before sending another invite. Acceptance is checked
again on the server, so an invitation cannot grant access after the seat was
used by someone else.

Ryu uses Better Auth's native organization endpoints for this lifecycle: create,
list, update, delete, active-organization selection, member roles, invitation
creation, resend, acceptance, rejection, cancellation, and leave. An invitation
can optionally name a Better Auth team; after acceptance, the member is added to
that team. The organization and team pages, Desktop workspace settings, Native
organization screen, and extension context menu persist the active team on the
Better Auth session, so team-aware requests use one server-owned context across
all four clients.

See the [Better Auth organization plugin reference](https://better-auth.com/docs/plugins/organization)
for the underlying endpoint, schema, access-control, and lifecycle-hook contract.

An organization with an active Teams or Business subscription must cancel that
plan before it can be deleted. Leaving an organization removes only the caller's
membership; it does not cancel the organization's subscription or reduce its
billed seat quantity.

The automatically created personal workspace cannot invite members. Personal
managed subscriptions stay personal even when they include managed AI; use the
same workspace's Teams or Business checkout when more than one person needs shared
access, roles, budgets, or an organization audit trail.

Organizations use built-in **Owner**, **Admin**, **Member**, and **Viewer** roles and can add
narrower custom Ryu roles. A custom role is a permission bundle that can be assigned at the whole
organization or one team. Billing, notification management, node management, member management,
and capability execution are separate permissions, so an organization can give a process
operator access without giving them spending authority.

Ryu keeps two role systems deliberately separate. Better Auth owns organization membership,
invitations, teams, and its own organization-management actions; its dynamic-role support remains
available for those actions. Ryu owns the capability catalog used by Core, Gateway, Agent Mail,
Marketplace, and resource ACLs. A Better Auth role or custom Better Auth action never widens a Ryu
capability by itself. Ryu checks the live member row, custom assignment, team membership, node
scope, and resource ACL on every protected operation.

Better Auth's organization lifecycle hooks remain the extension seam for organization, member,
invitation, and team events. Ryu adds seat admission, business-email policy, step-up checks, audit
projections, and notifications in those hooks; it does not replace the organization or invitation
records with a second membership store.

To create or assign a Ryu role, a caller needs `roles.manage` and may only grant permissions they
already hold. Deleting a role removes its assignments. A role assignment cannot target a team
unless the target user is a member of that team. Deleted role keys remain reserved so a stale node
ACL cannot unexpectedly reactivate when a later role uses the same key. This prevents a role editor
from minting a privilege ceiling above their own authority.

## Organization roles and API keys

Organization roles also govern the Better Auth organization-management actions exposed by Ryu. A
member can view organization API-key metadata; an owner or admin can create and revoke an
organization key. Custom roles can narrow those management actions without becoming a shortcut to
Ryu's capability scopes.

API keys are separate from membership. When an owner or admin creates an organization key, Ryu
clamps the requested `resource:action` scopes to the caller's server-side permissions and returns
the plaintext only once. The key remains owned by the organization, so removing its creator does
not silently remove the integration. Revoke or rotate the key from **Organizations → API keys** if
the integration changes hands or the secret may have leaked.

Ryu still evaluates the key's scopes, organization membership, server ACLs, entitlements, budgets,
and Gateway policy on every protected request. A Better Auth role that can manage a key record does
not, by itself, grant access to chat, agents, files, or other Ryu resources. Personal nodes are
owner-only; team nodes require team membership (or organization-level node management); and
organization nodes remain visible to organization members.

## Review, decline, and block

People review pending requests from **Invitations** in the user menu. They can
accept or choose **Decline and block**. A blocked organization cannot send that
person another invitation.

For safety, the same organization cannot send another invitation to the same
email address for 24 hours. A decline is stronger than the cooldown and blocks
future invitations from that organization for that recipient.

Invitation activity is transactional. The invited person receives the invitation or account
confirmation email, and the organization’s configured notification roles receive server-side inbox
activity for invitations sent, accepted, declined, or cancelled. The same role-aware fan-out is
used for organization-scoped node, credit, Marketplace, and Agent Mail events; a transfer involving
two organizations resolves both rosters. Email can be paused from **Organization → Notifications**,
while the server-side Inbox history remains available.

<Callout type="info">
  Billing access, member invitations, and invitation acceptance are separate
  decisions. Buying organization access never silently adds a member.
</Callout>
