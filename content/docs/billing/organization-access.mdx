title: "Organization access and invitations"
description: "How organization billing, member roles, and invitations work."
---

## Paid access skips the waitlist

Completing a paid Teams checkout approves the account when payment is confirmed.
Enterprise approvals are handled through the organization conversation and
contract.

## Choose the organization before checkout

When signed in, select the organization that should own Teams. The selection is
carried through checkout and checked again when payment completes. Billing is
organization-scoped; it is not a collection of individual seat toggles.

Only an organization owner or admin can buy Teams for that organization. The local
desktop license is separate from organization billing and does not grant hosted
agents or a shared organization wallet.

## Personal workspaces and business email

Every account starts with a default one-person workspace. Accounts using a
public mailbox (Gmail, Outlook, Live, Yahoo, Proton, and similar) stay in the
personal boundary: they cannot invite, add, or accept another member. A
company-domain account is stamped as a Teams boundary instead, but its owner
still has one member until the business email is verified and a paid Teams
subscription is active. The one-member rule for personal workspaces is enforced
by the server, so it also applies to direct API calls and older invitation links.

Shared organizations are Teams boundaries. Creating one, joining one, and
starting its Teams checkout require a verified business-domain email. Public
mailbox domains such as Gmail, Outlook, Live, Yahoo, and Proton are not accepted.
The default company-domain bootstrap may exist while verification is pending so
the owner has a home; Ryu then requires both the domain and Better Auth's
`emailVerified` state for shared membership, invitation acceptance, and checkout.
This is an admission heuristic; Enterprise can add stronger domain, SSO, or DNS
verification requirements.

If the owner started with a personal email, do not create a second Ryu account.
Change and verify the email on the same account, then return to **Organizations
→ Billing**. The completed business-email change moves the same workspace into
the Teams boundary; the Teams purchase then activates paid shared access after
Polar confirms the subscription. For a legacy personal workspace whose owner
already has a company email, the paid webhook performs that same conversion.
The organization id, agents, workflows, wallet, and audit history remain intact.
No email change or checkout can add a second member to the personal boundary.

## Invite organization members

Organization owners and admins can open **Members → Invite a member** and enter
email addresses or usernames. Invitees receive an accept-invitation link, and
people without an account receive a sign-up link that returns them to the
invitation.

The automatically created personal workspace cannot invite members. Personal
managed subscriptions stay personal even when they include managed AI; use the
same workspace's For Teams checkout when more than one person needs shared
access, roles, budgets, or an organization audit trail.

Organizations use built-in **Owner**, **Admin**, **Member**, and **Viewer** roles
and can add narrower custom roles. Billing and member management remain separate
permissions, so an organization can give a process operator access without
giving them spending authority.

## Organization roles and API keys

Organization roles also govern the Better Auth organization-management actions exposed by Ryu. A
member can view organization API-key metadata; an owner or admin can create and revoke an
organization key. Custom roles can narrow those management actions without becoming a shortcut to
Ryu's capability scopes.

API keys are separate from membership. When an owner or admin creates an organization key, Ryu
clamps the requested `resource:action` scopes to the caller's server-side permissions and returns
the plaintext only once. The key remains owned by the organization, so removing its creator does
not silently remove the integration. Revoke or rotate the key from **Organizations → API keys** if
the integration changes hands or the secret may have leaked.

Ryu still evaluates the key's scopes, organization membership, node ACLs, entitlements, budgets,
and Gateway policy on every protected request. A Better Auth role that can manage a key record does
not, by itself, grant access to chat, agents, files, or other Ryu resources.

## Review, decline, and block

People review pending requests from **Invitations** in the user menu. They can
accept or choose **Decline and block**. A blocked organization cannot send that
person another invitation.

For safety, the same organization cannot send another invitation to the same
email address for 24 hours. A decline is stronger than the cooldown and blocks
future invitations from that organization for that recipient.

<Callout type="info">
  Billing access, member invitations, and invitation acceptance are separate
  decisions. Buying organization access never silently adds a member.
</Callout>
