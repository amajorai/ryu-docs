title: "Organizations in Console"
description: "Manage an organization's usage, spend limits, members, keys, policies, vault secrets, and billing in Console."

Console is Ryu on the web for your account and your organizations. Every organization has its own
section of Console at `/organizations/<organization id>`. It uses the same layout as the desktop
app's Settings: a grouped sidebar beside the page, with a search box at the top. Searching lists
matching pages and individual settings, such as **Payment methods** or **Invoices** on the billing
page, and choosing one opens it. Drag the divider to resize the sidebar. The sidebar lists
everything you can manage for that organization:

| Group | Pages |
|---|---|
| Monitor | Overview, Usage, Limits & budgets, Logs |
| Organization | Projects, Members, Teams, Invitations, Notifications |
| Access | Service accounts, API keys, Admin keys, Gateway keys, Bot access, Security (SSO and SCIM) |
| Governance | Policies, Vault, Data controls, Config, Audit log |
| Platform | Integrations, Features, Products, Marketplace & payouts |
| Nodes | Cloud computers, Fleet status, Rules, Stack templates, Managed content |
| Billing | Billing, Credit history |

The sidebar shows only the pages your role can use. For example, a member without audit or billing
access does not see **Limits & budgets** or the **Audit log**. Hiding a page is a convenience: the
server checks your permissions again on every request. Reading **Config**, the organization's
**API keys**, or its firewall rules requires Gateway configuration permission, the same permission
that changing them requires. Owners and admins have it by default, and a custom role can grant it.
**Policies** stay readable to members because members can manage their own user-level policy. On a narrow screen, the sidebar
sits above the page in its own scrolling area. Search only offers pages your role can open.

Opening an organization in Console also makes it your active organization, so the organization
switcher in the header matches the page you are viewing. Earlier links such as
`/organizations/members` or `/organizations/billing` still work. They open the same page in your
active organization in Console and keep any query parameters, including checkout returns. The
former seller settings address, `/settings/seller`, opens **Marketplace & payouts** for your active
organization in Console. With no active organization it asks you to select one, and choosing one in
the organization switcher opens that organization's page.

## Overview

**Overview** summarizes every Gateway that reports to the organization: total requests, error rate,
spend, and tokens, then spend by Gateway, an eval leaderboard ranking providers by average score,
shared budgets, and the most recent requests. A budget is marked **Near limit** at 80% of its cap
and **Exhausted** once spend reaches it. Turn on **Errors only** to list only the failed requests;
the rest of the page does not change. **Refresh** reloads everything and shows when the data was last
updated.

Members who can configure Gateways or manage billing see every Gateway in these totals. Other
members see only the Gateways they can reach: organization-wide Gateways, their teams' Gateways,
and their own personal Gateway. Another member's personal Gateway or a team they are not in is left
out.

If no Gateway has reported yet, the page says so. Members who can configure Gateways also see a
link to create a Gateway key.

## Projects

**Projects** shows the organization's teams with the projects that belong to each, followed by
projects without a team, plus a count of each. Search narrows the list by team name or by project
name or slug; a matching team keeps all of its projects.
**New team** and **New project** create either one; a project can be assigned to a team when you
create it.

## Usage

**Usage** charts cost and tokens over time and totals them in a table. Choose a range (the last
24 hours, 7, 30, or 90 days, or a custom range of whole UTC days). Owners and members with audit or
billing access can group the table by member, API key, team, model, provider, or project, and choose
**Export CSV** to download the same rows. An export holds at most 10,000 rows; when more matched,
the page warns that the file is incomplete so you can narrow the range or choose a larger bucket.
Other members see only their own usage.

If the organization's server does not report usage breakdowns yet, the page says so instead of
showing empty charts. Credit spend stays available under **Billing → Credit history**.

## Limits and budgets

**Limits & budgets** sets spend caps and rate limits at four levels:

- **Organization cap**: one cap on the whole organization.
- **Member default**: applies to every member who has no limit of their own.
- **Per member**, **per team**, and **per key**: limits for specific people, teams, or API and
  Gateway keys.

Each limit has an optional budget in US dollars, a window (daily, weekly, monthly, or lifetime;
windows follow UTC calendar days), what happens when the budget runs out (**Stop requests**,
**Downgrade model** to a model you name, or **Notify only**), alert thresholds as percentages of the
budget, and optional requests-per-minute and tokens-per-minute limits. Leave a field empty for no
limit. A budget of $0 blocks all charged spend, and rate limits must be at least 1 per minute.

Every limit that applies to a request is checked, not only the most specific one. A request is
blocked when any applicable **Stop requests** budget is used up, whether it belongs to the member,
their team, the key, or the organization.

The **Current window** table shows spend so far against each cap, highest first, with a progress
bar. Shared budgets coordinated across Gateways appear below the editor. Members with audit access
can view limits. Changing them needs the billing permission, and every change is written to the
organization's audit log. If another admin saves limits while you are editing, your save is refused
with a message asking you to reload, so one change cannot overwrite the other.

### Tag budgets

**Tag budgets** caps spend by the [builder tags](/docs/standalone/connect-api-tags) callers put on
Ryu Connect and Ryu Gateway calls in `x-ryu-tags`. Each budget names a tag, a daily or monthly UTC
window, a limit in US dollars, and whether reaching it refuses tagged calls or only reports it. The
table shows each budget's spend in its current window, and you can export the last seven days of
spend per tag as CSV. The same permissions apply as for limits: audit access to view, the billing
permission to change, and a save is refused if someone else saved since you loaded the page.

## Billing

The **Billing** page is organization-scoped. Owners and billing managers manage the shared plan
and payment details, while members can still view the shared credit balance and usage. You can
also reach it from the organization switcher: choose **Organization settings**, then the
**Billing** tab. The page includes:

- the current plan, renewal status, upgrade action, and a link to view all plans;
- [payment methods and invoice history](/docs/billing/checkout#payment-methods-and-invoices),
  with PDF invoices;
- the shared credit balance, included monthly pool, rollover top-up balance, and credit usage;
- a link to Gift Ryu for personal gift cards, credit gifts, and non-renewing subscription gifts
  (see [Gift Cards, Credits, and Subscriptions](/docs/billing/gifts));
- manual credit top-ups, low-balance email alerts, and bounded automatic top-up settings.

Payment methods and invoice downloads open in the provider portal only after Ryu verifies that
provider-side plan and seat editing is disabled. Plan changes, cancellation, and seat changes use
Ryu's server-side billing routes, and Ryu keeps the organization role check on every billing
action. Every card-funded action also requires enrolled authenticator-based 2FA; email-only
confirmation is not accepted for billing.

Organization billing-status subscriptions receive a fresh status message after an accepted
payment. Each refresh is addressed to the members of the organization that owns the payment. A
subscription opens for the organization active in the account session and keeps that
organization while it stays open. Payment-triggered refreshes preserve the member's applicable
gift entitlement. If a member belongs to several organizations, a payment in another
organization does not change this subscription's organization.

### General settings

Owners and admins can rename a shared organization and change its URL slug. An owner can delete
the organization; an admin or member can leave it. Personal workspaces can be renamed but keep
their signup slug and do not show leave or delete actions. Failed saves, leaves, or deletes keep
the dialog open with an error so the action can be retried. Deleting an organization also
requires a fresh identity check.

## Vault

**Vault** is the browser view of the organization [Secret Vault](/docs/security/secret-vault):

- **Secrets** lists organization and team secrets for one environment, with their folder, version,
  and status. **New secret** and **New version** write a value. **Versions** lists earlier versions
  and can roll back to one. **Delete** keeps the versions, so a deleted secret can be restored from
  **Show deleted**.
- Values stay hidden. **Reveal** appears only when you hold the `read` capability on that secret;
  it first asks you to confirm it's you, and one confirmation lasts 10 minutes. Revealing a value
  is recorded in the vault audit log, and **Hide** clears the value from the page.
  Members who can only use a secret never see its value.
- In a protected environment, a write or delete from someone without `admin` becomes a change
  request. **Change requests** lists the requests you opened and those you can approve, with
  **Approve**, **Reject**, and **Cancel request**.
- **Policies** grants or denies capabilities on secret paths and binds policies to users, teams,
  roles, or machines. **Audit** lists vault activity without secret values. Both tabs need vault
  admin access.

## Gateway keys that serve your own users

If your application calls models through a gateway key on behalf of its own users, turn on
**Serves end users** for that key on **Gateway keys**. Requests on that key can then name the
person behind each request with the `x-ryu-end-user` header, and that person, rather than the
signed-in member, becomes the identity the request is attributed to. On every other key the header
is recorded but never replaces the member, so a member cannot appear as a different person on each
request. Keys are off by default. Changing the setting needs permission to manage nodes and a fresh
confirmation code, and the **Audit log** records it. A node's paired credentials always change
together. The switch is on the keys listed on **Gateway keys**; gateway keys that belong to a
service account do not have it yet. See
[End-user identity](/docs/gateway/managed-data-plane#end-user-identity) for the header format.

## Account standing and gateway keys

Ryu staff can act on accounts that break Ryu's usage policies. Each action is a person's decision,
with one exception described below, and owners and admins get a notice in their Inbox when it
affects their organization:

- **A member is banned.** Gateway keys and personal API keys the member owned are revoked and stay
  revoked even if the ban is lifted later. Organization keys that member created keep working for 72
  hours so you can rotate them: create a replacement on **Gateway keys**, move your traffic, then
  revoke the old key. After the deadline an unrotated key stops working. When the key itself was
  misused, it is suspended right away instead. Nodes the member enrolled for the organization keep
  running while Ryu reviews them, so a ban does not take your nodes offline.
- **A gateway key is suspended.** Requests with it are refused until Ryu lifts the suspension.
  Suspension is reversible, unlike revoking. You can create a replacement key at any time. This is
  the one action Ryu takes automatically: when Ryu's screening blocks a child safety violation on
  traffic that spends Ryu credentials, the key that sent it is suspended at once, and only a person
  at Ryu can lift it. Traffic on your own provider accounts never triggers it, and nobody is banned
  automatically.
- **The organization is restricted or suspended.** Ryu can limit an organization while it reviews
  activity on the account. Owners and admins are told when that happens and again when access is
  restored.

The **Audit log** records each of these changes as a system action, without the staff member's
identity or the reason details.
