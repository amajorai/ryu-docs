Hosted agent deployments are for teams whose output is capped by a recurring
process backlog. Each agent owns a named process—such as document intake,
working-paper preparation, lead follow-up, or translation review—with its
instructions, tools, files, and approval points.

The subscription gives the organization one shared agent workspace. Start
with a workflow app, then ask Ryu to change its form, approval rule, view, or
integration. Ryu should present that change as a proposal; an authorized person
publishes it. Seats measure the people who operate and review the deployment, not
the number of apps the organization creates.

Teams and Business are self-serve organization plans. Business is an optional

that need custom capacity, deployment, or procurement requirements.

Personal managed plans are separate individual subscriptions. Pro and Max are
shown on the Individual pricing shelf for one person who wants Ryu-managed AI;
they do not grant shared organization process capacity. A business process used
by multiple people belongs on the Teams, Business, or Enterprise path.

The public Teams floor is $250/month. Annual billing is $2,500/year, equivalent
to exactly $208.33/month at five seats, with two months free. The hosted path is not
a generic five-message chat trial: qualified organizations may receive a
private, conversation-led paid pilot; the voucher is issued directly to the
organization and is not a public checkout tier.

## What a Teams seat means

The seat is a person with access to the shared organization workspace. It does
not mean one person receives a separate AI wallet or one agent is assigned to
one person. The useful measurement is the process outcome: hours returned,
backlog cleared, response time improved, or dollars of capacity that would
otherwise require a hire or contractor.

Teams includes:

- five billed member seats at the minimum;
- $50/month of managed AI credits at the floor, pooled across the organization;
- guided setup around the organization's existing process;
- organization-scoped access, spend controls, and audit history;
- an organization-specific managed server with 2 vCPU, 4 GB RAM, and 40 GB SSD
  in the default region (the Singapore equivalent is 2 vCPU, 4 GB RAM, and
  80 GB SSD);
- unlimited Agent Inboxes, 100,000 sends/month, and 20 GB of stored mail;
- 50 GB of space storage and 8 concurrent background runs.

Additional billed member seats cost $50/month each through the 50-seat self-serve
limit. The included Teams pool adds $50 whenever the billed quantity crosses
another five-seat bundle. Private negotiated seats may expand member capacity
without adding credits or changing the billed quantity.

## What a Business plan adds

Business starts at $300/month for five human member seats, then adds $50 for
each additional seat. Its pooled AI-credit grant is $100/month at five through
nine billed seats and adds $100 at each completed five-seat bundle. Business
includes a performance managed server backed by 4 vCPU, 8 GB RAM, and 160 GB SSD
in the default region. It keeps the same organization roles, wallet, audit
history, 50 GB space limit, and member-seat meaning as Teams. Managed-plan
top-ups carry a 16% deposit fee with a $2.75 minimum.

## Organization boundary

The subscription and seat quantity belong to the selected organization. Members
share the organization's access controls, wallet, budgets, and audit history.
Teams and Business support up to 50 active organization members. Organizations with
51 or more active members need an Enterprise agreement.

See [Teams seats](/docs/billing/seats) for invitation, acceptance, upgrade,
downgrade, and proration rules.

## Multi-tenant boundary

Ryu runs a pooled control plane for many organizations, but an organization is
the tenant boundary. Every hosted request carries an organization identity, and
the server checks membership and role before reading or changing organization
data. Billing, seat quantity, workflow capacity, wallets, spend controls, audit history,
files, inboxes, and gateway credentials are scoped to that organization.

Background work carries the same organization identity instead of relying on a
browser's active tab. Queries and service-to-service calls fail closed when the
organization is missing, and the organization id is never taken from an
untrusted cross-tenant request body.

## Servers and isolation

Servers are runtime capacity, not the commercial allowance. Teams gets one
organization-specific managed server through 49 seats and two at the 50-seat
self-serve tier; the provider's underlying shared-vCPU hardware is not a shared
Ryu tenant VM. Ryu can resize or replace a server without changing the
organization's contracted seat quantity. Enterprise can use
dedicated or self-hosted deployment when a stronger infrastructure boundary is
required.

Business gets one included performance-oriented organization server. The server is
still runtime capacity, not an additional member or automation allowance.

Local model execution is off by default on shared hosted capacity. If you
self-host Ryu, you control the machine and can enable local inference according
to your own resource and isolation choices.

## The quickest path to a useful first result

The first hosted step is a guided Teams pilot, not an empty chatbot. Ryu and the
organization agree on one repeatable process, the required access and approval
points, and what “live” means. Ryu then creates the organization-scoped agent,
connects the approved tools, runs a test case, and hands over a workflow the
team can use. A five-message sandbox is useful for product discovery, but it is
not a meaningful test of business automation and does not replace this setup.

## Adding capacity

Start with the people who need to operate and govern the shared workspace. When
another person needs access, an owner/admin adds a billed seat in the
organization billing screen. The slider shows the monthly total and hands
larger organizations to Enterprise. These are planning choices, not promises
about a process's output.

## Compare hours and dollars

Compare Teams or Business with the fully loaded cost of adding capacity: salary, benefits,
management time, training, contractor fees, and the cost of leaving the work
undone. The [pricing calculator](/pricing) lets you edit those assumptions. Use
the result as a planning anchor, then validate it against the workflow's actual
hours and output.
