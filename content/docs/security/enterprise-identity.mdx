---

Enterprise identity controls connect an organization's identity system to its Ryu workspace. Employees sign in with the company identity provider (single sign-on, SSO), and the directory can keep Ryu membership in sync through SCIM (System for Cross-domain Identity Management) when people join, change teams, or leave.

SSO answers **"how does this person sign in?"** SCIM answers **"which people should have an account, and are they still active?"** They work together, but one does not replace the other.

## Before you start

You need:

- an active Enterprise entitlement for the organization;
- an organization owner or admin account (a custom role with `access.manage` can view this
  configuration and revoke a SCIM connection, but registering, updating, verifying, or deleting an
  SSO provider and generating or rotating a SCIM token need the owner or admin role);
- a public HTTPS Ryu auth URL reachable by your identity provider and directory service; and
- an administrator account in Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, or another OIDC/SAML/SCIM-compatible system.

A self-hosted deployment that uses SCIM also needs a MongoDB deployment with transaction support (for example, a replica set or sharded cluster), so multi-record provisioning changes are committed together.

Open **Organizations → your organization → Security**. If the organization is not entitled, the page explains how to contact Ryu about Enterprise access. Protocol endpoints remain server-side; provider secrets and SCIM bearer tokens are not returned in list responses.

Production deployments must keep identity encryption keys and provider credentials in server-side
secret storage. Those values are never sent to the browser.

## Configure SSO

Ryu supports OIDC and SAML 2.0 connections. Start with OIDC when your provider supports it; SAML is useful when your organization's identity policy requires it. The Security page shows only provider types included in the organization's Enterprise entitlement.

### OIDC

1. In your identity provider, create a web application for Ryu.
2. In Ryu, choose **OIDC (Okta, Entra ID, Google Workspace)** and enter a short provider ID, your company domain, the issuer URL, client ID, and client secret.
3. Save the connection. Copy the redirect URI returned for the provider into the identity provider's allowed callback URLs.
4. Ask Ryu to issue a domain-verification record, add the TXT record to DNS, and choose **Verify now**.
5. Test sign-in from the Ryu login page with a work email.

The issuer must be the identity provider's issuer, not an arbitrary API URL. Ryu uses the provider ID and the verified domain to route the login to the organization. A verified provider is still attached to one explicit Ryu organization; a matching email domain alone does not grant organization access.

### SAML 2.0

1. In Ryu, choose **SAML 2.0**, then enter the provider ID, company domain, identity-provider SSO URL, and signing certificate.
2. If your provider supplies metadata XML, paste it into the optional metadata field.
3. Use the generated Ryu SP metadata and ACS values in the identity provider application.
4. Configure the provider to send a stable NameID/subject, email, and display name. Assertions must be signed.
5. Verify the domain and test the login before enabling the connection for employees.

The ACS URL follows this shape:

```text
https://<your-ryu-auth-host>/api/auth/sso/saml2/sp/acs/<provider-id>
```

Keep signing certificates current. When a certificate or endpoint changes, update the connection, run a test login, and keep a break-glass owner/admin account available until the new assertion works.

## Employee sign-in

The login page includes **Continue with SSO**. An employee enters their work email; Ryu resolves the matching verified provider and redirects to the company's identity provider. After the callback, the user joins the configured organization as a member. An organization admin can change roles separately.

Joining through SSO follows the same admission rules as an invitation. It uses one
of the organization's unassigned paid seats, and the employee's work email must be
verified. If no seat is free, or the email is not verified yet, sign-in still
completes, but the employee does not join the organization. They join the next
time they sign in through SSO after an admin frees or adds a seat, or after they
verify their email. An employee with a pending invitation accepts that invitation
instead. Signing in with a social account whose email domain matches the verified
provider does not add anyone to the organization.

An employee's first SSO sign-in creates their Ryu account. That sign-in does not
ask for the sign-up page's date-of-birth check, because the organization's
identity provider vouches for the person, as with SCIM and admin provisioning.
The organization is responsible for making sure the people it provisions meet
Ryu's minimum age. This exemption applies only to a provider attached to an
organization whose domain is verified, and only when the new account's email is
in that verified domain or one of its subdomains. An identity provider that lets
anyone sign in, such as a public Google client, does not exempt accounts outside
the domain. Any other new account, including one created through Google, still
needs the age check first.

After a successful SSO callback that leaves the employee as a member, that
organization is also selected as the user's active organization for the new
session, so the employee lands in the workspace that owns the connection instead
of their personal organization.

An SSO connection only signs in addresses on its verified domain (or a subdomain
of it). Ryu refuses an SSO login, and creates no account, when the identity
provider asserts an email outside that domain. It also refuses an SSO login to an
existing Ryu account whose email is no longer on that domain, for example after
the account's email was changed. Those people sign in with another method, such as
an email code, or join through an invitation.

SSO does not automatically make someone an owner or admin. Do not use an email-domain match or an unreviewed IdP claim as a privileged role grant.

## Configure SCIM directory sync

SCIM is for the directory administrator, not ordinary Ryu users.

1. Open **Security → SCIM directory provisioning**.
2. Enter a connection name and choose **Generate SCIM token**.
3. Copy the base URL and bearer token into the directory application immediately. Ryu displays the secret only when it is created.
4. Configure the directory to use the SCIM 2.0 Users endpoint:

```text
https://<your-ryu-auth-host>/api/auth/scim/v2
```

5. Start with a small assigned group and verify create, update, deactivate, and remove behavior before assigning the full company directory.

Generating or rotating a SCIM token requires the built-in owner or admin role together with
`access.manage` and `members.manage`, because a SCIM token can remove any member who is not an
owner, admins included.

SCIM connections are scoped to the organization. Ryu stores the token hashed, displays the plaintext
only at creation or rotation, and gives the credential a one-year lifetime. Generating a new token
invalidates the previous one: once the new token is shown, every earlier token for that connection
is refused. The replacement is all-or-nothing: if it fails, the previous token keeps
working and no new token exists. Revoke a connection from the Security page when the directory
application is removed or its credential may have been exposed.

When the directory deactivates a user (`active: false`) or deletes them, and they have no other active
directory record in the organization, Ryu:

- signs them out of their current sessions, unless another organization's directory still lists them as active;
- removes their organization membership, including team memberships and custom role assignments;
- revokes the personal gateway keys and Fleet nodes the organization issued to them;
- records the removal in the organization audit log.

They can still sign in to their own personal Ryu account, but not to the organization.
Reactivating the user in the directory does not restore membership: they rejoin through SSO
sign-in (subject to the usual seat and email checks) or a new invitation.

SCIM never removes an organization owner: only an owner can remove another owner, so remove or
demote owners in Ryu. Revoking a SCIM connection retires the connection and signs out the users it
provisioned, but does not remove their memberships. A replacement connection takes over: when it
deactivates or deletes a user, Ryu removes their access even if the revoked connection still listed
them as active.

Employees who signed in through SSO before SCIM was set up are matched to their existing Ryu
account when their verified email is on one of the organization's verified SSO domains and they
are not an owner. This lets the directory deprovision them later. SCIM does not change these
accounts' name or email.

SCIM cannot create an account for a Ryu platform administrator's address, or change an account's
email to one; the request is refused with `403`. If the directory changes the email of an account
it created, the new address starts unverified, and any platform-administrator role on that account
is removed in the same write.

SCIM does not delete the company's source directory record. Keep the source directory as the system
of record, and use the Ryu audit log to investigate provisioning changes.

## Recovery and safe rollout

- Keep at least one owner/admin account with a working non-SSO recovery method until the first SSO login succeeds.
- Test an employee account and an intentionally unassigned account.
- Deactivate a test user in the directory, then verify that they no longer appear as an organization member and cannot open the organization.
- Rotate the SCIM token after testing if the setup was performed in a shared screen or ticket.
- Remove a provider only after the replacement connection has been tested.

## Audit export

Provisioning and sign-in changes appear in the organization audit log. To export it as CSV, see
[Audit log export](/docs/security/organization-admin-controls#audit-log-export).

For larger rollouts, start with one organization and one directory group.

Group-to-role mapping, private networking, dedicated deployment, data-region controls, and
contractual support are separately gated capabilities. They depend on the organization's Enterprise

</Callout>

## Availability

Visit [Ryu for enterprise](https://ryuhq.com/enterprise) for an overview of
identity, agent governance, and deployment choices. Use **Talk to sales** to
discuss your organization's rollout, required controls, and Enterprise access.

Enterprise identity controls are available on eligible plans and deployments. The server checks
the organization's Enterprise entitlement; it is never a browser credential. Availability and the
included capabilities vary by deployment.

The entitlement is checked on every SSO sign-in and every SCIM request, not only when
an admin changes the configuration. If it is suspended or expires, sign-in through the
organization's providers and directory requests with its SCIM tokens are refused right
away, before any account, session, or membership change. Providers and SCIM connections
stay configured, so access resumes when the entitlement is active again.
