## Which factors count

Ryu requires every account to hold a strong second factor: an **authenticator app** (TOTP) or
at least one **passkey**. Email codes do not count, because they rely on the same inbox as password
resets and magic links. A self-hosted Ryu service can turn the requirement off.

Ryu platform staff (service administrators and marketplace moderators) are always under this
requirement, whether or not the service has switched it on for everyone. Their accounts can see
and change data across every workspace. An unenrolled staff account is sent to **Setup 2FA** like
any other, and its requests, including the account administration endpoints, answer with
`mfa_enrollment_required` until a passkey or an authenticator app is in place.

For staff, holding a factor is not enough: the session itself must have used it. A staff session
that signed in with a passkey, an authenticator code, or a recovery code works normally. A staff
session that signed in any other way (an emailed code, a magic link, a social or single sign-on
login, or a password on a remembered device) gets `staff_session_factor_required` on its requests,
and cannot approve desktop or CLI sign-ins or add another factor, until it is confirmed. The website shows a **Confirm it's
you** prompt: confirm with your authenticator app, or sign out and sign in again with your passkey.
Desktop and CLI sign-ins approved from a confirmed staff session count as confirmed.

Staff changes also ask for a fresh authenticator or recovery code before they run, and one code
covers the next 30 minutes of staff work in that session. This applies to marketplace
moderation: approving or rejecting submissions, setting the Ryu-reviewed mark, choosing Staff
Picks, hiding or restoring reviews, triaging reports, and yanking or deprecating a listing as a
moderator. Resolving a seller-visible report on a listing your organization publishes is a seller
action and is not asked for this code, even for staff. Publishers who yank or deprecate their own
releases are not asked for it either; a staff account always acts as a moderator there, so it is.

## Organization two-factor policy

An organization can also [require two-factor authentication](/docs/security/organization-admin-controls#access-policy)
for its members. That page owns the organization rules: either an authenticator app or a passkey
satisfies the policy, and a session that signed in without its factor gets `org_mfa_session_required`
until it is confirmed.

While the requirement is on and an account has neither, signing in still works, but the website
sends you to **Settings → Setup 2FA** before any account page opens, and account requests answer
with a `403` response whose `code` is `mfa_enrollment_required`. On that page you can:

- **Add a passkey.** This works for passwordless accounts (Google, GitHub, or magic-link sign-in)
  too. Ryu first asks you to verify your identity, the same check it uses for other security
  changes.
- **Set up an authenticator app.** This needs an account password. A passwordless account can add a
  passkey first, then set a password in **Profile → Account** and add an authenticator app.

Once either is in place, you go back to the page you were opening.

Until the account is enrolled, Ryu also refuses to issue new credentials for it: personal API keys,
desktop and CLI sign-in approvals, tokens for your own nodes, OAuth and MCP client authorizations,
new workspaces, and workspace invitations. These answer with the same `mfa_enrollment_required`
code. Approving a desktop or CLI sign-in on the website takes you to **Setup 2FA** and then back to
the approval. API keys created before the requirement was turned on keep working with the scopes
they were given.

The apps check the same requirement after sign-in. Desktop, the browser app, Mobile, the CLI
workspace, and the browser extension's popup and New Tab show a **Set up two-factor authentication**
screen in place of the workspace. It opens **Settings → Setup 2FA** on the website and offers
**Check again**; the workspace opens as soon as a passkey or authenticator app is in place. Sign in
to the website with the same account if it asks. When an app cannot reach Ryu to check, it keeps
the workspace open and checks again later. A one-shot `ryu-cli` command that Ryu refuses for this
reason prints the setup page address and exits with status 1.

Accounts still on the waitlist do not need a second factor. When an account is approved, setting
one up is the first onboarding step: the approval sends you to **Setup 2FA** before the dashboard
opens.

While the requirement is on, you cannot remove your last second factor. Disabling the
authenticator app is refused while you have no passkey, and deleting your only passkey is refused
while the authenticator app is off; both answer with `mfa_last_factor`. Add the replacement first,
then remove the old one.

## Enable two-factor authentication

When two-factor authentication is off, the signed-in website can show a reminder on the Console
and Account Settings pages. **Enable 2FA** opens setup. Dismissing the reminder hides it on both pages
for that account in the current browser; enabling 2FA removes the reminder automatically. The prompt
does not change your account's security settings until you complete setup.

When a billing action asks for fresh identity verification, use the current
authenticator code from your enrolled two-factor account. Email and backup codes
cannot satisfy that billing check. A refused code does not authorize the action;
retry explicitly after checking the code. Verification applies to the current
signed-in session, so another device must complete its own check.
