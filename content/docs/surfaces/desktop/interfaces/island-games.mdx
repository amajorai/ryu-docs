These are 3D game interfaces. See [Games](/docs/surfaces/desktop/interfaces/games) for how every game shows what your agents are doing.

## Path

Your conversations as a cosy 3D island, seen from above at an angle. A narrow dirt trail winds up
a terraced hill to a summit flag, one stepping-stone pad per conversation, grouped into units: your
projects, then teams, then each agent's other conversations, then those **Without an agent**. Each
row of the switchback is a terrace higher than the last, with steps where the trail climbs. A
chunky wooden signpost starts each unit. A pad shows where its conversation stands: finished ones
glow gold with a little check flag, the ones waiting on you have a bouncing **!**, and those still
waiting on an agent are grey, mossy stones; the legend explains the colours.

Each agent is a hiker standing by the stone of the conversation it is on. A working hiker holds a
tool for its step (a hammer to edit, a wrench to run, a book to read, a lens to search, a quill to
write, a clipboard to plan), with the plan's pips on its stone and its progress, time and next step
on a small board. A hiker asking you walks down to the trailhead at the front of the island and
waits in the queue there, and a hiker with an answer sets a parcel on the trailhead's tray. A
failed run shows an alarm at the stone, and a hiker at a usage limit rests beside its stone with
**Resting till 15:10**. While agents are on the trail, the signposts step aside and the stones keep
only their marks, so the hikers' plates have room; **New thread** starts a conversation.

The top bar keeps your streak (days in a row on which a conversation finished with an answer), your
XP (every message) and the **Daily goal** (conversations you replied to today); select one to see
its card. On a phone, the summary of what needs you takes the streak's place. Select a stone or a
hiker to open its conversation in a panel beside the island (a hiker asking you opens its ask card),
a signpost's **+** to start one in that unit, or **New thread** to start one anywhere. `Tab` and the
arrow keys move between stones, hikers and signposts; drag to pan, and use the zoom buttons to zoom
in, out or back to the whole island. `Esc` closes the conversation. Where 3D is unavailable, the
path is shown as a list of units and stops.

In **Interface settings**, **Streak and goal** shows or hides the streak, XP, and daily goal in the
top bar, along with the 3D games' **Overlay density** and **Work bubbles**.

## Kitchen

Your agents as a kitchen team, in a cosy 3D restaurant kitchen on a little island, seen from above
at an angle. Each agent is a chef in a white hat at their own station (a stove, a chopping board or a
grill), and conversations are paper order tickets hanging on the rail across the top. A chef who is
working cooks with a ladle, pan or knife, and their ticket on the rail runs a timer against their
usual run time; a reply is a plated dish on the pass, ready to serve, and its chef waves under a
pulsing **!**; a failed run is a burnt order with smoke over the station and a slumped chef. Today's
tally (served today, need you, cooking, burnt) sits in the bottom left corner and the service clock in the
bottom right.

The chefs act out what your agents are doing. A working chef holds the tool for its step (a knife to
edit, a pan to run, the order book to read, a ladle to think), with the plan's pips on the counter
and its words on a board beside it. A chef asking you carries its ticket round to the front of the
pass and queues there facing you, and the pass bell rings once as it arrives. A chef with an answer
sets a plated dish on the pass and goes back. A failed run shows an alarm behind the counter, and a
usage limit reads **86'd till 15:10**. The rail puts the tickets that need you first, each with a
patience bar that drains while it waits.

Serve a ticket or a dish to open its conversation in a sheet beside the kitchen. Select a chef's name
plate to see their open and served tickets, and choose **New order** to start a conversation with a
chef you pick. The arrow keys move along the rail and between the chefs and dishes in the kitchen,
drag pans and the zoom buttons or `+` and `-` zoom, and `Esc` steps back out. Opening a chef's sheet
glides the camera to their station. Light themes are a
sunny day and dark themes night, with the windows lit. The kitchen is drawn with WebGL; without it,
the line lists your chefs as buttons that open the same panels.

In **Interface settings**, **Service clock** shows or hides the clock in the corner, along with the 3D games' **Overlay density** and **Work bubbles**.

## Market

Your workspace as a cosy 3D market square on a little island, seen from above at an angle. Each
project is a shop with a striped awning and its name painted on its signboard, each team a striped market stall,
and agents in neither share the **General store**. The shops stand in two rows around a cobbled
square with a fountain in the middle, bunting, lamps, benches and crates of goods. Your agents are the
shopkeepers, standing out front: one who is working holds a basket, one with a reply waiting for you
shows a **!**, and one whose run failed slumps. The asks that need you queue at the
fountain in front of your own little stall, where answers are set down as wrapped parcels, and the
customers waiting at a shop are its upcoming runs.

A shopkeeper whose run failed pulls the shutter half
down, with the reason on a sign beside it, and one that hit a usage limit chalks **Back 15:10** on
the shop's board.

The title plate and counters show how many conversations wait on you, how many shops are busy and
how many orders are open, and **Directory** lists every shop. Select the plate by a shop's sign to open its sheet
beside the square: its shopkeepers, an order board of conversations pinned under what they need, and
a box to place a new order with the shopkeeper you pick, filed under that shop's project or team.
Select a shopkeeper's name plate to address the order to them straight away. **Serve** opens an
order's conversation in the sheet, with **Back** to its shop. `Tab` and the arrow keys move between
signs and shopkeepers, drag pans and the zoom buttons or `+` and `-` zoom, and `Esc` steps back out:
first the open order, then the shop.

Light themes are a sunny day and dark themes a starry night with
lit windows, lamps and bunting lights. Where 3D is unavailable, the square is shown as a list of
shops and their shopkeepers.

In **Interface settings**, **Awnings** paints the shops' awnings in mixed colors, warm harvest
tones, or seaside blues, and **Bunting** shows or hides the pennant strings over the square, along
with the 3D games' **Overlay density** and **Work bubbles**.

## Workers

Your workspace as a cosy 3D open-plan office on a little island, seen from above at an angle, with
big windows, a bookshelf, potted plants, a rug and a coffee corner. Every agent sits at its own desk
before a monitor whose screen glows in the colour of what it is doing right now: green while it
works, amber when a reply waits on you, red after a failed run, and a dim screensaver when it is
idle. The desks face you, so each screen shows over its worker's shoulder, with the current
conversation's title on a small plate just above it and the worker's name on their chair. A worker
who is busy faces their screen; one waiting on you turns round, and one whose run failed slumps.
The counters show how many workers need you, are working or failed, and the legend explains the
screen colours. The office takes your team's (or single project's) name when every worker
belongs to it.

The workers act out what your agents are doing. The screen glows amber while its worker asks you,
green while it works, red after a failed run, grey after an interrupted one, and shows the
screensaver otherwise; its plate says the step (**Editing snapshot.ts**) or why the run stopped,
and the plan's pips run as a bar under the monitor. A worker asking you carries its laptop to your
office door and waits in the queue there, and one with an answer drops a folder in your inbox tray
by the door. A worker at a usage limit, or with nothing to do, waits in the coffee corner, its mug
saying when it is back (**Back 15:10**).

Select a screen's plate to open that conversation in a sheet beside the office. **Back** shows the
worker's desk: what they do, all their tasks, and a box to give them a new one. Select a worker's
name plate to open their desk straight away, or **New task** to start a task with any worker. `Tab`
and the arrow keys move between workers and screens, drag pans and the zoom buttons or `+` and `-`
zoom, and `Esc` steps back out: first to the worker, then out of the sheet. Light themes are a sunny
day and dark themes evening, lit by the screens and desk lamps. Where 3D is unavailable, the office
is shown as a list of workers and their screens.

In **Interface settings**, **Screen plates** chooses what each monitor's plate says: what the worker
is doing, or the conversation's title. The 3D games' **Overlay density** and **Work bubbles** apply
too.

## Arcade

A cosy 3D arcade hall on a little island, seen from above at an angle, its walls trimmed with neon
that glows at night. Every agent is an arcade cabinet in its own colour, standing in rows across the
floor with every screen turned toward you, with a lit marquee, a big glowing screen, a joystick and
buttons, and its player standing beside it. More than six agents fill a second row behind the
first, and on a phone the rows are two cabinets wide. The screen's three reels are the agent's
latest conversations, in bold pixel symbols: a bell for your turn, a seven for working, a BAR plate
for waiting, cherries for done, and a flame for a failed run.

A working cabinet's screen glows, a reply waiting on you lights it gold while the player turns round under a
pulsing **!**, and a failed run tilts the machine with a red lamp while its player slumps. The ends of
the rows hold a claw machine and a prize counter, whose **Collect** sign opens the reply that has
waited longest. Your projects and teams are chips under the title; press one to narrow the hall to
its machines. The credits, running
and jackpot meters sit in the bottom left corner, and the paytable in the bottom right explains the
reel symbols.

Select a player's name to open their machine in a sheet beside the hall. There, cash a ticket or a
reel to open its conversation at the redemption counter, and pull the lever, or **Spin**, to open a
play slip that starts a conversation with that agent. **Collect** opens the reply that has waited
longest, and **New game** starts a conversation without picking an agent. `Tab` and the arrow keys
move between players; drag to pan, and use the zoom buttons or `+` and `-` to zoom. Opening a machine
glides the camera to it. `Esc` steps back,
and from a filtered hall shows every machine again. The hall is drawn with WebGL; without it, the
machines stand in an aisle with the same controls.

## Farm

Your workspace as a cosy 3D farm on a little island, seen from above at an angle. Each agent tends a
raised bed and stands beside it as a villager; projects fence their agents' beds into fields, and the
barn, silo and pond sit in the yard. Every conversation is a crop whose growth shows where it stands:
a sprout with a water drop while the agent has yet to answer, a leafy plant while it works, ripe fruit
when it is your turn, and a withered brown plant when a run failed. Each farmer shows their bed's most
urgent crop: waving under a pulsing **!** when something is ripe, working with a tool while crops
grow, and slumped with a red mark when a run withered. The counters in the corner show how many
farmers failed, need you or are working, and the coins count one per message.

The farmers act out what your agents are doing. A working farmer's crop grows with its plan, the
pips a row of leaves behind the bed and the step's words on a board at its far end. A farmer asking
you carries a letter to the farmhouse porch by the gate and queues at the mailbox facing you, each
with an hourglass on a fence post. A farmer with an answer sets a basket of its crop on the porch
and walks back, and a reply left unread over-ripens while it waits. A failed run puts a scarecrow
with a crow and a red lantern in the bed, and a farmer at a usage limit sits on a bench by the
sundial, **Back 15:10**.

Click a crop to open its conversation beside the farm. Select a farmer's name (or a plot's sign) to
open that plot: its crops and a **Plant a seed** composer that starts a new conversation with that
plot's agent. The **Barn** lists every crop by status. `Tab` and the arrow keys move between the
farmers, plots and the barn; drag to pan, and use the zoom buttons or `+` and `-` to zoom. Opening a
plot or the barn glides the camera to it. Light
themes are a sunny day and dark themes a starry night with the lamps lit. The farm is drawn with
WebGL; without it, the same fields, plots and crops are listed.

In **Interface settings**, **Coins** shows or hides the coins counter, along with the 3D games' **Overlay density** and **Work bubbles**.

## Warehouse

The fulfillment side of your agents, as a cosy 3D toon warehouse on a little island, seen from above at
an angle. Six aisles of blue and orange shelving hold the stock, a conveyor runs to the packing table,
and a truck is backed up to the dock. Every agent is a picker in a cap working an aisle: walking it
with a basket while it works, waiting at the aisle's end when it is idle, waving under a **!** when a
reply waits on you. Every conversation is an order box moving through the building: on a pallet at
the receiving door, picked in the aisles, riding the conveyor to be packed, staged with a label on the
dock when the agent has replied, loaded on the truck when done, or flagged in the returns cage when a
run failed.

The shift's numbers sit along the bottom: each one opens the orders it counts. **Pause**
stops the floor's motion. Light themes are the day shift and dark themes the night shift, under the
work lights.

Each picker walks to the shelves its current step needs. A picker asking you carries a clipboard to
the dispatch desk by the outbound dock and waits in the queue there; select it to open the ask card.
Answers are set down on the desk as labelled parcels, which ship once you have read them. A picker
whose message has not been picked up yet waits at intake, and one that hit a usage limit waits by its
forklift, with **Back 15:10** on its board.

The orders sit on a clipboard beside the floor (under it on narrow windows), with **All**,
**Unfulfilled**, **Ready to ship**, and **Exceptions** tabs. Select an order for its details and
progress, and its box is marked on the floor; choose **Fulfil** to open its conversation, and `Esc`
closes it. Select a picker's name plate to see only their orders, or a zone's plate (receiving,
packing, the dock, the cage, the truck) to open the tab that lists its orders. **Create order** picks
who fulfils it and starts a conversation with what you describe. `Tab` and the arrow keys move
between pickers and zones, and drag pans and the zoom buttons or `+` and `-` zoom. Where 3D is
unavailable, the floor is shown as lists of pickers and orders.

In **Interface settings**, **Shift** follows your color theme or holds a day or night shift, and
**Paused** keeps the floor still (the **Pause** button saves its choice there), along with the 3D
games' **Overlay density** and **Work bubbles**. With **Work bubbles** off, the shelf labels that
name what each picker is working on are hidden too.
