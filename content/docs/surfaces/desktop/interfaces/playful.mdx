These interfaces are listed with the [workspace layouts](/docs/surfaces/desktop/interfaces/workspace#more-interfaces). This page describes each one.

## Card Table

Up to six agents sit around a felt table in a cosy 3D card room, seen from above at an angle, each
with a name plate over their head showing their conversation count; you sit at the near side with your hand. Drag to pan, and use
the zoom buttons to zoom; where 3D is unavailable, the table is shown flat. The chairs go to the agents
that most need attention: those asking you first, then stopped, limited, and working ones. Everyone else waits on the **rail**, a panel in the corner on wide windows
or a pill at the top right, which also lists conversations that have no agent under **Off the
table**.

- The chips stacked on the felt in front of a seat count its conversations, and face-down cards
  mean the agent holds the action: it is working, or you are waiting on it. A working agent also
  shows a small action timer over its name plate.
- The **pot** in the middle counts the runs going on right now. Select it to list them.
- Conversations that need you are dealt to you as a hand of cards: failed runs first, then
  stopped runs, then asks. Each card shows its agent's initial as the rank, its status suit, the
  conversation's title, a colored band along its top edge and a status mark in its corner. When
  more than fit, the rest are held back; select **held** next to the hand to list them.
- On a phone the hand is tucked into a compact fan along the bottom edge, so the table stays in
  view above it. Tap the fan to open it, then tap a card to play it; tapping the table tucks the
  hand again.
- Play a card to open its conversation in a drawer beside the table, which stays in view.
- An agent asking you slides a face-up card across the felt to your seat, where it waits in the
  queue; select it to open the ask card. A failed run lies as a folded card beside the chair, with
  its reason next to it. A player who hit a usage limit is **sitting out**, with the time it can
  play again (**Sitting out, back 15:10**), and the dealer button by the pot names the next
  scheduled deal.
- Select a seat for that agent's conversations and **Deal a thread**, which starts a new one with
  the agent. **Deal a thread** at the top right, or an open seat, starts one without picking an agent.

| Action | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Move along your hand | `←` / `→` (or `↑` / `↓`); `Home` and `End` jump to the ends |
| Play the selected card | `Enter` or `Space` |
| Close the open conversation, or tuck an open hand on a phone | `Esc` |

### Table or Cards

Switch between **Table** and **Cards** at the top right. **Cards** puts the seats and your hand away
and lays your conversations out as decks on the felt: each agent is a deck of its conversations,
most urgent on top and face up, and conversations with no agent form an **Unassigned** deck.

- Select a deck to fan its cards out face up while the other decks move aside into a row along the
  bottom. A deck with more cards than fit ends on a face-down card that lists the rest.
- Play a card to open its conversation in the drawer. A deck's **Deal** starts a new conversation
  with that agent.
- **All decks**, `Esc`, or a click on the bare felt gathers the deck back.

The choice lasts until you leave the interface; the Card Table opens on the table again next time.

| Action (Cards) | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Choose a deck | Arrow keys (`↑` / `↓` move a row); `Home` and `End` jump to the first and last |
| Fan the selected deck out | `Enter` or `Space` |
| Move along the fanned cards | `←` / `→` (or `↑` / `↓`); `Home` and `End` jump to the ends |
| Play the selected card | `Enter` or `Space` |
| Close the open conversation, then gather the deck | `Esc` |

In **Interface settings**, **Table** picks the layout it opens in (switching **Table** and
**Cards** saves your choice there), **Felt** dresses the table in classic green, blue, or red, and
**Card backs** picks classic, red, or black backs, along with the 3D games' **Overlay density** and
**Work bubbles**.

## Ticker

Your agents as a stock market. Each agent is listed as a ticker with a short symbol. Its **price** is
the number of messages across its conversations, and its **change** is how far that price has
moved since yesterday's close: the number of messages in its conversations that were active today.
A conversation counts on the day it was last active, so the change includes that conversation's
earlier messages too.

- The header shows the whole workspace as an index, how many tickers had a conversation active
  today and how many stayed quiet, and how many agents are working. A tape scrolls the newest
  messages.
- The watchlist lists every ticker with a sparkline of its recent activity within the chart's
  range. Sort it by **Symbol**, **Price**, or **Change**.
- The selected agent's page charts its price over **1W**, **2W** (the default), or **1M**, with its
  key statistics (**Active today**, the messages in its conversations active today, then its
  conversations, how many are working, and how many need attention) and its conversations. **Message** starts a new conversation with the agent.

| Action | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Move through the watchlist | `↑` / `↓` or `J` / `K`; `Home` and `End` jump to the ends |
| Open the agent's most pressing conversation | `Enter` |
| Message (start a conversation with the agent) | `T` |
| Read the chart day by day (on the chart) | `←` / `→`; `Home` and `End` jump to the ends |
| Close the open conversation | `Esc` |

## Aquarium

A cosy 3D aquarium, seen from above at an angle: a big glass tank on a wooden cabinet in a room,
with a board floor, a rug, two walls and a window. Inside are sand, rocks, coral, swaying kelp and
starfish, with ripples on the surface. Each agent swims there as its own creature (a reef fish, an angelfish,
a jellyfish, a sea turtle, or a manta ray) in its own colors. The tank mixes them: every species
appears once before any appears twice. Each creature keeps to its own spot in the tank: working
agents swim high and brisk, trailing bubbles, and idle ones drift low over the reef. Every creature
has a name plate with a badge: a red mark when a conversation needs attention, a pulsing **!** when
one waits on you, and dots while it works.

At night the reef glows: coral tips, kelp, jellyfish and
spots on the creatures. The title plate sums up what needs you, the counters in the corner show how
many agents need you, are working or failed, and the legend explains the marks.

The creatures act out what your agents are doing. A creature asking you swims to the front glass
and taps there in the queue along the right-hand side, and one with an answer drops a shell in the
chest by the glass. A stopped creature sinks to the sand by its cave, one at a usage limit rests in
its clam (**In the clam till 15:10**), and one waiting on its run hovers at the feeder under the
surface. On the sand under each creature's home sit its plan's pips as coral beads, its next step,
alarms, unread shells and suggestion notes; the list of agents in the tank and the ask queue reach
them from the keyboard.

- Select a creature or its name plate, or `Tab` to it and press `Enter`, to open its card beside the
  tank: its species and status, its description, how many conversations it has, how many need you,
  and how many are live, its conversations grouped by status, and a box to message it. A creature
  holds still while the pointer rests on its plate and while its card is open, and the camera
  glides to it with a gentle zoom, so the rest of the tank stays in view.
- Opening a conversation shows it in the same panel, with a button back to the creature.
  Selecting the sand or the cabinet puts the card away; an open conversation stays.
- When some conversations have no agent, a button in the corner lists them. **New thread** in the
  header starts a conversation without picking an agent.

| Action | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Move between creatures (those that need you first, then working ones) | `Tab`, then the arrow keys; `Home` and `End` jump to the ends |
| Open the creature's card | `Enter` |
| Back from a conversation to the card, then close the card | `Esc` |
| Pan and zoom | Drag the scene; the zoom buttons, `+` and `-` |

The tank is drawn with WebGL. If this device does not support it, the interface says so and lists
your agents as buttons instead; selecting one opens the same card. When your system asks for
reduced motion, the creatures hold still and the kelp stops swaying; panning and zooming still work.

In **Interface settings**, **Wallpaper** papers the room in cream, sage, or blue, along with the 3D games' **Overlay density** and **Work bubbles**.

## Planets

A small, cosy solar system of your work in 3D. You are the star at the center, a friendly smiling
sun. Each agent is a little toon planet on its own orbit, painted in the agent's own color with
soft bands, and on top of it a tiny world: a grassy cap with a cottage, a couple of trees, and the
agent's villager, who works, waves when a conversation waits on you, or slumps when one needs
attention. Each of its conversations is a moon in its status color: **Needs attention**, **Your
turn**, **Working**, **Waiting on agent**, or **Not started**. Conversations with no agent orbit the
star itself. The system sits in deep space in dark themes, with the cottages' windows lit, and in a
lighter dusk sky in light themes.

- Agents with more conversations orbit closer to the star and are drawn larger. Each agent keeps
  its own kind of world (rocky, gas giant, ice, ocean, or ringed), which sets its bands and whether
  it wears a ring, and a working planet's atmosphere pulses. When agents belong to teams, their
  orbits are tinted by team, and the legend names each color.
- A planet shows up to eight moons, most urgent first. Its name plate shows how many conversations
  are waiting on you and, when there are more than eight, how many are not drawn; its panel lists
  them all.
- The card in the top-left corner counts your agents, how many are working, and how many
  conversations are waiting on you, and lists every body from the star outward. On smaller windows,
  and while a conversation is open, the list folds away behind **List**.
- On a phone the planets hold still in a column under the star, seen from above, so each little
  world is big enough to read. The card becomes a sheet along the bottom with the counts; open it
  for the list of bodies and the legend.
- The buttons in the top-right corner zoom in and out, return to the **Overview**, and start a
  **New thread**. On a phone, pinch to zoom instead.

Select a planet, its name plate, or its row in the list, and the camera flies to it while its
panel opens: the agent's status, kind of world, orbit, description, and teams, how many moons it
has, how many are waiting on you, and how many are working, its conversations grouped by status,
and a box to message it. Once the camera arrives, the planet's moons show their titles. Select a
moon to open its conversation in the same panel, with a button back to the planet.

Select the star (**You**) for what is waiting on you across the whole system, the conversations
no agent is on, and a box to start a conversation with any agent.

| Action | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Look around / zoom (on the scene) | Drag / scroll |
| Visit the previous or next planet (on the scene) | `←` / `→` |
| Visit the first planet from the overview (on the scene) | `Enter` |
| Zoom in / out (on the scene) | `+` / `-` |
| Back to the overview (on the scene) | `0` |
| Move through the list | `↑` / `↓`; `Home` and `End` jump to the ends |
| Visit the selected body in the list | `Enter` |
| Back from a conversation to the planet, then back to the overview | `Esc` |

While agents are asking you or working, a small space station with four berths sits in the
foreground. An agent asking you something launches a probe from its planet that docks in a berth,
with a ring light that drains as it waits; select the probe's plate to open its ask card. A ready
answer waits as a cargo capsule in the station's bay, blinking while unread. At the planet, a
failed run shows a red storm band, an interrupted one a dark moon, a usage limit an eclipse with
the time it can go again (**Back 15:10**), and a working agent a satellite on its orbit. The moon
of the conversation the agent is on comes first, with an arc of pips for its progress, and the
planets hold their orbits so the plates stay readable. The card's subtitle becomes the summary of
what needs you, and choosing an asking agent's row in its list opens its ask card.

Click or tab into the scene first so it has keyboard focus. Dragging or scrolling interrupts a
camera flight. The solar system is drawn with WebGL. If this device does not support it, the
interface says so; the list in the top-left card still opens each planet's panel and its
conversations. When your system asks for reduced motion, the orbits and spins stop and the camera
cuts straight to each new view.

In **Interface settings**, **Orbit speed** runs the orbits and moons at normal or slow speed, or
holds them still, and **Moon labels** turned off shows a moon's name only on hover or focus, along
with the 3D games' **Overlay density** and **Work bubbles**.
