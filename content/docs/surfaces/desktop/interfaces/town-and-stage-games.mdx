These are 3D game interfaces. See [Games](/docs/surfaces/desktop/interfaces/games) for how every game shows what your agents are doing.

## Town

Your agents as a small city, with you as its mayor. Projects and teams are districts, and agents in
neither live in Old Town. Every agent is a cosy cottage that grows bigger as its conversations add
up. A crane works over an agent while it works, and a red beacon smokes over a failed run. Each
agent also walks the town as a little villager: hammering at their building while they work,
slumped by the door after a failed run, and strolling round their block otherwise. A villager
asking you walks to the steps of City Hall and queues down the plaza, and asks with no villager to
carry them wait there as petition letters. A villager with an answer brings a crate to the inbox
beside the steps. An agent at a usage limit hangs **Closed till 15:10** on its door, and unread
answers and saved suggestions wait at its building.

- The town fills the window as a cosy island in the sea, with paved stone lanes, lush lawns, parks,
  ponds and a storybook City Hall with a clock tower. Light themes are a sunny day and dark themes a
  starry night with the lamps and windows lit. Its name, date and speed sit top left, and its
  population, happiness, construction and funds sit in the bottom left corner.
- Replies and failures are petitions at **City Hall**. The **City Hall** plate at the top shows
  how many asks wait on you and opens City Hall in a sheet beside the town, with the petitions, upcoming
  runs, a map, and the districts. **Hear petition** opens one, and **Hear next petition** opens the
  first.
- Click a building to focus the camera on it and open its card, where **Commission work** starts a
  conversation with that agent.
- The zoom buttons, **Fit the town to the view**, the **Data view**, and the speed control (pause,
  normal, fast) sit around the map.

The town is drawn with WebGL. Without it, every building is listed, and each one opens the same
card.

In **Interface settings**, **Speed** (automatic, normal, fast, or paused) and **Data view**
(districts, activity, or what needs you) set how the town opens; changing either from the controls
on the map keeps your choice. Automatic runs at normal speed, or starts paused when you prefer
reduced motion; picking Normal runs at normal speed either way. The 3D games' **Overlay density** and **Work bubbles** apply too.

## Rides

A ride-hailing app over your agents, set in a cosy 3D toon city on a little island, seen from above at
an angle: soft roads with dashed avenues between blocks of rounded houses and little towers, a river
under its bridges, and a park with a pond. The city shows only the streets your trips use, so it stays
compact. Agents are drivers, each in a small rounded car of their own colour. A driver working on a
conversation drives a route to its destination pin, with an estimated arrival from the agent's
typical reply time, then parks at the pin once it replies. A trip's word is the same on its pin, its row and the
open trip. A driver asking you
something pulls up at the kerb by your pin and waits in the queue there. You are the orange pin
every trip starts from. Light themes are a sunny day and dark themes night, with lit windows,
street lamps and headlights.

The trip panel floats over the city's left side (a sheet along the bottom on windows narrower than
about 1000 pixels, so the city keeps its width), and the
counters show trips under way, arrived trips, and free drivers. The panel has **Now** (trips under
way), **Arrived**, and **Trips** (the history). The first trip under way is selected when you
arrive: its route stands out in the city, and the bottom of the panel shows the driver, the route from
you to the conversation, the estimate, and **Open trip**. Type **Where to?**, pick a driver, and press
`Enter` to book a trip, which starts a conversation with that agent. Select a pin's plate to show its
trip and again to open it, a car's plate to show its trip, or an available driver's car to pick them.

Open a trip to see the conversation in the sheet. `Tab` and the arrow keys move between cars and pins,
drag pans and the zoom buttons or `+` and `-` zoom, and `Esc` closes the open trip. Where 3D is
unavailable, the cars and pins are shown as lists.

## Passport

Your workspace as a passport booklet lying open on a desk, a 3D toon diorama seen from above at an
angle. Turning its pages is how you move through it: a wide window shows a two-page spread, a phone
one page at a time. The identity page is the passport's summary: you as the holder, the totals of
countries, stamps and travel days, and the same summary line as the title plate. Each agent has a
visa page with its photo (the agent's figure standing in the photo frame), its name, its emblem and
where it stands. Each conversation is an entry stamp on its agent's page, marked with where it stands
from **APPLIED** to **ENTRY DENIED** in its country's ink. Light themes are a sunny desk and dark
themes an evening one.

Every visa page has an index tab poking out round the booklet's edge, and the agent stands on its
tab whichever page is open, stamping while it works. An agent asking you sends a visa slip to the
customs desk at the booklet's front edge, where it waits in the queue for your signature under a desk
lamp that burns in the colour of the oldest wait; select the agent to open its ask card. Where no asks
reach the desk, the customs desk reads **No visas waiting**. Alarms, a usage limit (**Border closed till
15:10**, shortened to **Opens 15:10** on a phone), an answer that is ready and suggestions are
notes in the margin beside the agent, never on the printed page: off the booklet's side for a side
tab, and in a clip strip between the back row and the booklet for a back tab. A failed run also gets
a red **DENIED** stamp, and a delivered answer is set down on the customs desk as a stamped page.

The page controls at the bottom turn the pages, and their tabs turn straight to the identity page or
an agent's visa pages (on a phone they scroll sideways under the arrows). `Page Up` and `Page Down`
turn pages, `Home` and `End` jump to the cover and the last page, and `←` and `→` turn pages while the
page controls have focus. **Booklet** opens the open page up close in a wide sheet (the whole screen
on a phone), and **Map of travels** lists the same entries plainly. Open a stamp to file the
conversation as an immigration record; `Esc` files it away, then closes the sheet. Select an agent,
or **Apply for a visa** in the booklet or the map, to start a new conversation with that agent; the
booklet turns to its visa page.

In the desk, `Tab` and the arrow keys move between agents, and drag
pans and the zoom buttons or `+` and `-` zoom. Where 3D is unavailable, the countries are shown as a
list.

In **Interface settings**, **Cover** binds the booklet in navy, burgundy, green, or black, along
with the 3D games' **Overlay density** and **Work bubbles**.

## Lobby

A game lobby on a cosy 3D plaza, seen from above at an angle. **Lobby** puts up to four agents
on a lit podium in the middle as your squad (the most urgent first; your picks lead among agents
doing the same thing), each with a level plaque at their feet; select one
to choose it (a glow on the podium follows), or an **Open slot** to add an agent. Around the podium stand
the quest board (it opens your **Daily quests**), a glowing portal (it starts a conversation with
the chosen agent) and the board of recent matches with your squad. Select **Play** to write a
one-line brief, then **Drop in** (or `Enter`) to start a conversation; `Esc` cancels. `Tab` and the
arrow keys move between everything on the plaza; drag to pan, and use the zoom buttons to zoom.
Where 3D is unavailable, the plaza is shown as a list. The menu tabs and your level sit along the
top; the other tabs are:

- **Quests**: **Daily quests** from what is waiting on you (claiming one opens it), **Weekly
  quests** from finished conversations per team and project, and **Milestones** from your messages.
- **Squad**: the roster. Picking an agent puts it in the squad.
- **News**: the latest activity as stories you page through.

Your level comes from messages: 100 XP each, and a level every 1,000 XP. Anything you open shows the
conversation in a panel beside the plaza; `Esc` closes it.

## Quiz Show

A quiz show in a cosy 3D TV studio, seen from above at an angle. The board is a big wall of glowing
tiles facing you at the back of the set, under a curved backdrop lined with marquee bulbs: always
six categories (projects and teams, then the busiest agents) on lit header panels, by five values,
100 at the top to 500 at the bottom. A category's conversations fill its column from the bottom, so
the one that needs you most is worth 500; the cheaper slots above read as already played. Tiles that
need you glow brightest, played ones are dimmed, and each live tile carries a small badge for its
state. Pick a tile to reveal its clue, the latest message; **Answer** opens the conversation in the
answer sheet beside the studio, and **Back to board** (or `Esc`) returns. An answer waiting to be
read flips its tile to the answer's headline.

Your agents stand behind podiums in front of the board (the most urgent four), each podium's
screen saying in a word what its contestant is doing, with the host (you) at a lectern marked **Host** beside them and the audience in theatre rows along the front.
A contestant's current step shows in the gap beside its podium, and a stopped run's alarm hangs
under its podium's screen.
A contestant asking you something buzzes in and walks to the queue beside the lectern; select it
to open its ask card. Choose a contestant's name to ask them the **Final question**, which starts a new conversation with
what you type. `Tab` and the arrow keys move between the tiles' badges and the contestants; drag to
pan, and use the zoom buttons to zoom. Where 3D is unavailable, the board is shown flat, and there
the arrow keys move between tiles and `Home`, `End`, `Page Up`, and `Page Down` jump.

## Seasonal

A seasonal board as a cosy 3D island, seen from above at an angle, with your agents as villagers
busy by their conversations. **Advent** is a snowy village square round a big advent house: its 24
doors and windows face you on its two front walls, numbered in reading order across both walls with
painted plaques, and opened ones are lit from inside with the shutters folded back. **Halloween** is
a pumpkin patch at night: each conversation is a carved jack-o'-lantern glowing from inside, and
the places still empty are plain grey-green pumpkins. **Lunar New Year** is a lantern festival
street hung with 24 numbered lanterns, blazing brightest where they need you. The season switcher at the top right
changes the board. By default it follows the date: Halloween in October, Advent from November 20
through December, Lunar New Year from January 15 through February, and Advent the rest of the year.

Each place is a conversation, the most urgent at the lowest numbers, and today's number is marked
in the board's own month. A conversation where an agent is waiting on your answer carries a red
number plate and a glow; one an agent is working on shows dots; a stopped run is frosted shut (a
cobweb over the pumpkin, a lantern gone dark) with a snowflake mark; the legend explains the marks.
Each door's words say what its agent is doing. An agent asking you carries a letter to the board's
mailbox (the cauldron at the patch's gate, the post box at the street's end), and a usage limit
reads in the season's words, such as **Snowed in, back 15:10** or **Stall shut, back 15:10**. Select a villager to open
that agent's conversation.

Open a door to reveal a gift card, then choose **Open conversation** to hang the conversation beside the board. A door stays shut while its
conversation has an unread answer and opens once you have read it. An empty pumpkin or lantern opens a box
to start a conversation, as does the add button at the top right on every board (the advent house
keeps its empty doors plain). Past the 24th, conversations go to **More gifts**. **All doors** at the bottom right
lists every conversation on the board. `Tab` and the arrow keys move between the numbers and
`Enter` opens one; drag to pan, and use the zoom buttons to zoom. Where 3D is unavailable, the
board is shown as a plain grid of numbered doors.

Each season dresses the shared agent cues its own way. The wait beside a queued ask is a candle
whose wax melts down as the ask's time runs out; its flame turns the wait's colour and flickers
only when the ask is about to expire (it holds still when your system asks for reduced motion). A
reply set down at your spot is a wrapped gift (a treat bag in the patch) tagged with the answer's
headline, such as **Edited 3 files**, shortened to 32 characters; there is no tag when the answer
has no headline or conversation details are redacted, and none on a phone-sized board. An agent's
helpers are dressed for the season: elves in green with red beanies, the scarecrow's helpers in
his purple and straw hats, and festival helpers in red caps.

An agent with a scheduled run has
tomorrow's door beside it (a little closed door, an uncarved pumpkin, an unlit lantern); an agent
whose next step is only a plan step keeps the plain sign.

In **Interface settings**, **Season** picks the board (the season switcher saves your choice
there), and **Weather** shows or hides the snow over the Advent square and the motes drifting over
the patch and the street, along with the 3D games' **Overlay density** and **Work bubbles**.
