Ryu records a call, transcribes it live, and generates structured AI notes: a summary, key points,
action items, and decisions. It can also detect when a meeting starts and offer to begin
recording.

## Record a meeting

The **Meetings** section in the sidebar is your meeting list.

- Click **+** (or open **Meetings** from the command palette) to open the record screen and start
  a meeting.
- A row shows a pulsing red dot while it is recording.
- Open a row to see that meeting's live transcript and, after finalizing, its notes.

On a meeting's detail page, double-click the title to rename it inline. Press **Enter** to save the
new title or **Esc** to cancel. The **Rename** action remains available beside the title as well.

Audio is captured from both your **microphone and system loopback** (so you get both sides of the
call), mixed down, and transcribed in chunks as the meeting runs.

## Automatic detection

Ryu detects a meeting in progress by watching the operating system for **a process that is using
the microphone**, not for the window in focus. When it detects one, it shows a prompt so you can
start recording with one click. Capture and detection run in the Shadow sensor on the device.

Turn auto-detection on or off, and edit the list of apps that count as meeting apps, in
**Settings → Meetings**.

<Callout type="warn">
  System-audio capture and mic-in-use detection are **Windows-first**. On other platforms recording
  falls back to mic-only, and automatic detection is not available.
</Callout>

## Notes

When you **finalize** a meeting, Ryu generates the notes with a model call and writes them, together
with the transcript, as a document in an auto-created **Meetings** Space. You view and edit notes
in the same editor as the rest of your [Spaces](/docs/surfaces/desktop/user-guide/spaces-memory),
and retrieval search covers them. The meeting detail's **Open notes** button opens the document.

Choose the notes model, reasoning effort, and prompt in **Settings → Meetings**.

## Speaker-label processing

Enable **Identify speakers** in **Settings → Meetings**, then choose a local model:
[pyannote 3.1](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1) or
[NVIDIA Nemotron 3](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Nemotron-3-Diarization). The selected
model runtime must be installed on the node. Pyannote requires accepting its Hugging Face
model terms. Nemotron uses its optional Transformers runtime.

Diarization runs on the finished recording, after a meeting ends. It labels the microphone
channel as **Me** and maps the model's other speaker turns onto transcript segments. It does
not change the live transcript. Requests run one at a time. If the chosen model or its
dependencies are unavailable, Ryu reports that limitation instead of inventing labels.
