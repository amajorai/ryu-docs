The **Island** (`apps/island`) is an Electron overlay that sits beside the desktop app on the same
machine, shaped like a small pill at a screen edge. It talks to the same Core as the desktop app, so
it shares your conversations, agents, and Gateway routing. Switching from the app to the Island
keeps your context.

<Callout type="warn">
  Island is not available in the current desktop release. The installer may download and update the
  Island bundle in the background, but the desktop app does not launch it and does not show its
  controls (service status, tray toggle, onboarding install, global shortcuts, or settings). Island
  is also hidden from the Store until it ships. This page describes the companion as it will work
  once it ships.
</Callout>

Island is an **optional Desktop app**. Once it ships, you install it from the Store; it is not
installed automatically. After installation, its node-scoped settings are under
**Gateway settings → Apps → Island** for the node that runs the companion. Use the app's Enabled
control in Gateway settings to turn the companion on or off.

## What it does

- A global shortcut expands the resting pill into a **command bar and mini chat** connected to Core.
  Stopping a chat reply in the Island also stops it in Core.
- **Shadow** context monitoring and a **local-model proactive engine** surface suggestion chips
  based on what is on your screen, once you allow it.
- **Push-to-talk voice input** sends speech to the Island chat, and
  [system-wide dictation](/docs/surfaces/island#system-wide-dictation) types speech into the focused app.
- **Generated screens** show an agent's interactive answer in a floating window.

Screen context and proactive suggestions start off; see the privacy settings below.

## Island settings

Install and enable Island, then open **Gateway settings → Apps → Island**. The settings apply to the
active node that runs the companion and save as you change them. If a preference save fails, Island
restores the previous value and shows an error so you can retry.

- **Privacy and capture:** Chat is on by default; Screen context and Proactive suggestions start off.
  Proactive suggestions require Screen context. **Hide from screen capture** keeps the Island out of
  screenshots, recordings, and screen sharing while remaining visible to you. **Hide on fullscreen
  apps** is available on Windows only. The Shadow capture mirrors let you toggle frame recording and
  pause all capture. Revoking screen context hides capture details right away.
- **Agents and voice:** Choose separate agents for chat and proactive suggestions, or choose
  **Default local model**. Push-to-talk has its own enable switch, shortcut, and toggle or hold-to-talk
  mode. While push-to-talk is on, the **Activation** row explains how the chosen mode starts and
  stops recording, and that pressing Tab while recording switches which agent handles the task. You
  can choose the voice recognition engine. Read-back speaks assistant replies aloud; choose
  its audio engine and voice when it is enabled. Read-back is on by default, uses Kokoro 82M, and
  pauses while a meeting is recording.
- **Appearance and position:** Choose Native glass, Mica, or Translucent (see below). The edge
  offset applies to each docked edge and both axes at corners; its default is 0 px on macOS and
  20 px on other desktop platforms. **Auto-jump to active monitor** follows the monitor under your
  cursor only while the Island is at rest: not while you drag it, while its panel is open, or while
  a wider pill (an app label, a recording, or a suggestion) is pulled in from a screen edge. It has
  no effect on a single-monitor setup. Drag the companion near a corner, edge, or the center to dock
  it there, or drop it anywhere else to leave it in place. It opens away from the screen edges
  nearest to where it rests: from the bottom-right it opens up and to the left, and from the top
  center it opens downward. When it collapses, it returns to the same spot. If you move the open
  panel, the companion collapses where you dropped it.
- **Notch mode:** Attach the companion to the camera notch on a supported Mac. It uses a black
  surface with rounded lower corners and keeps interactive content below the camera. Click the
  Ryu eyes or use the command shortcut to expand it. The setting is off by default and updates
  the running companion immediately. While attached, the companion stays on the notched display;
  dragging, edge offset, and auto-jump do not move it away from the notch. Background and floating
  placement preferences remain available when you turn it off. If no connected display has a detected camera
  notch, or on other operating systems, the companion uses floating mode.
- **Shortcuts:** Click a shortcut, press a key combination, or press Escape to cancel. Use **Reset**
  to restore the default command-bar or push-to-talk shortcut.

## Shortcut priority

The command-bar, push-to-talk, and dictation shortcuts take priority over plugin
shortcuts. If you set one of them to a key combination an app uses, the Island
feature takes that combination and the app's shortcut stops responding; a
combination the Island stops using goes straight back to the app that declared it.

The Island checks your enabled apps about every ten seconds, so the shortcut of an app you enable or
disable starts or stops working within that time without a restart. If your saved shortcut
settings cannot be read, the Island keeps the plugin shortcuts it already has rather than restoring
app defaults, and retries on its own.

## Appearance sync

The Island reads the Desktop's shared appearance preference through Core. Its hosted app and plugin
surfaces receive the same selected light/dark palette, custom theme colors, contrast, radius,
spacing, zoom, font stacks, cursor, overlay, motion, display timezone, and locale settings as the
Desktop Companion surface. Theme and Date & time changes update an already-open surface without
remounting it; switching the Island's window material recreates only its native window because the
compositor material is chosen at construction time.

The Island's **Background** setting controls its transparent window independently of the Desktop
window surfaces. **Native glass** is the default: macOS 26 and later use native Liquid Glass,
older macOS versions use Sidebar vibrancy, Windows 11 22H2 and later use system Acrylic,
and older Windows versions use native Acrylic when its companion module is available.
The logo, detail panel, and suggestion actions keep their clear gaps. **Mica** uses
Windows 11 Mica when available and falls back to Acrylic elsewhere.

**Translucent** draws the logo and detail panel as separate floating shapes with a soft shadow
and a CSS backdrop blur, inside a larger transparent window. It applies no native OS material on
macOS, Windows, or Linux, so the blur only reaches the Island's own contents and other apps behind
it stay sharp. At rest, the window around the shapes passes clicks through to the apps beneath it.
While the panel or command bar is open, the whole Island window takes the pointer until it closes.
Changing **Background** applies right away: the running Island rebuilds its window in the new
style, with no restart.

On Linux, Translucent needs an X11 compositor; without one, the transparent area around the shapes
appears black. In a Wayland session the Island runs through XWayland unless it is started with
`--ozone-platform=wayland` or `--ozone-platform-hint=wayland` (or `auto`). Native Wayland rendering
has not been verified.

Within the expanded Island, Chat, companion apps, and contributed views share one panel. Changing
pages cross-fades the content with a short blur while the panel adjusts to the selected view. Reduced
motion removes the translation and blur.

<Callout type="warn">
  The Island is built, but its live hotkey summon, focus handling, and context loop are unverified:
  they need a real display to exercise. Shadow context capture is cross-platform.
</Callout>

## Generated screens

When an answer has structure, such as options to compare, numbers, a plan, or details to fill in,
the agent can draw it as an interactive screen with the built-in
[`ui.render` tool](/docs/core/built-in-tools). The Island shows the agent's latest screen on your
desktop, in its own floating window beside the Island, rather than stacking cards in the chat:

- **Generated when you ask**: the screen appears on the Island's display as soon as the agent
  renders it, below an Island docked at the top or above one docked at the bottom.
- **Reshapes as you talk**: when you refine a request, the screen stays up, marked *Updating*,
  until the agent renders its replacement in the same place.
- **Disappears when you move on**: a reply without a screen closes it. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> while
  the screen is focused, or use its close button, to dismiss it yourself. Closing a screen hides
  only that screen; the next one the agent renders still appears, even if it looks the same.

The screen floats above other windows without taking focus, and clicks outside the card reach the
apps underneath. It stays with the Island: it hides whenever the Island is hidden, including when
**Hide on fullscreen apps** tucks the Island away, comes back when the Island does, and moves when
you drag the Island or it jumps to another display. It follows the Island's **Hide from screen
capture** setting, so it is hidden from screen capture whenever the Island is, and uses the Island's
language pack and text direction.

Each render keeps a compact row in the Island chat; choose
**Show** on an earlier row to bring that screen back. Screens are not saved: what you type or tick
on a screen is kept only while it is shown, and **Show** brings an earlier screen back as the agent
first drew it. Forms and buttons on a screen send your choice to the agent as your next message.
Island turns ask the agent to keep screens to a single narrow column. Agents that don't have the `ui.render` tool answer in text as usual. With reduced
motion enabled, screens fade instead of morphing.

## Voice input and voice mode

The Island's push-to-talk pill uses a multicolor glow along its lower edge that brightens and rises
with microphone input. The continuous voice-mode screen shares that visual; while the agent is
thinking, the glow gathers into a single beam that sweeps along the edge. The animation is an
activity cue alongside the transcript and the voice-mode controls.
With reduced motion enabled, the color drift stops and the thinking beam stays centered.

## System-wide dictation

The Island also hosts **system-wide dictation** - a separate global shortcut (default
`CommandOrControl+Shift+D`) that captures speech and types the transcript straight into whatever
native app has OS focus. This is distinct from the Island's voice input, which drops what you say
into the Island chat to run an agent; dictation just types.

Insertion goes through **Ghost**: the transcript is sent to Core's tool call endpoint as a
`ghost.ghost_type` action with no target app, so it lands in the currently focused window. Paste mode uses the clipboard plus a Ghost hotkey instead, and an
optional post-process step can clean the transcript up first. The shortcut never focuses the
Island, because stealing focus would break typing into your target app.

<Callout type="warn">
  Dictation is a companion feature that needs a display, a microphone, and the Ghost sidecar to
  exercise, so it is not yet live-verified. Configure its shortcuts, recognition engine, and
  insertion mode in **Gateway settings → Plugins → Dictation**. See the
  [Dictation app guide](/docs/apps/dictation). Island chat voice input remains under
  **Gateway settings → Apps → Island**.
</Callout>

## Microphone access

Island voice input and dictation request microphone audio from the Island window. Apps you
have approved to record audio, such as Teleprompter and Life Recorder, also record through the
Island window rather than through their own embedded frame. One app records at a time, and an
app's recording stops when you switch away from its tab or close the expanded panel. Camera,
display capture, and microphone requests from embedded subframes are not approved by its media
permission handler. OS microphone permission remains a separate gate: on macOS, review the
responsible application in Privacy & Security; on Windows, review microphone privacy settings;
on Linux, check the selected audio device and session access. A permission grant on a remote
Ryu node does not grant access to your local microphone.
