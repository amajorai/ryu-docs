Ryu's hardware line is three ESP32-S3 devices that act as **thin clients to a node**: a watch, a necklace, and a desk hub. These pages are for people who build, flash, pair, and run them.

<Figure name="hardware" />

A device is a microphone, a speaker, a small screen, and a radio. It runs **no inference of its own**.

The node (Ryu Core on your desktop, or on a remote machine over Tailscale) does the heavy work:

- speech-to-text (ASR);
- the LLM chat turn;
- text-to-speech (TTS);
- rendering dashboards to images.

A device reaches the node over direct WiFi or through a phone relay, and both paths carry the same encrypted protocol. [Hardware Architecture](/docs/hardware/architecture) explains the two modes. See [Core vs Gateway](/docs/start-here/architecture/core-vs-gateway) for the wider rule about where work runs.

<Callout type="warn">
  The Core WebSocket, registry, pairing, and turn path are covered by software tests, and the shared firmware components are tested on Linux.

  The firmware ships as ESP-IDF source with build and flash steps. Building it for the ESP32-S3 is not part of the automatic checks, and the physical watch, necklace, and desk boards have not completed hardware-in-the-loop testing. Treat the firmware and pairing pages as first-bring-up guidance, not a proven appliance image.
</Callout>
