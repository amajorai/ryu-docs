## 1. Scope and roles

This Privacy Notice explains how **A Major Pte. Ltd.** (“A Major,” “Ryu,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) handles information when you visit a Ryu website or documentation site, use a Ryu desktop, web, mobile, or extension surface, run Ryu Core or Gateway, use Ryu Cloud or a managed service, contact support, or connect a provider through Ryu.

The role we play depends on the data and the deployment:

- For account, website, billing, security, service-usage, and support administration, A Major may act as a controller or equivalent business under applicable law.
- For Customer Data processed through a managed Ryu service on an organization’s instructions, A Major generally acts as a processor or service provider for that organization. The organization is responsible for its own notice, instructions, permissions, and data-subject obligations.
- For a local or self-hosted installation, the customer or operator controls the infrastructure and decides whether any data is sent to A Major or another provider.

The [Data Processors](/docs/legal/data-processors) page lists the conditional providers that may receive data through Ryu. A separately signed Data Processing Agreement (“DPA”) or order form controls where it differs from this notice.

## 2. Ryu in one paragraph

Ryu is an open-core, local-first control layer for AI agents, models, tools, workflows, plugins, apps, and channels. Core coordinates execution, while Gateway provides policy, routing, access, network, budget, DLP, audit, and other controls. Ryu can run locally or be connected to managed nodes and cloud services. “Local-first” means that a local deployment can keep task data on customer-controlled infrastructure; it does not mean that remote model calls, synchronization, managed hosting, connected services, diagnostics, or support access never transmit data.

## 4. Where data is processed

### Local and self-hosted paths

When Core, Gateway, and a model run on your device or infrastructure, prompts, files, conversations, agent content, and local traces can remain there. The operator controls local storage, backups, network exposure, model providers, plugins, and connected services. A Major does not automatically receive local Customer Data merely because you installed Ryu.

Local deployments can still send data when you select a remote model, enable synchronization, connect an app or channel, configure diagnostics, use a Ryu-managed service, or grant support access. Review the provider and feature settings before enabling a path.

### Ryu-managed and cloud paths

When you use Ryu Cloud, a hosted node, organization controls, synchronization, billing, web access, or a managed channel, the relevant account and service records are processed by A Major and its deployment providers. Customer Data may also be processed by the managed node, model provider, tool, plugin, channel, or connected app you select. The service plan, DPA, and [Data Processors](/docs/legal/data-processors) page describe the applicable path.

## 6. Analytics, crashes, diagnostics, and support

Ryu separates content-free UI telemetry from data-plane content and uses independent controls for optional streams.

### Product analytics

Product analytics are **on by default and opt-out** in the current desktop and web product configuration. They use a fixed, content-free set of events such as onboarding state, agent installation, engine or model changes, chat-turn counts, feature toggles, and stable error codes. They use a random installation identifier rather than an account identity.

Product analytics are not intended to include prompts, completions, conversation text, files, file paths, tool arguments, or agent content. You can turn them off in the product’s privacy settings. Web deployments may also provide a browser-local preference or may be configured without an analytics key.

### Crash reports

Crash reporting is a separate **on-by-default, opt-out** choice in the current desktop configuration. Reports are scrubbed error or crash information used to improve stability. They are not intended to include prompts, files, or agent content. You can disable crash reporting independently of product analytics.

### Local traces and audit records

Core and Gateway can keep local run traces and audit records for the operator. These records may include model, provider, token, cost, latency, status, and error metadata. Tool arguments are represented by a hash or redacted form rather than raw arguments in the documented local trace path. Local records do not leave the device by themselves.

### Diagnostics export

Diagnostics export is **off by default and opt-in**. If you enable it and configure a destination, Ryu can export redacted OpenTelemetry or audit spans to the destination chosen by the operator. The destination is not a fixed Ryu vendor; it can be an Axiom, Grafana, self-hosted collector, PostHog, or another compatible endpoint. Review the destination and its privacy terms before enabling export.

### Anonymous aggregate community statistics

Some Ryu builds can send anonymous aggregate compression or savings statistics. The current preference is enabled by default and opt-out. The purpose is to understand aggregate performance and cost-saving patterns, not to identify a person or organization. The intended payload does not include prompts, files, hostnames, API keys, organization identifiers, or account identity.

### Support access

Support access is **off by default** and requires an explicit grant. Cloud support access is limited to the selected control-plane scopes, such as billing, subscription, organization, synchronization status, channels, or marketplace records. It is revocable, expires after a short period of no more than one hour in the current implementation, and is recorded with the actor, reason, scope, and time.

Local support access is also user-granted and time-limited. The documented diagnostic bundle is read-only and limited to version, active engine, sidecar status, preference names rather than values, and redacted trace information. It is not intended to include prompts, agent content, or credentials. See [Privacy and Telemetry](/docs/surfaces/desktop/transparency) for the detailed stream-by-stream explanation.

## 7. Model, app, plugin, and channel providers

Ryu can send Customer Data to an external provider when you or your organization selects or authorizes that provider. Examples include hosted model providers, local-model gateways, MCP servers, plugins, repositories, databases, Slack, Telegram, Discord, WhatsApp, and other channels or connected apps.

The provider may process the data under its own terms and privacy notice. A provider may be:

- an A Major-selected subprocessor for a managed Ryu feature;
- a provider you select with your own account or API key; or
- a customer-directed service that is not operated by A Major.

The provider’s retention, training, security, international-transfer, and deletion practices may differ from Ryu’s. Review the provider’s terms before sending sensitive data. Ryu does not use your prompts, files, or activity to train Ryu models, but this commitment does not rewrite a provider’s independent policy.

## 8. Sharing and disclosure

We may disclose information to:

- infrastructure, database, email, payment, analytics, crash, security, and support providers that process it for Ryu;
- model, app, plugin, channel, or other services that you or your organization direct to receive it;
- affiliates, successors, or advisors in connection with a reorganization, financing, acquisition, or sale, subject to appropriate confidentiality measures;
- authorities, courts, or other parties when required by law or reasonably necessary to protect rights, safety, security, or the Services; and
- other parties with your instruction or consent.

We do not sell Customer Data or use prompts, files, or activity for targeted advertising. We may use aggregated or de-identified information that does not reasonably identify a person or organization to operate, secure, measure, and improve Ryu.

## 9. Security

Ryu uses technical and organizational measures appropriate to the deployment, including access controls, credential isolation, network and policy boundaries, redaction, audit records, and encryption where supported by the relevant store or service. Security controls reduce risk; they do not guarantee that every threat, provider, plugin, model, channel, or customer-controlled system will be secure.

The level of protection depends on the deployment. Self-hosted operators are responsible for their infrastructure, backups, operating-system security, network exposure, credentials, plugins, and providers. Do not place secrets in prompts or grant integrations broader access than necessary.

## 10. Retention and deletion

Retention depends on the data and service path:

- **Local and self-hosted data:** the operator controls storage, backups, retention, and deletion, subject to the operator’s own obligations.
- **Managed Customer Data:** retained as needed to provide the selected service and then deleted, returned, or anonymized according to the plan, DPA, order form, and documented deletion process.
- **Account, billing, entitlement, security, and support records:** retained as needed to operate accounts, prevent abuse, resolve disputes, meet tax or legal duties, and prove or enforce agreements.
- **Analytics, crash, and diagnostics data:** retained by the configured Ryu or vendor service according to its configuration and policy. Local privacy settings stop future collection; they do not necessarily erase an already delivered report.
- **Backups and legal holds:** copies may persist for a limited operational or legal period before being overwritten or released.

Concrete retention periods or links to service-specific retention schedules should be maintained where the law or DPA requires them. We will not promise a deletion date that the active deployment cannot meet.

## 11. International processing

A Major, its providers, and customer-selected services may process information in countries other than where you live or where your organization is established. The final legal notice and DPA should identify the transfer mechanisms and supplementary safeguards required for the relevant jurisdictions, such as an adequacy decision, standard contractual clauses, or another lawful mechanism.

The [Data Processors](/docs/legal/data-processors) page is intentionally deployment-aware. It does not claim a single region or fixed vendor for self-hosted, customer-selected, or operator-configured paths.

## 12. Your choices and privacy rights

Depending on where you live and the role A Major plays, you may have rights to request access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection, portability, or withdrawal of consent. You may also have rights concerning automated decision-making, sensitive data, or targeted advertising under local law.

You can manage available analytics, crash, diagnostics, community-statistics, and support-access choices in the product or organization settings. Organization users should first contact their organization administrator for Customer Data requests. A Major may direct a request to the organization when the organization controls the data or instructions.

To submit a privacy request, contact **hello@ryuhq.com** and include the account or organization involved, the request, and enough information to verify your identity and authority. Do not send passwords, API keys, prompts, files, or other sensitive content unless support specifically requests a safe, appropriate channel. We may retain information where required by law or needed to protect rights, security, or the integrity of the Services.
