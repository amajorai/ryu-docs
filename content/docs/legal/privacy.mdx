## 1. Who we are and what this covers

This Privacy Notice explains how **A Major Pte. Ltd.** (UEN 202616096G), a company incorporated in Singapore (“A Major,” “Ryu,” “we,” “us,” or “our”), handles personal data when you visit Ryu websites and documentation, use the Ryu desktop, web, mobile, or browser-extension apps, run Ryu Core or Gateway, use Ryu Cloud or another managed service, or contact us.

Our role depends on the data and the deployment:

- **Accounts, websites, billing, security, and support.** A Major decides how this data is used and is responsible for it (a “controller”).
- **Content processed by a managed service for an organization.** A Major processes it on the organization’s instructions (a “processor”). The organization is responsible for its own notice to its users; a signed DPA governs where it differs from this notice.
- **Local and self-hosted installations.** You or your administrator operate the node and decide what, if anything, is sent to A Major or to other services. Installing Ryu does not by itself send your content to us.

## 2. Local-first, not “nothing leaves the device”

When Core, Gateway, and a model run on your own device or server, your prompts, conversations, files, and agent content are stored there. Content leaves the node only through paths you or your administrator enable, such as a remote model provider, cross-device sync, backups, a connected app or channel, diagnostics export, a managed service, or a support grant. Each of these is described below.

## 4. Where your content goes

- **On your node.** Core stores conversations, Spaces, agents, traces, and settings in its local data directory. Message content, memory, secret settings, and stored tokens are encrypted with a key held in your operating system’s keychain (or a key you supply). Some local databases, such as Spaces documents and run traces, are not yet encrypted, so protect the device with disk encryption.
- **Model providers.** When you use a remote or Ryu-managed model, the content needed for the request (prompts, conversation context, files, and tool results) is sent to that provider. See Section 7.
- **Cross-device sync (off by default).** If you turn it on, chats, editable Spaces documents, and settings are uploaded to your Ryu account so your other nodes can read them. Synced content is encrypted at rest on our servers with a key we manage; it is **not end-to-end encrypted**, so our systems can decrypt it to serve it to your nodes. Binary attachments are not synced.
- **Backups (off by default).** If you configure S3-compatible backups, encrypted backups are sent to the storage provider you choose, using a recovery key you hold.
- **Managed cloud nodes and hosted agents.** If you use a Ryu Cloud node or managed agents, your content is processed and stored on infrastructure we operate for you. Managed-agent session data is kept for 30 days by default.
- **Ryu Mail.** Messages sent and received through Ryu-managed inboxes are stored on our servers and delivered through Amazon SES.
- **Connected apps, plugins, channels, and tools.** When you connect Slack, Telegram, Discord, WhatsApp, a repository, an MCP server, a Composio integration, or another service, the content an agent sends to it is processed under that service’s own terms.
- **Shared links.** A conversation you share is stored as a frozen, redacted copy viewable by anyone with the link until you revoke it.

Ryu’s desktop features that read your screen, audio, clipboard, or selected text (such as Quick Capture and screen-memory tools) send what they capture to your own Core node. Whether that content then leaves the node depends on the model you have configured for it. The **browser extension** reads the visible text, title, and address of pages you use it on and can take screenshots; it sends them to your own Core node, not to Ryu’s servers. The **mobile app** asks for camera, microphone, photos, contacts, calendar, and Bluetooth permissions only for the features that use them, and sends push-notification tokens to your own Core node.

## 5. How we use information

We use personal data to:

- create and secure accounts, run sign-in, and prevent fraud and abuse (for example, sending a security alert when an account signs in after 90 or more days of inactivity);
- provide the features you use, including sync, managed models, cloud nodes, Mail, the Marketplace, organizations, and family sharing;
- process payments, credits, refunds, payouts, and program rewards;
- enforce plan limits, rate limits, budgets, and these Terms;
- send service messages about your account, security, and billing;
- send promotional email where permitted (Section 9);
- understand and improve reliability and product use through the content-free telemetry described in Section 6; and
- comply with law and protect rights and safety.

**We do not use your prompts, conversations, files, or activity to train Ryu models.** If you use the fine-tuning app, it trains a model from data you select, on your own node or on a cloud GPU node you choose, for your use. Thumbs-up and thumbs-down feedback is used by your local Core to adapt your own setup.

Where the law requires a legal basis, we rely on performance of our contract with you, our legitimate interests in operating, securing, and improving Ryu, your consent (for optional processing you can withdraw), and legal obligations.

## 6. Analytics, crash reports, and diagnostics

Ryu keeps telemetry separate from your content. The defaults are:

| Stream | Default | What is sent | How to turn it off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Desktop product analytics (PostHog, US) | On | A fixed set of content-free events, such as onboarding completed, agent installed, model install started, chat started (count only), feature toggled, and error code, tied to a random installation ID that is never linked to your account | Settings → Privacy |
| Website analytics | **Off until you accept** | Page views and page-leave events, and clicks on public pages (not on sign-in, account, or settings pages), sent anonymously to PostHog; page views are also counted by a Plausible-compatible analytics service A Major operates at data.amajor.ai | Choose **Reject analytics** in the cookie banner, or change your choice at any time with **Cookie settings** in the site footer. A browser Global Privacy Control signal is treated as a refusal |
| Crash reports (Sentry) | On | Error stacks with home-folder paths, user, request, and server-name details removed, tagged with the random installation ID | Settings → Privacy on desktop and mobile; the website’s error reporting has no per-user setting |
| Community statistics | On | Every five minutes: aggregate request, token, and cost-savings counts, cache hit rate, operating system, CPU architecture, app version, active engine, and counts for verified public Marketplace items, tied to a random installation ID | Settings → Privacy |
| Local traces and Gateway audit | On, stays on your node | Model, provider, token, cost, latency, and error metadata; tool arguments stored as a hash; API keys redacted. Traces are kept for 90 days or 1 million rows by default | Stored locally only |
| Diagnostics export (OpenTelemetry) | **Off** | Redacted traces sent to an endpoint you choose. On Ryu Cloud-enrolled nodes, the same opt-in also sends a small heartbeat and model-usage summary to Ryu | Settings → Privacy |

Session recording is turned off in all Ryu apps and websites. None of these streams is intended to include prompts, completions, conversation text, file contents, or tool arguments. Turning a stream off stops future collection; it does not delete reports already sent. The [Privacy and Telemetry](/docs/surfaces/desktop/transparency) page gives the full stream-by-stream detail.

Ryu’s own servers log each request’s method, route, status, and timing without query strings, bodies, user content, or user IDs. Hosted Gateway usage (model, provider, token counts, latency, outcome) is recorded to meter and bill managed usage.

## 7. Model providers

When you use a Ryu-managed model route, your request is sent to the model provider that serves it. Depending on the model or feature, this may include OpenRouter (and the provider it routes to), OpenAI, Anthropic, Google (Gemini API and Vertex AI), Amazon Bedrock, Cloudflare Workers AI, Replicate, fal.ai, Modal, Deepgram, ElevenLabs, or Cohere. Agent tools Ryu operates may also use Composio (app connections), Daytona (code sandboxes), and Exa (web search). The [Subprocessors](/docs/legal/data-processors) page lists each one. When you use your own API key or account, the request goes to the provider you configured, under your own agreement with it.

Requests that Ryu Gateway sends to OpenRouter always require providers that do not collect prompts for training and that meet OpenRouter’s Zero Data Retention policy; on Ryu-managed gateways, OpenRouter batch jobs, which cannot carry that per-request policy, are refused. Other providers process requests under their own terms, which may include retention for abuse monitoring and, for some providers, use of data to improve their models. Ryu’s commitment not to train on your data does not change a provider’s policy. Review the provider before sending sensitive content, and prefer local models for content that must not leave your node.

On Ryu's hosted Gateway, each request to OpenAI, Anthropic, OpenRouter, Fireworks, and Vercel AI Gateway also carries an opaque identifier (the `safety_identifier`, `user`, or `metadata.user_id` field, depending on the provider) that Ryu derives from the user, end user, or gateway key the request is attributed to. It is pseudonymous: it contains no email address or name, but the same subject always gets the same identifier, so a provider can link that subject's requests and report abuse. If your application passes an end-user id in the `x-ryu-end-user` header, only a keyed hash of it is kept and used for this; the raw value is dropped. A self-hosted Gateway adds no identifier.

## 8. Who we share data with

We share personal data only:

- **with service providers** that operate Ryu for us, including Amazon Web Services (Lightsail), which hosts our servers and the database we run ourselves, Hetzner for managed cloud nodes and for storing uploaded files, Cloudflare for DNS, the network proxy in front of our websites and APIs, inbound email routing, and the Turnstile bot check, our own useSend instance and Amazon SES and S3 for email, Polar (merchant of record for plans, licenses, and credits) and Stripe for payments, PostHog and Sentry for analytics and crash reports, and Axiom for server logs and usage metering;
- **with the services you direct us to send content to**, such as model providers, connected apps, channels, and plugins;
- **with other users as you choose**, such as your public profile and Wrapped statistics, shared conversation links, Marketplace listings and reviews, and content you save in organization or family workspaces;
- **with your organization**, whose administrators can see your membership, role, and usage and audit records for the organization;
- **with Marketplace publishers**, who see aggregate totals of how their templates are used, never who used them;
- **in a business transfer**, such as a merger or acquisition, subject to confidentiality;
- **when required by law** or to protect rights, safety, and security; and
- **with your consent**.

The [Subprocessors](/docs/legal/data-processors) page gives more detail about these providers.

**We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it for targeted advertising.**

### Family workspaces

Where family sharing is available, the holder of an eligible plan (the family owner) can invite others into a family workspace. If you join a family, everyone in it can see your name, email address, and role. The family owner and organizers can also see:

- usage metadata for your activity in the family workspace, such as models and providers used, request and token counts, cost, and when the usage occurred;
- the policies, spending limits, and feature controls that apply to you; and
- configuration changes recorded in the workspace’s audit view.

These views do not show the content of your prompts, completions, or conversations. Messages you send to a bot set up for the family, and conversations on an assistant node shared with the family, are processed by and may be stored on that node, and whoever runs it may be able to see them. Material you save to a shared area can be seen by other family members with access to it. Your personal workspace stays separate. When a family owner turns on family sharing, organizers they appoint can also see that workspace’s existing usage and audit records.

To enforce the personal-use limits in the [Terms of Service](/docs/legal/terms), authorized A Major staff may review family membership records, such as how many families an account belongs to and the domains of members’ email addresses, for signs of business use. This uses membership records, not conversation content, and does not trigger automatic action.

### Support access

Support access is **off by default**. A Major staff can see your account data only after you grant access from your account settings, only within the scopes you choose (billing, subscription, organization, sync status, channels, or Marketplace), and for no longer than one hour. It never exposes prompts, messages, documents, provider credentials, or API keys. Every session is recorded in an audit log you can read, and you can revoke access at any time. Local support access to a Core node is separately off by default, time-limited, and limited to a redacted diagnostic bundle.

## 11. The documentation site

The docs assistant sends your questions to OpenCode Zen (opencode.ai) to generate answers, and uses your IP address in memory to apply a rate limit. Page feedback you submit on the documentation site is posted as a public issue in the Ryu GitHub repository, so do not include personal information in it.

## 12. Security

We use access controls, credential isolation, network and policy boundaries, redaction, audit logs, and encryption in transit and at rest. On our servers, sensitive fields such as synced content, integration tokens, and single-sign-on secrets are encrypted at the application layer. Gateway API keys are stored as hashes. No system is completely secure, and protection on self-hosted nodes depends on how the operator configures and secures them. Report vulnerabilities to **security@amajor.ai**.

## 13. Retention

| Data | Retention |
| --- | --- |
| Account, profile, and organization data | While your account is active. When you delete your account, personal data is removed immediately; the account record is kept only as an anonymous placeholder so retained billing and audit records stay consistent |
| Synced conversations, documents, and settings | Until you or your administrator delete them, or your account is deleted |
| Billing and transaction records | As required for tax and accounting law |
| Managed-agent sessions | 30 days by default |
| Ryu Mail | Inboxes are deactivated 30 days after a plan lapses; stored mail is kept for about 90 days |
| Managed cloud nodes | Deleted after a 3-day grace period once the subscription ends, or 72 hours after you ask to delete them |
| Email delivery events | 90 days |
| Local traces on your node | 90 days or 1 million rows by default (configurable by the operator) |
| Support-access audit log | Kept after access ends so you can review it |
| Abuse-detection violation events from Ryu's hosted Gateway and Ryu-managed nodes | 400 days. They hold categories, detectors, actions, and hashed identifiers, not prompts |
| Redacted excerpts of screened text (at most 2 KB) attached to those events | 30 days, held longer only while an open case, ban, or appeal needs them, then 90 days after the last hold is released. The Gateway's own local copy is deleted once delivered, and after 72 hours in any case |
| Appeal text (up to 4,000 characters) and its contact address | 90 days after the appeal is decided; the address on file for a ban is emptied 90 days after the ban ends |
| Analytics and crash reports | According to the retention settings of PostHog, Sentry, and Axiom |
| Local and self-hosted data | Controlled by the operator |

Database backups may still contain deleted data until those backups are removed under our backup retention schedule. We do not restore deleted personal data from backups except to recover from an incident.

## 14. Your rights and choices

You can:

- update your profile and make it **private**, which removes your public profile and Wrapped statistics;
- turn off product analytics, crash reports, community statistics, and sync in **Settings → Privacy**;
- accept or reject website analytics in the cookie banner, and change that choice at any time with **Cookie settings** in the site footer;
- delete your account from **Settings → Account** (an account without an authenticator app or passkey adds one first);
- revoke shared conversation links, connected accounts, API keys, and support access;
- unsubscribe from promotional email; and
- copy or export your local data from your node’s data directory.

Depending on where you live, you may also have rights to access, correct, delete, or receive a copy of your personal data, to object to or restrict certain processing, and to withdraw consent.

You can **delete your account** yourself from your account settings. Deletion removes your sign-in methods, profile, synced content, shared links, reviews, personal mail, and workspaces where you are the only member, and anonymizes the records we must keep (Section 13). See [Deleting your account](/docs/security/authentication-account#deleting-your-account) for what happens and what must be resolved first.

Self-serve export of your account data is not yet available. For an export, or for any other request, email **privacy@ryuhq.com** from the address on your account, and we will verify your identity and respond within the time the law requires. If your account belongs to an organization, we may refer requests about its content to the organization’s administrator.

You may also complain to your local data-protection authority. In Singapore, this is the Personal Data Protection Commission.
